NURSING INTERVENTIONS

STEP 1 · MONITOR & PROTECT

Two jobs, all day long: measure the fluid, and stop anything that would hurt the kidney further.

⚖️ Daily weights — 1 kg = 1 liter of fluid retained

Answer first: the scale is a more reliable fluid measure than I&O, because nobody forgets to record a weight the way they forget a urinal.

🧠 “A kilo is a carton of milk 🥛.” One kilogram, one liter, one carton. If she gained two cartons overnight, that fluid is sitting in her lungs — assess breath sounds first.

✅ Procedures — the daily checklist

1
⚖️ Daily weights — same time, same scale, same clothing. 1 kg = 1 L.
2
💧 Strict I&O — every mL in and out, including ice chips, IV flushes and med volumes.
3
🚰 Fluid restriction — commonly the previous day's urine output plus about 500–600 mL. Amounts vary by facility & provider — follow the order.
4
🫁 Assess lungs, edema, JVD & BP every shift — this is how overload announces itself.
5
🫀 Cardiac monitor whenever K⁺ is over 5.0.
6
🧪 Track the labs — K⁺, Na⁺, phosphorus, calcium, BUN, creatinine, H&H, pH.
7
🦠 Infection prevention — uremia suppresses immunity; infection is a leading cause of death in ESRD.
8
🤲 Skin care for pruritus — cool water, gentle soap, moisturize, keep nails short, no scratching.
🧠 “Weigh · Watch · Write · Warn.” Weigh daily · Watch lungs & edema · Write every mL · Warn the provider when the numbers move.

🩸 Hemodialysis — the machine version of the kidney

Source definition: the machine version of the kidney, used to wash the blood. It is only used for a number of years until a kidney transplant is available.

  • 🖐️ Assess the access every shift: feel the thrill, hear the bruit. No thrill/bruit = clotted = report immediately.
  • 🚫 Never take BP, draw blood, or start an IV in the fistula arm
  • ⚖️ Weigh before and after every treatment — that difference is the fluid removed.
  • 💊 Some meds (especially antihypertensives) are held before dialysis to prevent hypotension — follow the provider's order; practice varies.
  • 📉 Watch for hypotension, cramps, headache during and after treatment.

Go deeper: NG-248 hemodialysis · NG-261 peritoneal dialysis · NG-267 transplant & biopsy.

🧠 “Thrill & Bruit — the fistula's pulse and purr.” If you can't feel the buzz or hear the whoosh, the lifeline is clotted.

THE RENAL DIET

STEP 2 · FOUR LOWS

Low sodium · low potassium · low phosphorus · protein adjusted to the stage. Every food question on the exam comes from these four lists.

🚦 The renal food traffic light

NCLEX TIP Renal diets are individualized to the patient's labs and stage by a renal dietitian — the lists above are the ones these exams test.

🧠 “An apple a day keeps the potassium away 🍏.” When a food question involves a high potassium level, look for the apple — it's the answer more often than any other food on the NCLEX.

🧂 NO SODIUM — and NEVER a salt substitute

  • 🥫 No canned / packaged foods
  • 🌭 No processed meats — deli meat, bacon, hot dogs, sausage
  • 🧂 No salt shaker; season with herbs, lemon, garlic, vinegar
  • 🚫 NO salt substitutes — they are potassium chloride

Why: sodium drags water with it → more edema, more hypertension, more work for the heart.

HESI Statement that shows a need for further education: “I should reduce my sodium intake by using a salt substitute.” ❌

🧠 “Salt substitute = Secret Potassium.” The label says salt-free; the bottle is full of K⁺. In renal failure that swap can stop a heart.

HESI Q: Best food choice for a potassium of 6.5? A: Apple slices or apple juice — low K⁺.

🧠 “Bananas, Oranges, Potatoes, Spinach — BOPS your potassium up.” Anything that BOPS — skip it. Reach for the apple.

🦴 LOW PHOSPHORUS — NO dairy

  • 🥛 No milk · 🍮 no pudding · 🥣 no yogurt NCLEX TIP
  • 🧀 Cheese, ice cream — same problem
  • 🥤 Dark colas, nuts, seeds, whole grains, dried beans

Why: phosphorus rises → calcium falls → itching, muscle cramps and brittle bones (renal osteodystrophy).

🧠 “Phosphorus and calcium ride a seesaw 🎢.” Phos up = calcium down. Cut the dairy, take the binder, save the bones.

🥩 LOW PROTEIN — and the exception

KAPLAN Rationale for a low-protein diet? → to preserve renal function. Protein breakdown makes urea — less protein in, less waste for a dying kidney to filter.

KAPLAN Teaching is effective when the client says: “I will eat red bell peppers and avoid red meats.” ✅

⚠️ The exception: once a client is on dialysis, protein needs go UP — dialysis pulls protein out with the waste. Pre-dialysis = restrict; on dialysis = replace. Exact grams are set by the renal dietitian and vary.

🧠 “Before the machine, keep protein lean. On the machine, feed the protein.” Same disease, opposite answer — read whether they're dialyzing.

DRUG THERAPY

STEP 3 · AVOID & GIVE

First the five drugs that finish off a kidney — then the ones that replace what the kidney stopped making.

🚫 AVOID: the top drugs that can cause kidney failure

🧠 “No More Very Good Dye” — NSAIDs · Milk of Magnesia · Vancomycin · Gentamicin · Dye. Five words, five ways to finish a kidney.

☢️ Before contrast dye — what the nurse does

  • 🧪 Check the creatinine / GFR first
  • 💧 Hydrate before and after per order — IV fluids protect the kidney
  • 🩸 Ask about metformin and any nephrotoxic meds
  • 🐚 Ask about iodine / shellfish / prior dye reactions
  • 📈 Recheck creatinine after — contrast injury shows up in 24–72 hours
🧠 “Cement in the washer.” Picture pouring wet concrete into a washing machine — that's contrast dye in a fragile kidney. Flush it out with fluids or it sets.

⚠️ Other drugs to watch

  • 💊 Magnesium- or aluminum-containing antacids — the kidney can't clear them
  • 🧂 Potassium-sparing diuretics & K⁺ supplements — no pee, no K
  • ❤️ ACE inhibitors / ARBs — protective in chronic proteinuric CKD, but they raise K⁺ and are usually held in acute injury. Monitor K⁺ and creatinine.
  • 💓 Digoxin and many others are cleared by the kidney → doses must be reduced; watch for toxicity
🧠 “Renal dose everything.” A normal dose in a failing kidney becomes an overdose by tomorrow.

💊 GIVE: replacing what the kidney stopped doing

ProblemDrugNursing pearl
Anemia 🩸Epoetin alfa / darbepoetin + ironReplaces missing erythropoietin. Monitor BP — it can rise. Hgb is titrated to a target, not maxed out.
High phosphorus 🦴Phosphate binders — calcium acetate, sevelamerGive WITH meals — they bind the phosphorus in the food. Given between meals they do nothing.
Low calcium / vit D ☀️Calcitriol (activated vitamin D)The kidney normally activates vitamin D; now it can't.
High potassium 🫀Sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Kayexalate); insulin + D50; calcium gluconateFull priority order is on NG-265. Expect diarrhea with Kayexalate — that's how K⁺ leaves.
Fluid overload 💧Loop diuretic — furosemideOnly works if nephrons still function. Watch for hypokalemia & ototoxicity (push slowly).

Hypertension 🩸Antihypertensives per orderBP control is the #1 way to slow CKD progression.
🧠 “Binders with Bites 🍽️.” Phosphate binders go with the food — no food, no point. It's the most-tested timing question on this page.

🍽️ Phosphate binder timing — see it once, never miss it

🧠 Binder = a net 🥅. You only catch fish while the fish are swimming by. No meal = no fish = no point.

📚 Teaching she can hand the patient

  • ⚖️ Weigh yourself every morning, same scale, same clothes — call for a gain of 2–3 lb in a day or 5 lb in a week (threshold varies — use your provider's number)
  • 🧂 Read every label for sodium, potassium, phosphorus
  • 🚫 No OTC ibuprofen/naproxen — ask before any new medicine or supplement
  • 🩹 Protect the fistula arm — no BP cuffs, no blood draws, no tight sleeves or jewelry
  • 💉 Keep vaccinations current; report fever early
  • 🗓️ Never skip dialysis — missed sessions are how potassium reaches 8.0+
🧠 “Scale, Label, No-Ibuprofen, Save the Arm.” Four sentences the patient must be able to repeat back before discharge.

🎯 Cover & check — 7 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A client gains 2 kg overnight. How much fluid is that, and what do you assess first?
2 kg = 2 liters retained (1 kg = 1 L). Assess breath sounds and respiratory status first — that fluid goes to the lungs. Then edema, JVD, BP.
Q2: Name the top drugs to avoid because they can cause kidney failure.
NSAIDs · Milk of Magnesia (magnesium antacid) · antibiotics vancomycin and gentamicin · CT contrast dye ("thick cement in the washer — contrast kills the kidney!").
Q3: HESI — best food choice for a potassium of 6.5?
Apple slices or apple juice — low potassium. Avoid bananas, oranges, strawberries, tomatoes, avocado, carrots, spinach and potatoes.
Q4: HESI — which statement shows a need for further education?
"I should reduce my sodium intake by using a salt substitute." Salt substitutes are potassium chloride — dangerous in renal failure.
Q5: Kaplan — what's the rationale for a low-protein diet?
To preserve renal function — less protein means less urea for the failing kidney to filter. Note the flip: once on dialysis, protein needs increase because dialysis removes protein.
Q6: Kaplan — which statement shows nutrition teaching was effective?
"I will eat red bell peppers and avoid red meats."
Q7: When are phosphate binders given, and why?
WITH meals — they bind dietary phosphorus in the GI tract so it leaves in the stool. Given between meals there is nothing to bind. Also teach: no dairy — no milk, yogurt or pudding.
