Peritoneal Dialysis 🫙

Fill · Dwell · Drain — your own belly lining is the filter

NG-261 Renal ADHD-friendly visual edition

The peritoneal cavity is filled with a hypertonic solution to PULL solutes out. No machine filter, no blood outside the body — the peritoneal membrane is the dialyzer. Three test magnets live here: sterile technique 🧤, cloudy drainage ☁️, and sudden trouble breathing 🫁.

HOW IT WORKS

STEP 1 · THE BELLY IS THE FILTER

One catheter, one membrane, one sugar gradient — that's the entire machine.

🩹 The set-up — catheter, cavity, two bags

The peritoneal cavity is filled up with hypertonic solution to PULL solutes out. A permanent peritoneal catheter tunnels through the abdominal wall into the peritoneal space. Fluid runs in by gravity from a bag hung ABOVE and drains out by gravity into a bag BELOW.

Fresh dialysate in Peritoneal membrane Effluent out Sterile exit site
🧠 “Bag up, belly full, bag down.” Picture a hot-water bottle in the belly ♨️ — it goes in warm, sits and soaks up the trash, then drains into a bag on the floor.

🧲 Why a SUGARY solution pulls fluid

Answer first: the dialysate is hypertonic (high dextrose). Hypertonic = more particles than the blood, so water is pulled OUT of the capillaries into the belly by osmosis. Waste follows by diffusion.

Stronger dextrose = more fluid removed. Concentrations commonly run from about 1.5% up to 4.25% — the provider picks it based on how much fluid must come off.

🧠 “Sugar is a sponge.” The sweeter the bag, the more water it sucks out.

⚖️ Peritoneal vs Hemodialysis

 Peritoneal 🫙Hemo 🩸

WhereAt home, often nightlyCenter, ~3×/week

#1 dangerPeritonitis ☁️Access clotting / bleeding
DietMore liberal; protein loss is higherTighter K⁺ & fluid limits
🧠 PD is the slow cooker 🍲, HD is the pressure cooker ♨️. Slow & steady vs fast & risky.

🔁 The exchange: FILL ➜ DWELL ➜ DRAIN

Outflow should be equal to or MORE than inflow — that extra volume is the fluid you pulled off the client. Less out than in = a problem to solve (see DURING).

🧠 “Fill · Chill · Spill.” Three beats, always in that order. Chill = the dwell, where the actual dialysis happens.
🌡️

BEFORE THE EXCHANGE

STEP 2 · WEIGH · WARM · STERILE

"Proper preparation for peritoneal dialysis" — the exam wants two actions and one technique.

✅ Proper preparation — in order

1
⚖️ Take the weight. Empty bladder first, same scale, same clothes. This is the baseline for how much fluid you removed. 1 kg = 1 L.
2
🌡️ WARM the solution. The FIRST action before infusing is to warm the dialysate.
3
🧤 Sterile technique — PRIORITY. Mask, hand hygiene, sterile field, scrub the connection. The catheter is a straight road to the peritoneum.
4
📏 Baseline assessment. Vital signs, abdominal girth, lung sounds, exit-site inspection, last bowel movement.
5
🚽 Have the client empty the bladder before the fill for comfort and to reduce puncture risk with a new catheter.
🧠 “Weigh · Warm · Wash.” Three W's before a single drop goes in.

🌡️ Why warming is the FIRST action KAPLAN

🧠 “Cold cramps, warm welcomes.” If a question asks the first action before an exchange, the answer is warm the dialysate — not "check the drain," not "position the client."

🧤 Sterile technique is the PRIORITY — this is how peritonitis starts CAUTION

Every touch of that catheter is a possible direct inoculation of the peritoneum. Contamination almost always happens at the connection or the exit site.

  • 😷 Mask on the nurse and the client for connect/disconnect.
  • 🧼 Hand hygiene → sterile gloves → scrub the connection port.
  • 🩹 Exit-site care daily with the ordered solution; keep it dry; report redness, warmth or drainage.
  • 🚿 Teach: showers, not tub baths or swimming pools (varies by policy).
  • 🔍 Inspect every bag: expiration, clarity, leaks, correct dextrose concentration.
🧠 One dirty connection = one infected belly. There is no "clean enough" here — the peritoneum has no defense once bacteria are inside.

🧠 Sugar UP, protein DOWN. The two labs peritoneal dialysis changes that hemodialysis doesn't.

🍗 Diet: protein goes UP

Answer first: peritoneal dialysis loses protein into the drained fluid every single exchange, so the client needs MORE dietary protein, not less.

  • 🥚 High-quality protein — eggs, fish, poultry, lean meat
  • 🍬 Watch carbohydrate/calories — the dextrose counts as sugar
  • 🥦 Fiber & fluid — constipation blocks outflow (see DURING)
🧠 Protein leaks out the drain. Refill it with the plate.
🔁

DURING THE EXCHANGE

STEP 3 · TROUBLE & FIRST ACTIONS

Three scenarios come up over and over: can't breathe, won't drain, and the belly turns hot.

🚨 Sudden respiratory distress ➜ FIRST ACTION: raise the HOB KAPLAN

Why it happens: too much fluid, infused too fast — the full abdomen pushes the diaphragm UP and the lungs can't expand.

🔎 KEY SIGNS

  • 🫁 Crackles in the lung bases
  • 💨 Rapid respirations
  • 😮‍💨 Dyspnea — sudden onset during or after a fill

🚑 PRIORITY INTERVENTION

  • 1️⃣ Raise the HOB — first action.
  • 2️⃣ Slow or stop the inflow; drain the fluid per order.
  • 3️⃣ Reassess lung sounds, SpO₂, respiratory rate; notify the HCP.
🧠 “Breathing beats circulation.” A client on peritoneal dialysis who suddenly starts breathing faster — the first action is always raise the head of the bed. Picture the belly as a balloon shoving the lungs upstairs 🎈 — sit them up and the balloon slides back down.

🚱 Insufficient outflow — assess in this order KAPLAN SCENARIO

Answer first: Patient ➜ Device ➜ Intervention. Assess the client before you touch the tubing, and touch the tubing before you reposition.

1
🧍 Assess the PATIENT — abdominal distention and CONSTIPATION. A full bowel is the most common reason fluid won't drain.
▼
2
🔌 Assess the DEVICE — catheter kinks, closed clamps, obstruction, fibrin clots, the bag not low enough, tubing trapped under the client.
▼
3
🔄 INTERVENE — reposition the client to a side-lying position; turn side to side; get them up and walking if allowed. Then notify the HCP if outflow is still poor.

Also do: keep the drain bag below the abdomen · check that inflow volume and outflow volume are recorded every exchange · less out than in = fluid retained, report it.

🧠 “P-D-I: Patient, Device, Intervene.” Same letters as Peritoneal Dialysis Issue. And remember the sneaky #1 cause: constipation — a loaded colon squeezes the catheter shut. 💩

🩸 Other things you may see in the drain

🩸 Blood-tingedCan be expected in the first few exchanges after catheter placement, and in menstruating clients. New or bright bleeding = report.

🧠 Pale yellow = pass. Cloudy = call.

⚠️ Other complications to watch

  • 🍬 Hyperglycemia — dextrose is absorbed; check glucose, especially in diabetics.
  • 📉 Hypotension / hypovolemia — too much fluid pulled off with strong dextrose.
  • 💧 Leakage at the exit site — report; increases infection risk.
  • 🫃 Hernia & back pain — from the constant intra-abdominal volume.
  • 🍽️ Anorexia / fullness — the belly is literally full; offer small frequent meals during a drain.
🧠 A belly full of sugar water explains all five: high sugar, low pressure, leaks, bulges, no appetite.

AFTER + PERITONITIS

STEP 4 · THE ONE YOU MUST NOT MISS

Drain, measure, weigh — and stare at the color of that bag.

🚨 PERITONITIS — key signs to REPORT to the HCP

What to do: notify the HCP · send the effluent for cell count, Gram stain & culture before antibiotics if ordered · expect antibiotics, often added to the dialysate (intraperitoneal) or IV per order · continue to monitor temp, HR, pain and the appearance of every bag.

🧠 “No one likes cloudy dayyyys — and nobody likes cloudy drainnnnage.” ☁️ Sing it. Cloudy fluid is white blood cells in the belly — that is infection, not sediment.

✅ After every exchange — the documentation habit

1
📏 Measure inflow and outflow and record both. Outflow should be ≥ inflow.
2
➖ Calculate the difference. Outflow − inflow = fluid removed. A negative number means the client kept fluid — report it.
3
👀 Describe the effluent — color and clarity, every single bag.
4
⚖️ Weigh the client after the drain, plus vital signs, lung sounds & edema check.
5
🩹 Exit-site check and dressing per policy.
🧠 “In, out, difference, describe, weigh.” If you can't say how much came back, you don't know what the treatment did.

🏠 Home teaching — what she tells the client

  • 😷 Mask + handwashing every connection — no shortcuts, no exceptions.
  • ☁️ Call for cloudy fluid, fever, or belly pain — do not wait for the next clinic visit.
  • ⚖️ Weigh daily, log every exchange, log in/out volumes.
  • 💩 Prevent constipation — it stops the drain. Fiber, fluids, ordered stool softener.
  • 🩹 Keep the exit site clean, dry, and secured so the catheter isn't tugged.
  • 🧊 Store bags clean & dry; check expiration and clarity before use.
🧠 Mask · Measure · Move the bowels. Three habits that prevent the three biggest PD problems.

🎯 Cover & check — 6 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What is the first action in proper preparation for peritoneal dialysis?
Warm the dialysate. (Take the weight, warm the solution — cold fluid causes cramping, chills and vasoconstriction, which means a poor exchange.) Never use a microwave.
Q2: A client on peritoneal dialysis suddenly begins to breathe more rapidly. First action?
Raise the head of the bed. The full abdomen is pushing the diaphragm up — breathing over circulation. Then slow/stop the inflow and drain per order, and notify the HCP. Key signs: crackles in the lung bases, rapid respirations, dyspnea.
Q3: The effluent is cloudy and the client has a fever of 101.4°F and a heart rate of 118. What is this and what do you do?
Peritonitis. Report to the HCP; send effluent for cell count, Gram stain and culture per order; expect antibiotics (often added to the dialysate or given IV). Sterile technique is the priority prevention.
Q4: The outflow is much less than the inflow. In what order do you assess?
1. Assess the PATIENT — abdominal distention and constipation. 2. Assess the DEVICE — catheter kinks, clamps, obstruction, bag position. 3. Intervene — reposition to side-lying. Then notify the HCP if it doesn't resolve.
Q5: Why does peritoneal dialysis raise blood glucose and lower albumin?
The dialysate is hypertonic dextrose — some sugar is absorbed across the peritoneum (glucose up). Protein crosses the other way into the effluent and is drained away (albumin down), so dietary protein is INCREASED.
Q6: What color should the drained fluid be?
Clear and pale yellow. Cloudy = peritonitis. Fibrin strands may clog the catheter. Blood-tinged fluid can be expected in the first few exchanges after catheter placement, but new or bright bleeding is reported.
