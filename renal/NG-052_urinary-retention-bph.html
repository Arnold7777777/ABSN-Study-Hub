Retention & BPH 🌰🚱

Urinary retention with an enlarged prostate — and the three drugs that fix it

THE SQUEEZE

STEP 1 · WHY IT WON'T COME OUT

Two completely different reasons a man can't void — and the whole drug list depends on which one you're looking at.

🌰 The prostate is a doughnut around the urethra

The prostate sits directly under the bladder and the urethra runs straight through the middle of it. Normally it's about the size of a walnut. In BPH the cells multiply — the doughnut thickens and the hole in the middle closes.

Benign = not cancer · Prostatic = the prostate · Hyperplasia = too many cells.

🧠 “Step on the garden hose.” The water is still on 💧 (kidneys never stop), but you stepped on the hose 🦶 (prostate). Everything downstream is a dribble and everything upstream is backed up.

Never give bethanechol when the retention is caused by a mechanical obstruction. You would be forcing a muscle to squeeze against a closed door — risk of bladder injury.

🧠 “Blocked door vs dead motor.” Blocked door 🚪 → open or shrink the door (the -osins and finasteride). Dead motor 🔋 → recharge the motor (bethanechol). Get this one distinction right and every question on this page falls over.

🔎 What BPH retention looks like at the bedside

Obstructive symptoms — the stream itself:

  • ⏳ Hesitancy — stands there waiting to start
  • 🚿 Weak, intermittent stream · straining to void
  • 💧 Post-void dribbling
  • 🫗 Sensation of incomplete emptying

Irritative symptoms — the bladder complaining:

  • 🌙 Nocturia — the symptom that drives them to the clinic
  • 🔁 Frequency · urgency · small voided volumes
🧠 “Wait, weak, dribble, again.” Four words in the order the client experiences them — that's an obstructive stream.

THE DRUGS

STEP 2 · WHAT EACH ONE DOES

Three drugs, three completely different jobs — squeeze the bladder, relax the gate, or shrink the gland.

💪 BETHANECHOL — squeeze the bladder

CLASS Direct-acting cholinergic agonist (muscarinic)   USE Non-obstructive urinary retention

MOA: stimulates cholinergic receptors = more secretions and a stronger detrusor contraction, so the bladder finally squeezes the urine out.

Indication in the exam's words: non-obstructive urinary retention — specifically neurogenic bladder, where clients with neuro issues get loss of strength in the urinary muscles. Also called urinary atony / functional urinary retention.

Test wording to recognize:

  • 📘 Kaplan: bethanechol treats functional urinary retention (neurogenic bladder)
  • 📗 HESI: bethanechol is used for urinary atony; TCAs plus bethanechol are used to prevent bladder dysfunction
  • 🎯 Expected outcome: a NON-DISTENDED bladder — that is how you know it worked
🧠 Bethan-echol = BLADDER CONTROL. Hear “-echol” as “e-KOHL” → choline → cholinergic. It's the only drug here that touches the bladder muscle instead of the prostate.

🚪 TERAZOSIN & TAMSULOSIN — relax the gate

CLASS Alpha-1 adrenergic blockers   USE Urinary retention with an enlarged prostate — BPH

MOA: relaxes smooth muscle in the bladder neck, prostate & periphery to help release urine. The gland is still big — the muscle around it just stops gripping. Relief in days, not months.

DrugWhere it actsExtra note
TerazosinBladder neck/prostate + blood vesselsAlso an antihypertensive → biggest orthostatic effect
TamsulosinMore uroselective — mostly prostateLess BP effect, still dizziness; retrograde ejaculation is common
Same familydoxazosin · alfuzosin · silodosin — all end in -osin
🧠 TeraGOOOsin 🙌 helps you GO. · TeraSLOWsin 🐢 — change positions slowly. · TeraLOWsin ⬇️ — it LOWERS the BP, so watch other BP-lowering drugs. Three nicknames, three exam points.

🌰 FINASTERIDE — shrink the gland

CLASS 5-alpha reductase inhibitor   USE BPH — shrinks the prostate   SIBLING dutasteride

MOA: blocks the enzyme 5-alpha reductase, so testosterone can't convert into DHT (dihydrotestosterone) — the hormone that drives prostate growth. No DHT → the gland actually gets smaller.

📗 HESI: “What med will shrink the prostate?” → Finasteride.

🧠 “If you want a FUN RIDE on a horse 🐴, take FIN-aste-RIDE.” FIN like a shark fin shrinking down into the water — the only drug here that makes the gland smaller.

WATCH FOR

STEP 3 · SIDE EFFECTS & SAFETY

Each drug fails in its own signature way — falls, faints, or fetal harm.

🚨 Orthostatic hypotension — the #1 alpha-blocker danger

First-dose phenomenon: the first dose (and every dose increase) can drop the BP hard enough to cause syncope. Give the first dose at BEDTIME.

🧠 “Dangle before you dash.” Sit and dangle the legs, count to 60, then stand. In an elderly client on terazosin, a hip fracture is a bigger threat than the BPH ever was.

⚠️ The rest of the alpha-blocker list

  • 💊 Do not combine with sildenafil or other PDE-5 drugs — additive vasodilation = dangerous hypotension
  • 🩺 Caution with other BP-lowering drugs — TeraLOWsin
  • 💦 Retrograde ejaculation (semen goes into the bladder → cloudy urine). Harmless, but tell them so they don't stop the drug in a panic
  • 👁️ Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome — the client MUST tell the eye surgeon before cataract surgery
  • 🤧 Nasal congestion · headache · fatigue
🧠 “-osin drops what stands up.” BP drops, the client drops, and the ejaculate goes backward. Not dangerous — but startling if nobody warned them.

⚠️ Finasteride — the pregnancy rule

  • 🤰 A pregnant woman must never handle crushed or broken finasteride tablets — absorbed through skin, causes genital abnormalities in a male fetus
  • 🧪 Lowers PSA by about half — the provider doubles the value to interpret it (matters for cancer screening)
  • 💔 ↓ libido · erectile dysfunction · ejaculatory changes · gynecomastia
  • ⏳ Do not stop early — it needs 3–6 months
🧠 “FIN cuts the PSA in half ✂️.” If a client on finasteride has a PSA of 2, the provider is really looking at a 4.

🚨 Bethanechol — cholinergic overload = SLUDGE

💧Salivation
😢Lacrimation (tears)
🚽Urination
💩Defecation
🤢GI cramping
🤮Emesis
  • 💓 Bradycardia & hypotension — check HR and BP before giving
  • 🫁 Bronchospasm — hold in asthma
  • 🍽️ Give on an empty stomach (about 1 hr before or 2 hr after meals) to reduce nausea/vomiting
  • 🚽 Keep the bedpan/urinal within reach — it works fast
  • 💉 Antidote: atropine at the bedside
🧠 “Everything that can leak, leaks.” Cholinergic = wet (SLUDGE, slow heart). Anticholinergic = dry (can't see, pee, spit, poop). Bethanechol and atropine are exact opposites — which is why atropine is the antidote.

TEACH IT

STEP 4 · WHAT YOU SAY

The exam tests teaching more than pharmacology — here is the exact question, option by option.

🎯 SATA — teaching an elderly client newly prescribed terazosin

“Which instruction(s) should be included in the teaching plan? Select all that apply.”

✅
1. “It is not necessary to avoid foods that contain grapefruit.”
Correct. Grapefruit juice is actually OK with this drug. Grapefruit is a problem for many other drugs — not this one.
✅
2. “Make sure to change positions slowly while on this medication.”
Correct. TeraSLOWsin — orthostatic hypotension and falls are the biggest risk in an elderly client.
❌
3. “I will not take this medication with antacids.”
Not a required teaching point for terazosin — this restriction belongs to other drug classes.
❌
4. “Do not take sildenafil while on this medication.”
Read carefully. This item was not keyed on this question — but combining an alpha blocker with a PDE-5 inhibitor is a real hypotension caution in practice. Know both: the fact is true, the SATA key on this item is #1 and #2.
❌
5. “If you forget a dose, take two pills as soon as you remember.”
Never double up a dose — especially a BP-lowering drug. This option is always wrong.
🧠 “Grapefruit is fine, go slow, never double.” Three phrases, one SATA. And if a distractor ever says take two pills, cross it out before you finish reading it.

✅ Teach every client with BPH

  • 💧 Fluids 2–3 L/day — but taper in the evening to cut nocturia
  • ☕ Limit caffeine & alcohol — both irritate the bladder and worsen urgency
  • ⏰ Timed voiding every 2–3 hours; don't hold it
  • 🔁 Double voiding — void, wait, void again
  • 🤧 Avoid decongestants and antihistamines — they can trigger ACUTE retention
  • 🚶 Stay active; treat constipation
  • 📅 Keep the annual prostate exam / PSA plan with the HCP
🧠 “Cold pill at bedtime = ED at midnight.” An over-the-counter cold remedy is the classic trigger that turns chronic BPH into a catheter emergency.

📈 When drugs stop working

  • 💊 First-line: alpha blocker for fast symptom relief
  • 🌰 Add a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor for a large gland (combination therapy is common)
  • 🩺 Recurrent retention, UTIs, stones, hydronephrosis or rising creatinine → surgery
  • 🔪 BPH refractory to medication → anticipate TURP (full surgical care on NG-240)
🧠 Relax → shrink → resect. The three escalating answers to the same squeezed urethra, in the order the exam expects.

🎯 Cover & check — 6 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Bethanechol is used for which type of urinary retention?
NON-obstructive — specifically neurogenic bladder / urinary atony, where the urinary muscles lost strength. Never for a mechanical obstruction.
Q2: What is the expected outcome of bethanechol therapy?
A non-distended bladder — the client empties.
Q3: How does terazosin help a client with BPH?
It relaxes smooth muscle in the bladder neck, prostate and periphery so urine can be released. It does not shrink the gland.
Q4: Which medication shrinks the prostate?
Finasteride (also dutasteride) — a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor that blocks conversion of testosterone to DHT. Takes 3–6 months.
Q5: Two teaching points for an elderly client starting terazosin?
Grapefruit does not need to be avoided, and change positions slowly (orthostatic hypotension). Take the first dose at bedtime; never double a missed dose.
Q6: Why can't a pregnant nurse or partner handle a broken finasteride tablet?
It is absorbed through the skin and can cause genital abnormalities in a male fetus. Handle intact tablets only.
