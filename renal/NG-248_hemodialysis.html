Hemodialysis 🩸

The machine version of the kidney · AV fistula care · DDS

NG-248 Renal ADHD-friendly visual edition

Hemodialysis is a machine version of the kidney. Blood leaves the body, runs past a filter, and comes back clean — it removes waste, excess fluid & electrolytes the failed kidney can no longer clear. Almost every test question is about the access arm 🖐️ or pulling fluid off too fast 🧠.

WHAT IT IS

STEP 1 · THE CIRCUIT

Blood out, through a filter, back in — and the whole thing runs through one precious arm.

🩸 The hemodialysis circuit — follow one drop of blood

Blood out ➜ pump ➜ dialyzer ➜ air trap ➜ blood back. The dialyzer is the artificial kidney. It does not "make urine" — it lets waste diffuse out of the blood into a fluid called dialysate running the other direction.

Dirty blood out Clean blood back Dialysate Waste removed
🧠 “DIAL the soap.” Any time you see the word DIAL — dialysis, dialyzer, dialysate — picture a bar of Dial soap scrubbing the blood clean. Soap in, dirt out.

🔬 Inside the dialyzer — 2 forces, 2 jobs

  • Diffusion — solutes move from high to low: urea, creatinine, K⁺ leave the blood.
  • Ultrafiltration — pressure pulls water off. That's how fluid comes off.
🧠 Diffusion cleans, ultrafiltration dries. One takes the trash, the other takes the water.

🚪 Three ways in — pick the right access

AccessWhat it isReady to use
AV fistula ⭐
best / longest life		Surgeon sews the client's own artery to veinNeeds weeks to mature — commonly 6 wk–4 mo (varies)
AV graftSynthetic tube bridges artery → veinSooner than a fistula, but clots & infects more
Central venous catheterLarge tunneled/temporary line in a central veinImmediate — but highest infection risk; short-term
🧠 “Fistula First.” Own vessels > plastic graft > catheter. The catheter is the fastest and the most dangerous.

🖐️ The AV fistula — feel the THRILL, hear the BRUIT

No thrill + no bruit = the fistula has clotted. That is not "wait and see" — report it immediately.

🧠 “Thrill you Touch, Bruit you Blisten.” (Say it wrong on purpose — Bruit = listen with the Bell.) Thrill = Touch. Teach her to check it several times a day at home.

💪 Help the new fistula mature

  • 🎾 Squeeze or grip a “rubber ball” or sponge — exercise enlarges the vein.
  • 💧 Keep the arm warm, keep the client hydrated and not hypotensive — low BP clots a new fistula.
  • 👀 Look at the site daily for redness, warmth, drainage.
🧠 Ball in the hand, blood in the vein. Squeezing is the fistula's workout.
🩺

BEFORE DIALYSIS

STEP 2 · SET THE BASELINE

Weight, vitals, fistula, and the meds you must NOT give — this is where the exam lives.

✅ The pre-dialysis checklist

1
⚖️ Weigh the client — current AND previous weight. Weight is the #1 measure of fluid status. 1 kg = 1 L of fluid.
2
🌡️ Full vital signs — especially BP (baseline for the hypotension that follows) and temp (infection screen).
3
🖐️ Assess the fistula — FEEL a thrill, HEAR a bruit, inspect for infection & bleeding.
4
💧 Assess fluid status — lungs, edema, JVD, breath sounds, I&O.
5
💊 HOLD the meds that will be washed out or drop the BP.
🧠 “W-V-F-F-M” → Weigh · Vitals · Fistula · Fluid · Meds. Say it as “We Very Fully Fix Meds.”

💊 HOLD antihypertensives — dialysis already drops the BP

Dialysis pulls volume off. Give a BP pill on top of that and you get hypotension during the run — the most common complication of hemodialysis.

🧠 “A-B-C-D-D” — ACE/ARB · Beta blocker · Calcium channel blocker · Diuretic · Dilator. Five letters, five held bottles.

🧪 “Washed out” meds — the dialyzer eats them NCLEX TIP

Small, water-soluble molecules cross the membrane and get thrown away with the dialysate. Giving them before the run wastes the dose — give them AFTER dialysis.

Washed outExamples
Antibiotics 💉Penicillins, cephalosporins
Digoxin ❤️Narrow therapeutic range — and a low K⁺ after dialysis makes dig toxicity worse
Water-soluble vitamins 🍊B vitamins, C, folic acid — replaced daily in dialysis clients
🧠 “A-D-V goes down the drain” — Antibiotics · Digoxin · Vitamins (water-soluble). Picture them swirling down the dialysate drain 🌀. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) stay put — fat floats.

🧪 Labs that drive the treatment STANDARD ADULT RANGES

LabNormalIn renal failure
BUN10–20 mg/dL📈 HIGH
Creatinine0.6–1.2 mg/dL📈 HIGH (best kidney marker)
Potassium3.5–5.0 mEq/L📈 HIGH ⚠️ peaked T waves
Phosphorus3.0–4.5 mg/dL📈 HIGH
Calcium8.6–10.2 mg/dL📉 LOW (mirror of phosphorus)
Hgb / Hct12–18 g/dL📉 LOW (no erythropoietin)
🧠 Phosphorus and calcium are on a see-saw ⚖️ — one up, the other down. Every time.

💊 The take-home renal meds

  • Phosphate binders (calcium acetate, sevelamer) — give WITH meals, not between. They bind the phosphorus in the food.
  • Epoetin alfa — replaces the erythropoietin the kidney no longer makes; treats the anemia. Monitor BP and Hgb.
  • Active vitamin D (calcitriol) — the failed kidney can't activate vitamin D, so calcium can't be absorbed.
🧠 Binders eat with you. If the client swallows it away from food, there's no phosphorus there to bind — wasted dose.
⚙️

DURING DIALYSIS

STEP 3 · WATCH THE BRAIN & THE BP

Everything that goes wrong during the run goes wrong because something was pulled off too fast.

🚨 DEADLY: Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome (DDS)

Definition: solutes are removed too quickly from the blood. Urea stays high inside the brain cells, so water rushes into the brain — the cells swell, and ICP rises. Untreated it goes to seizures, coma & death.

🔎 KEY SIGNS

  • 😵 Restless & disoriented (first change = LOC)
  • 🤢 Vomiting
  • 🤕 Headache
  • ➕ later: twitching, seizures, ↓LOC

🚑 PRIORITY ACTION NCLEX TIP

  • 1️⃣ STOP or SLOW the dialysis — fix the cause first.
  • 2️⃣ Report to the provider.
  • 3️⃣ Seizure precautions · protect the airway · reorient.
🧠 “Fast dialysis = swollen brain.” Any new confusion, headache or vomiting during a run is DDS until proven otherwise. The fix is always slow it down — never speed it up to "finish faster." Most likely in the FIRST few treatments and in clients with very high BUN.

📉 Hypotension — the MOST common complication

Why: volume is coming off faster than the vessels can refill.

  • 😵‍💫 Dizzy, nauseated, pale, diaphoretic, cramping
  • ✅ Slow/stop ultrafiltration, lay flat & raise the legs (Trendelenburg per policy)
  • ✅ Give normal saline bolus per order · recheck BP
🧠 Water out too fast = pressure down. Same fix as DDS: slow the machine.

🫧 Air embolism — rare but instant

  • Signs: sudden dyspnea, chest pain, cyanosis, ↓LOC
  • 1️⃣ Clamp the line · stop the pump
  • 2️⃣ Place left side-lying, head down (traps air in the right atrium)
  • 3️⃣ 100% oxygen · call for help
🧠 “Clamp · Left · Low · O₂.” Air floats — so put the air where it can't reach the lungs.

🩸 Bleeding — heparin is running

The circuit is heparinized to stop clotting, so during and just after a run the client bleeds easily.

  • Watch: gums, GI bleed, bruising, the needle sites
  • Hold pressure longer after any stick
  • Antidote to heparin: protamine sulfate
🧠 Heparin in the tubing = thin blood in the client.

💪 Muscle cramps & nausea

From rapid fluid and sodium shifts, usually late in the run.

  • ✅ Slow the ultrafiltration rate
  • ✅ Normal saline per order
  • 📉 Often a sign the client gained too much between treatments
🧠 Big gain = rough run. Cramping is the body's receipt for skipping the fluid restriction.
✅

AFTER + TEACHING

STEP 4 · PROTECT THE ARM

Weigh, watch the BP, guard the access — and the four NO-NOs she has to know by heart.

⚖️ Post-dialysis assessment — weight tells the story

🧠 The scale is the kidney's report card. 1 kg = 1 L = 2.2 lb. A client who gains 2 kg between runs drank 2 L more than they should have.

🚫 The fistula arm NO-NOs — never on that arm

🩺NO blood pressureuse the other arm
💉NO IVs or lab drawsno venipuncture
⌚NO tight clothingor watches / jewelry
😴NO sleeping on itcompression clots it
🧴NO creams or lotionsskin breakdown + infection
👜NO lifting >5 lbno purse on that arm

Every one of these does the same thing: it squeezes the fistula shut or lets bacteria in. A clotted fistula means surgery.

🧠 “No BP, no needles, no bling, no bag, no bed, no balm.” Six NOs — say them in one breath while pointing at your own arm.

🚨 Report to the HCP — the 5 P's distal to the shunt NCLEX TIP

  • Pallor — pale skin below the fistula
  • Paresthesia — numbness or tingling
  • Pulses diminished
  • Poor cap refill (over 3 seconds)
  • Pain distal to the shunt

These mean the fistula is stealing blood from the hand. ✅ Mild pitting edema of that arm is NORMAL.

🧠 5 P's = “Please Protect Pale Poor Painful hands.” Edema = fine. Cold, white, numb = call.

🌡️ Monitor the access site itself

  • 🔥 Infection — warm, red, tender, or drainage at the shunt site
  • 🩸 Bleeding — hold firm pressure, do NOT occlude the flow
  • 🔇 No thrill / no bruit = clotted = report NOW
🧠 Hot & red = infected. Silent = clotted. Both get a phone call.

🍽️ Diet & fluid teaching for the dialysis client

RestrictWhy · examples
Potassium 🍌The kidney can't excrete it → fatal dysrhythmias. Limit bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, avocado, salt substitutes.
Sodium 🧂Sodium holds water → weight gain, edema, high BP between runs.
Phosphorus 🥛Dairy, dark colas, nuts, processed foods. Take binders with meals.
Fluid 💧Usually restricted; goal is a small gain between treatments. Exact limit is individualized — follow the order.
Protein 🍗Different rule: pre-dialysis CKD restricts protein, but on hemodialysis protein is INCREASED — amino acids are lost through the dialyzer.
🧠 “Na · K · P · H₂O down — protein UP once dialysis starts.” The trap answer is telling a dialysis client to eat less protein.

📅 Living with it — daily teaching

  • 🤚 Check the thrill several times a day — teach the client and the family.
  • ⚖️ Weigh daily at the same time, same clothes; report rapid gains.
  • 💉 Never skip a treatment — missed runs = hyperkalemia + fluid overload.
  • 🆘 Carry ID that says "dialysis access — no BP/no sticks this arm."
🧠 Touch it, weigh it, show up. Three habits that keep a dialysis client alive.

🎯 Cover & check — 6 rapid-fire questions
Q1: You assess the fistula and feel nothing and hear nothing. Now what?
No thrill + no bruit = the fistula has clotted. Report to the provider immediately — do not use the access.
Q2: Two hours into dialysis the client is restless, disoriented, vomiting and has a headache. Priority?
Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome — solutes removed too fast, brain cells swell, ICP rises. STOP or SLOW the treatment first, then report to the provider. Seizure precautions.
Q3: Which meds are held before dialysis, and which are just given after?
HOLD antihypertensives (ACE/ARBs, beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, diuretics, nitroglycerin) — dialysis already drops the BP. Give AFTER the run: antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins), digoxin, and water-soluble vitamins (B, C, folic acid) — they get washed out through the dialyzer.
Q4: The client's hand below the fistula is pale, numb and has a cap refill of 4 seconds. Expected or report?
REPORT — that's the 5 P's (pallor, paresthesia, pulses diminished, poor cap refill, pain) = the access is stealing blood from the hand. Mild pitting edema of that arm, on the other hand, is expected.
Q5: The client weighed 72 kg before and 69 kg after. How much fluid came off?
3 L. 1 kg = 1 L = 2.2 lb.
Q6: Name four things that must never happen to the fistula arm.
No blood pressure cuff, no IVs/venipuncture/lab draws, no restrictive clothing or jewelry (watches), no sleeping on it, no creams or lotions, no lifting over 5 lb (no purse).
