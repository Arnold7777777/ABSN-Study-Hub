🤖 Growth & development — Erikson and the milestones

The eight stages, the ages that get asked about, and the growth numbers worth memorising.

⭐ The four numbers that carry most of the questions

ByWhat has happened
6 monthsBirth weight has doubled
12 monthsBirth weight has tripled; length is up about 50%
2 yearsAbout half of adult height; the anterior fontanelle is closed
2–3 monthsThe posterior fontanelle closes

The anterior fontanelle closes between 12 and 18 months. A fontanelle that closes far too early, or one that is still wide open at two years, both need investigating.

🧠 Erikson — the eight stages

Each stage is a conflict. The nursing question is nearly always “what does this age need from me while they are in hospital?”

AgeConflictWhat they need in hospital
0–1 infantTrust vs mistrustConsistent caregivers; the parent present; needs met quickly
1–3 toddlerAutonomy vs shame & doubtChoices — two options, both acceptable. Routines and a comfort object
3–6 preschoolInitiative vs guiltLet them help. Correct magical thinking — they think illness is a punishment
6–12 school ageIndustry vs inferiorityLet them finish things and keep up with school; explain how equipment works
12–18 adolescentIdentity vs role confusionPrivacy, peers and control. Talk to them, not only the parent
Young adultIntimacy vs isolationRelationships, partner involvement
Middle adultGenerativity vs stagnationBeing useful; contributing; work and family roles
Older adultIntegrity vs despairLife review; being listened to
🧠 Toddlers need choices. Preschoolers need to know it is not their fault. Teenagers need privacy. If a question gives you an age, it usually wants one of those three.

🚨 Red flags — refer, do not reassure

  • No social smile by 3 months
  • Not sitting unsupported by 9 months
  • Not walking by 18 months
  • No words by 18 months, or no two-word phrases by 2 years
  • Loss of a skill they already had, at any age
  • Persistent primitive reflexes past the age they should have gone

🏠 What each age needs from the nurse

👶 Infant

Keep the parent there. Same nurse where possible, feed on their schedule, swaddle, dim the lights. Give injections vastus lateralis.

🧘 Toddler

Two choices, both fine. Keep routines and the comfort object. Expect regression in hospital — tell the parents it is normal. Prepare them just before a procedure, not hours ahead.

🎨 Preschooler

“You did not cause this.” Magical thinking makes them think illness is punishment. Let them handle safe equipment. Prepare them about an hour ahead.

📚 School age

Explain how it works. They want the reason and the equipment. Let them keep up with school and finish things. Prepare a few days ahead.

🎧 Adolescent

Privacy, peers, control. Interview them without the parent for part of the visit. Body image is a real clinical concern. Prepare a week or more ahead.
