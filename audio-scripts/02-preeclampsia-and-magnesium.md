# Preeclampsia and magnesium sulfate — the signs that mean a seizure is coming

**For NotebookLM:** make a short audio overview, about two to three minutes,
two speakers, conversational. Do not add material beyond this source. Do not
read out headings.

---

A client at thirty-four weeks has a blood pressure of one hundred and
fifty-eight over one hundred and four, and three plus protein in her urine.
That is preeclampsia, and the diagnosis is the easy part.

The exam question is almost never "what is this". It is "which finding worries
you most". So let us go straight there.

The blood pressure and the protein establish the **diagnosis**. What sets the
**urgency** is the central nervous system.

A severe headache that will not lift. Visual changes — blurring, flashing
lights, spots. And epigastric or right upper quadrant pain. Those three are
telling you the brain and the liver are becoming involved, and that eclampsia,
meaning a seizure, is close. If a question offers you a headache with visual
changes alongside a high blood pressure and some protein, the neurological
signs are the answer.

That right upper quadrant pain deserves a second look, because it is easy to
dismiss as heartburn. It can mean **HELLP syndrome**. H, E, L, L, P.
Haemolysis. Elevated Liver enzymes. Low Platelets. And here is the catch —
HELLP can happen with a blood pressure that is barely raised. So a pregnant
client with upper abdominal pain gets those labs drawn regardless of how well
she looks.

Now, magnesium sulfate.

Understand what it is for, because this is misremembered constantly. Magnesium
is **not** given to lower the blood pressure. It is given to **prevent a
seizure**. If the blood pressure needs treating, that is a separate drug —
labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine. Magnesium protects the brain. Antihypertensives
protect the vessels. Different jobs.

Magnesium is a central nervous system depressant, which is exactly why it
works and exactly why it is dangerous. Toxicity arrives in a predictable order,
and knowing the order is what lets you catch it early.

**Reflexes go first.** The deep tendon reflexes become sluggish, then absent.
That is your earliest warning, and it is why you check them every hour.

**Then respirations.** Breathing slows. Below twelve is a problem.

**Then the heart.** Cardiac arrest.

There is a fourth thing to watch that is not a symptom at all: **urine output**.
Magnesium is cleared entirely by the kidneys. If output drops below thirty
millilitres an hour, magnesium is accumulating even though the infusion rate
has not changed. So low urine output predicts toxicity before the reflexes do.

And the antidote is **calcium gluconate**. Not fetched from the pharmacy when
something goes wrong — kept at the bedside before the infusion is ever started.
If a question asks what should be immediately available for a client on
magnesium, that is the answer.

---

**The thirty-second version.**

Blood pressure and protein make the diagnosis. Headache, visual changes and
epigastric pain make it urgent — those mean a seizure is coming.

Right upper quadrant pain, think HELLP, and draw the labs even if the pressure
looks fine.

Magnesium prevents seizures. It does not treat blood pressure.

Toxicity goes reflexes, then respirations, then heart. Watch the urine output,
because it warns you first.

Calcium gluconate at the bedside. Always.
