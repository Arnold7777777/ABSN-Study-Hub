# Croup versus epiglottitis — the one where you do not touch the throat

**For NotebookLM:** make a short audio overview, about two to three minutes,
two speakers, conversational. Do not add material beyond this source. Do not
read out headings.

---

A small child arrives struggling to breathe with a noise on inspiration. Two
conditions are on the table, and confusing them can cost the child an airway.
This is the paediatric discrimination worth knowing cold.

**Croup** first, because it is far more common and much less frightening.

Croup is viral, and it comes on gradually — often a day or two of an ordinary
cold first, then worse at night. The sound is unmistakable once you have heard
it described: a **barking cough**, like a seal. There is inspiratory stridor,
and usually a low-grade fever. The child looks unwell but is generally
manageable, and the treatment is corticosteroids, sometimes nebulised
epinephrine, and calm.

**Epiglottitis** is the emergency, and everything about it is different.

It is bacterial and it is **sudden** — a child who was fine a few hours ago.
The fever is high. And here is the picture to memorise, because the exam
describes it rather than names it. The child is **drooling**, because
swallowing hurts too much. The child will not lie down; they sit forward,
propped on their hands, chin pushed out. That is the **tripod position**, and
it is the body opening the airway as wide as it will go.

Notice what is missing. **There is no barking cough.** The child is often
strikingly quiet, because making noise hurts. So the frightening presentation
is the quiet one.

If you want a four-word hook: **drooling, dysphagia, distress, dysphonia.**
Four Ds, and none of them belong to croup.

Now the intervention that matters more than any other, and it is a thing you
must **not** do.

**Do not put anything in that child's throat.** No tongue blade. No throat
swab. No looking with a torch. The epiglottis is swollen and inflamed, and
touching it can trigger complete laryngospasm — the airway closes entirely,
and now you are performing an emergency surgical airway on a four-year-old.

Everything you do instead is about keeping the child calm and undisturbed.
Leave them in the position they have chosen. Keep the caregiver with them,
because a crying child breathes worse. Do not force them to lie flat. Do not
start an IV in the corridor. The team assembles airway equipment and someone
skilled at intubation, and the throat is examined only in a place where the
airway can be secured immediately.

One more thing worth knowing: this used to be far more common. The Hib vaccine
is the reason it is now rare — which also means it turns up in unvaccinated
children.

---

**The thirty-second version.**

Croup — viral, gradual, barking seal-like cough, low fever, stridor. Steroids
and calm.

Epiglottitis — bacterial, sudden, high fever, drooling, tripod position, and
no cough at all. The quiet one is the emergency.

Nothing goes in the throat. Keep them calm, keep them upright, keep the
caregiver close, and have the airway team ready.
