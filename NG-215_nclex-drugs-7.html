STEP 2 · CLASS → MECHANISM → TRAP

One mechanism map, then one card per class with its own safety trap.

🎯 Where each class attacks the bacteria

🧠 "Wall, ribosome, DNA, folate" — four targets, four families. If you can name the target, you can guess the class.

💊 Penicillins Amoxil · Omnipen · Unipen · Pipracil · Zosyn

Mechanism: beta-lactam — disrupts bacterial cell wall synthesis, same family as cephalosporins and carbapenems.

⭐ Key nursing point: assess for a penicillin allergy history before every dose; watch for hives, wheeze, or anaphylaxis, especially with the first dose.

🚨 NCLEX trap: A penicillin allergy raises the same cross-reactivity question with cephalosporins and (less so) carbapenems — verify before substituting.

⚠️ Sulfonamides Bactrim · Bactrim DS · Septra · SMZ-TMP

Mechanism: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole blocks two sequential steps of bacterial folic acid synthesis, so bacteria can't make DNA.

⭐ Key nursing point: the source study sheet lists these as needing caution if sulfa-allergic: erythromycin-sulfisoxazole, sulfasalazine, dapsone, other sulfonamides, and — as commonly taught cross-sensitivity cautions — celecoxib (Celebrex), sumatriptan (Imitrex), furosemide (Lasix), and hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ). Clinical opinion on how strong this cross-reactivity really is varies — always ask about the specific reaction and follow facility/provider guidance.

🚨 NCLEX trap: Encourage fluids and sun protection — sulfonamides increase photosensitivity and can crystallize in concentrated urine.

🔴 Vancomycin Vancocin

Mechanism: inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis at a different site than the beta-lactams — often reserved for resistant gram-positive infections (e.g., MRSA).

⭐ Key nursing point: watch for nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, and Red Man Syndrome (flushing, itching, hypotension from histamine release with rapid infusion). Draw a peak about 30 minutes to 1 hour after the infusion ends, and a trough about 30 minutes before the next scheduled dose.

🚨 NCLEX trap: Red Man Syndrome is usually rate-related, not a true drug allergy — the nursing response is to slow the infusion, not necessarily discontinue the drug.

☀️ Tetracyclines Tetracycline · Vibramycin (Doxycycline)

Mechanism: bind bacterial ribosomes and block protein synthesis.

⭐ Key nursing point: increases photosensitivity — teach sunscreen/protective clothing. Do not take with dairy products, antacids, or iron supplements — calcium, magnesium, aluminum, and iron bind (chelate) the drug and reduce absorption.

🚨 NCLEX trap: Contraindicated in pregnancy and in children under 8 — can permanently discolor developing teeth and affect bone growth.

🦵 Fluoroquinolones Cipro · Levaquin

Mechanism: inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase, preventing bacterial DNA replication — name clue: the suffix -oxacin.

⭐ Key nursing point: black-box warning for tendon rupture (especially Achilles tendon) — teach patients to report tendon pain/swelling and to stop activity immediately if it occurs; adjust dosage for patients with renal impairment.

🚨 NCLEX trap: Tendon rupture risk is higher in older adults and those also taking corticosteroids — a combination worth flagging on a medication reconciliation.

🛡️ Carbapenems Invanz · Merrem · Primaxin

Mechanism: beta-lactams with a very broad spectrum of activity — often reserved for serious or multi-drug-resistant infections when other antibiotics have failed.

⭐ Key nursing point: assess for beta-lactam allergy history, same as with penicillins/cephalosporins. Imipenem (Primaxin) is paired with cilastatin, which blocks a kidney enzyme that would otherwise break the drug down too quickly.

🚨 NCLEX trap: Because these are often "last resort" drugs, unnecessary or overly broad use contributes to antibiotic resistance — appropriate stewardship is a testable nursing/interprofessional concept.

STEP 3 · SAFETY & PATIENT TEACHING

General antibiotic teaching, plus the three special-case safety rules that stand out on this page.

✅ General antibiotic teaching — all six classes

  • 💊 Finish the entire course even if feeling better
  • 🕐 Take at evenly spaced intervals to keep blood levels steady
  • 🦠 Report diarrhea that is watery or bloody — possible C. difficile superinfection
  • ❓ Always confirm allergy history before the first dose

📊 Three special safety rules on this page — don't mix them up

DrugUnique safety issue
VancomycinInfuse slowly to avoid Red Man Syndrome; monitor peak/trough, renal function, hearing
Fluoroquinolones ("-oxacin")Black-box tendon rupture warning, especially Achilles tendon
SulfonamidesPhotosensitivity, adequate hydration, and caution with the broader sulfa cross-sensitivity list

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What causes Red Man Syndrome, and how does the nurse prevent it?
Histamine release from vancomycin infused too fast — prevent by infusing slowly (typically over 60 minutes or more per protocol).
Q2: When should a vancomycin peak level be drawn?
About 30 minutes to 1 hour after the infusion ends. The trough is drawn about 30 minutes before the next dose.
Q3: What is the black-box warning for fluoroquinolones like Cipro and Levaquin?
Tendon rupture, especially of the Achilles tendon — teach patients to report tendon pain/swelling immediately.
Q4: What food/supplement interactions reduce tetracycline absorption?
Dairy products, antacids, and iron supplements — the calcium/magnesium/aluminum/iron chelate (bind) the drug.
