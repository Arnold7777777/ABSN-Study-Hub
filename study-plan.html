14-week semester plan

Three courses. One to two hours a day.

A fixed weekly rhythm so you never have to decide what to study, an exam overlay for the weeks that matter, and a way back in when you miss days.

🤰 NUR 234 🪑 NUR 235 🩺 NUR 258

⭐ The whole plan in six lines

  • One course a day, not three. Mon 258 · Tue 234 · Wed 235.
  • Thursday is quiz day — all three courses mixed together.
  • Friday goes to whatever you got wrong on Thursday.
  • Saturday is the long block: patho, A&P, and next week's preview.
  • Sunday off. Actually off.
  • When an exam is within 7 days, the exam overlay replaces Friday and Saturday.

The single most important rule on this page.

Never try to catch up. If you miss three days, you do not do four days' work on Thursday. You rejoin the plan at today and let the missed days go. A plan you rejoin beats a plan you abandon.

📅 Your default week

Why one course a day and not a bit of each. Switching subjects costs you the first ten minutes every time you do it. Three switches a day is thirty minutes of your hour gone before you learn anything.

One course a day means one switch. You get your whole hour.

Super Mega Quiz, shuffled, 25 questions. Do not filter by course. Mixing them up is the thing that transfers to NCLEX, where nothing is labelled. Write down every one you get wrong.

Take Thursday's wrong answers. Open the module page each one came from and re-learn just that chunk. Not the whole module.

🧬 The long block — 2 hours if you have them

Hour 1: pathophysiology and A&P for whatever did not make sense this week. Hour 2: skim next week's three modules in Spotlight mode, so Monday is not a cold start.

SUN
💤 Off

Genuinely off. If you want something, 15 minutes flipping through Spotlight pages counts and nothing more is required.

NUR 258 gets Monday on purpose. Its exams run about a week ahead of the other two all semester, so it is the course most often closest to an exam. Monday is the day you actually have energy.

⏱️ What one hour actually looks like

The hour has a fixed shape so you never spend any of it deciding. Same four blocks every single day.

0–10 Recall

Yesterday's three cards. Say the answers out loud before you look. Getting it wrong here is the point — that is what makes it stick.

10–35 Learn

Today's module in One bite. One bite, then Next. Do not scroll ahead to see how many are left.

35–50 Test

That module's quiz. Answer first, then check. Read the rationale even when you got it right — it explains the wrong answers too.

50–60 Capture

Write three lines on an index card. Just three. That card is tomorrow's first ten minutes.

🔢 If you have two hours

Do not stretch all four blocks. Repeat the middle: Recall, Learn, Test, Learn, Test, Capture. Two modules, or one module plus one ATI template.

Recall and Capture stay at ten minutes no matter how long the session is.

✅ Why it starts with a test and ends with writing

Starting with recall means the hour opens with your brain producing instead of consuming — that is the difference between recognising a fact and being able to retrieve it under exam pressure.

Ending with three lines gives the hour a finish line you can see. There is a card on the desk that was not there an hour ago.

🚨 When an exam is coming

With three courses you will almost always have an exam within seven days. So this is not the exception — it is most weeks. When it happens, Friday and Saturday switch over to the exam. Monday to Thursday do not change.

Do not stop the other two courses to cram for one. That is how you win an exam this week and lose the next two.

T−3
⭐ Spotlight only

Open every module page for that exam and hit Exam spotlight. Only the high-yield card. No reading anything else. That is 6–14 facts per module and it is the right amount three days out.

T−2
🧠 Quiz only

Mega Quiz, filtered to that exam. Every wrong answer → open that module, re-read that chunk only, move on.

T−1
💤 30 minutes, then stop

Skim your index cards. Nothing new. Then sleep.

No new material the night before. It does not go in, and it pushes out what was already there.

⚠️ Two exams in the same week

It happens roughly four times this semester — see the map below. When it does: the earlier exam gets T−3 and T−2. The later one gets the day between them. Do not split each day in half; you will do neither properly.

💡 The pattern worth seeing

NUR 258 sits one week ahead of the other two, nearly all semester. Its exam lands the week before 234 and 235 collide.

That gap is the plan's shock absorber. Weeks 5, 9 and 11 are your genuinely light weeks — use them to get ahead, not to rest twice.

🚨 Week 14 needs planning in week 11

Three finals and a unit exam in one week cannot be revised in that week. NUR 234's final is comprehensive over all fourteen weeks.

So from week 11, Saturday's second hour becomes final review — oldest material first, because week 1 is the part you have not seen since week 1.

📚 The review layer — content mastery & NCLEX

Older material does not get its own study day — there isn't one to give. It gets attached to the new material instead. Three rules, and they cost almost no extra time.

💊 Rule 1 — drugs get learned the day they appear

When a drug turns up in any course, open its card in the Drug Guide that same day. Two minutes.

Pharmacology is heavily weighted on NCLEX and it is the subject that decays fastest when studied alone. Learned attached to a patient, it stays.

🧬 Rule 2 — patho and A&P are the fallback, not a subject

You do not study pathophysiology on a schedule. You study it the moment something will not make sense.

When a mechanism will not stick — why oestrogen causes clots, why the right lower lobe aspirates, why insulin needs collapse after birth — that is Saturday hour 1. Go to Physiology or Pharmacology and fix that one thing.

A mechanism you understand needs revising once. A fact you memorised needs revising five times.

🧠 Rule 3 — Thursday carries the old courses

The Super Mega Quiz holds everything — NUR 125, 175, 198 and all three current courses. Shuffled, unfiltered.

That is not a shortcut, it is the whole point: NCLEX does not tell you which course a question came from. Practising retrieval without the label is the skill the exam actually tests.

Once a month, make Thursday NUR 198 / 175 / 125 only. That is your content mastery insurance.

🛠️ When it goes wrong

It will go wrong. Fourteen weeks, three courses, a job and a life. A plan that only works on good days is not a plan.

⏰ You have 15 minutes, not 60

Do Recall and Test only. Skip Learn entirely.

Fifteen minutes of retrieval beats sixty minutes of reading, and it beats zero by more than you would think. Never skip a day because you cannot do the whole hour.

📆 You missed three days

Rejoin at today. If today is Wednesday, you do Wednesday.

The missed material comes back on Thursday in the quiz anyway — that is what the mixed quiz is for. It finds your gaps without you having to remember where they are.

Do not build a catch-up backlog. Backlogs are what make people quit in week 6.

🧠 You cannot start

Open the module page, press One bite, and read one bite. Then you are allowed to stop.

You almost certainly will not stop — but the deal has to be real, or it stops working. The hard part is starting, not continuing.

👀 You read it and nothing went in

That is reading, not studying, and it is the commonest way to waste an hour. Close the page and try to say the last chunk out loud.

If you cannot, that is your signal — go back and do that chunk as a quiz instead of a read. If you could, you did not need to re-read it anyway.

⚖️ Falling behind in one course

Let it be the one whose exam is furthest away — deliberately, and on purpose, not by accident.

Then use the next light week (5, 9 or 11) to bring it back. Choosing what to drop is a plan. Dropping whatever you happen to run out of time for is not.

✅ Before week 1 — a short setup list

You have about a week and a half. Do not study ahead. Do these instead — they are worth more than a head start on week 1 content.

  • Get the three syllabi and write the real exam dates onto the map above.
  • Pick your hour — the same hour every day. Which hour matters far less than it being the same one.
  • Buy index cards. Physical ones. The Capture block needs something your hands do, away from a screen.
  • Do one practice day now on any module, so week 1 is not also the week you learn the routine.
  • Tell someone the plan. Out loud, to a person.
