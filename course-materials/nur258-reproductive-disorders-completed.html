8 / 108Pap/HPV testing: client education

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Before: explain the purpose and steps, obtain permission and provide privacy. For a Pap test, avoid intercourse, douching and vaginal medicines/products for 2 days when instructed.

During: empty the bladder, position comfortably in lithotomy and use a chaperone according to preference/policy. Tell the client to report pain; they may ask to pause.

After: mild spotting can occur. Explain when/how results will arrive and the follow-up plan; abnormal screening is not the same as a cancer diagnosis.

12 / 108Endometrial biopsy: nursing care

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Before: verify consent, pregnancy status, allergies, bleeding history and anticoagulant use; explain cramping and the ordered pain-control plan. Have the client empty the bladder.

During: support positioning/privacy, assist with aseptic technique, monitor pain or a vasovagal reaction and label the tissue specimen accurately.

After: assess pain/bleeding and provide a pad. Follow prescribed pelvic-rest instructions; report heavy bleeding, fever, foul discharge or severe pain. Confirm the pathology follow-up plan.

14 / 108Hysterosalpingogram: nursing care

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Before: confirm no pregnancy or active pelvic infection; review prior contrast reactions. Schedule early in the cycle as directed and give ordered analgesic/antibiotic.

During: explain contrast-related cramping, support lithotomy positioning/privacy and monitor for pain, faintness or allergic reaction.

After: mild cramps and blood-tinged drainage may occur; use a pad. Report fever, worsening pain, heavy bleeding, foul discharge or allergic symptoms and follow the clinician’s activity/sexual-care instructions.

Sources for this addition

  • ACOG: Hysterosalpingography

20 / 108Fibrocystic Breast Disease

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Fibrocystic Breast Disease

Occurs in premenopausal women, often young adults

Can also develop with the initiation of HRT in post-menopausal women

Symptoms: tender breast lumps/cysts

Confirmed with breast ultrasound that reveals multiple cysts

Mammograms often not useful in young adults due to breast density

Fine needle aspiration can be done to reduce tender cysts

Treatment: reduce salt prior to menses, avoid caffeine, supportive bra, ibuprofen/acetaminophen, warm/cool compresses, oral birth control pills, and diuretics

Breast Cancer

Second-leading cause of cancer deaths in females in the U.S.

Woman’s risk of developing breast cancer is about 12% or one in eight

Approximately 268,600 new cases of invasive breast cancer are diagnosed in women each year

Risk of developing breast cancer increases with age

Common sites of metastasis are bone, lung, brain, liver

Nonsurgical Management of Breast Cancer

Hormonal therapy

Most effective in cancer cells with estrogen receptors that can be inhibited

Selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) — tamoxifen

Used for those who are high risk for breast cancer or have advanced breast cancer

Suppresses the growth of cancer cells

Increases the risk of endometrial cancer (vaginal bleeding)

Increases the risk of DVT/PE (leg swelling or SOB)

Common side effect is hot flashes

49 / 108Hysterectomy: fill in the procedure meanings

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Total: uterus and cervix. Subtotal/supracervical: uterine body removed, cervix remains.

Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (BSO): both fallopian tubes and ovaries; it is a separate removal that may accompany hysterectomy.

Panhysterectomy: commonly used for uterus/cervix plus both tubes/ovaries; confirm the operative definition. Radical: uterus/cervix, surrounding supporting tissue and upper vagina, with node assessment as indicated; ovaries are not automatically removed.

Hysterectomy Perioperative Care

Preoperative care

Negative pregnancy test

Administer preop antibiotics

Place anti-embolism stockings

Postoperative care

Indwelling urinary catheter inserted intraoperatively

Kept in place for 24 hr.

Monitor vaginal bleeding

Excessive bleeding 1 pad saturation in 4 hours

Monitor for signs of infection, atelectasis, paralytic ileus, wound dehiscence, DVT, bleeding, and hypovolemia

66 / 108Human Papilloma Virus (HPV)

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Human Papilloma Virus (HPV)

Most common sexually transmitted infection in the US

Vaccine

Starting at age 11 or 12 before they are sexually active and available for those 9-26 years of age

Treatment of genital warts

Topical agents or surgical removal of warts

Associated with cervical dysplasia and cervical cancer

Need regular Pap smears

If cervical dysplasia is present, will need colposcopy, and further treatment

Also increased risk of vulvar, vaginal, oropharyngeal, and penile cancers

Genital Herpes (HSV)

Recurrent, lifelong viral infection

More than 87% of infected individuals are unaware

Education:

68 / 108Trichomoniasis (Trich)

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Trichomoniasis (Trich)

Protozoal infection transmitted by asymptomatic carrier with infection of urogenital tract

Only about 30% of cases are symptomatic

Vulvovaginal itching and burning

BPH Medications

Goal is to re-establish unobstructed urine flow from the bladder

Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)-lowering medications (finasteride)

Inhibits the enzyme 5-alpha-reductase, which converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT). This reduces DHT levels, decreases prostate size, and improves symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH).

96 / 108CBI: are you getting out what goes in?

Open full size
Read slide text and study notes

Calculate actual urine: total drainage minus irrigant infused for the same interval. Example: 3,000 mL drainage − 2,400 mL irrigation = 600 mL urine.

Less drainage than irrigation, suprapubic pain/distention or leakage can mean obstruction. Assess tubing and stop inflow if outflow is blocked; follow the ordered irrigation protocol and notify the team.

Persistent bright-red bleeding, large clots or unstable vital signs need urgent escalation. Increasing irrigation alone does not treat uncontrolled hemorrhage.
