1 / 2 Caring for a client with a neurologic disorder

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Neuro assessment

  • Baseline: compare with the child's usual behavior and the caregiver's report.
  • LOC first: alertness, orientation/response and pediatric GCS (eye, verbal, motor).
  • Check pupils (size, equality, reaction), limb movement/strength and sensation.
  • Trend HR, BP, breathing pattern, temperature, oxygen saturation and pain.
  • Infants: head circumference, fontanel, sutures, feeding, cry and tone.
  • Urgent change: falling LOC, new weakness, unequal or nonreactive pupils, or seizures.

Diagnostic tests

  • CT: rapid look for injury or bleeding. MRI: detailed brain and spine anatomy.
  • Head ultrasound: infant ventricles through an open fontanel. EEG: seizure activity.
  • LP/CSF: cells, glucose, protein, Gram stain and culture; obtain ordered blood cultures.
  • LP safety: suspected dangerous raised ICP or mass effect needs medical review first. Do not delay antibiotics.
  • Labs: glucose, electrolytes and CBC as indicated.

VP shunt

  • Drains ventricular CSF to the peritoneum.
  • Assess neuro checks, infant head growth, the incision and the abdomen.
  • After surgery: follow positioning/activity orders; no pressure on the incision.
  • Report urgently: headache, vomiting, unusual sleepiness, bulging fontanel, fever, redness or abdominal pain.
  • Teach follow-up and malfunction signs. Programmable valve: ask about checks after MRI.

Medication management

  • Seizures: rescue midazolam/diazepam per plan; maintenance medicines as ordered.
  • Raised ICP: ordered hypertonic saline (your M7 page also lists mannitol). Monitor sodium, fluids and neuro status.
  • Infection: IV antibiotics; dexamethasone in selected cases.
  • Spasticity: baclofen or botulinum toxin when prescribed.
  • Verify weight-based doses, watch breathing/sedation, never stop antiseizure drugs abruptly.

Intracranial pressure

  • Rising ICP: headache, vomiting, irritability, reduced alertness. Infants: bulging fontanel, high-pitched cry.
  • Late sign — Cushing triad: hypertension with widening pulse pressure, bradycardia, irregular respirations. EMERGENCY.
  • Head midline, HOB about 30° if ordered; avoid neck/hip flexion and straining.
  • Serial neuro checks, fluid balance, ordered ICP/CPP targets. CPP = MAP − ICP.

Seizure nursing care

  • During: stay, call for help, time it, clear hazards, protect the head; turn to the side when safe.
  • Airway: watch breathing, oxygen and suction ready, check glucose when feasible.
  • Never restrain or put anything in the mouth. Nothing by mouth until fully alert.
  • Escalate at 5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, or breathing difficulty; give rescue medicine per plan.
  • After: reassess airway/LOC, let the child rest, document, teach the seizure action plan.

2 / 2 Neuro disorders: hydrocephalus to cerebral palsy

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Hydrocephalus

  • CSF builds up in the ventricles from blocked flow or poor absorption.
  • Clues: fast head growth, bulging fontanel, separated sutures, “sunset” eyes; older child: headache, vomiting, drowsiness.
  • Care: trend head size and neuro status; assess feeding, vomiting and skin.
  • Treat: VP shunt or selected endoscopic surgery; return urgently for renewed symptoms or fever.

Bacterial meningitis

  • Bacterial infection inflames the meninges; risk of sepsis and raised ICP.
  • Clues: fever, headache, stiff neck, photophobia. Infant: poor feeding, irritability, bulging fontanel. Nonblanching rash is urgent.
  • Prompt IV antibiotics; cultures/LP when safe, without delaying treatment. Monitor neuro status, seizures, fluids.
  • Meningococcal/Hib: droplet + standard precautions until 24 h of effective antibiotics. Vaccines, contact prophylaxis, hearing follow-up.

Head injury

  • Trauma causes concussion, bleeding or swelling; hypoxia and hypotension worsen it.
  • Clues: altered LOC, headache, vomiting, seizures, unequal pupils.
  • ABCs and cervical-spine protection if injury is suspected; serial GCS, pupils, vital signs; CT when indicated.
  • Escalate worsening drowsiness, repeated vomiting or new deficits. After concussion: gradual return to school and activity.

Myelomeningocele

  • Neural tube defect: meninges and spinal cord/nerves in an open sac on the back.
  • Clues: leg weakness or sensory loss, bladder/bowel dysfunction; hydrocephalus may follow.
  • Before repair: prone or side-lying; sterile, saline-moistened nonadherent dressing; prevent drying and soiling; latex-free supplies.
  • After: watch for infection, skin, head growth and movement; bladder/bowel program and catheterization plan.

Cerebral palsy

  • Nonprogressive injury or abnormal development of the immature brain affects movement and posture.
  • Clues: delayed motor skills, abnormal tone, spasticity/scissoring, poor coordination.
  • Care: feeding/swallowing, communication, skin and mobility; prevent contractures; assess for seizures.
  • Early PT/OT/speech therapy, braces, selected medicines or surgery; support independence and family goals.
