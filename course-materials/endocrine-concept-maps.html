1 / 3 Growth, thyroid and parathyroid

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Growth hormone deficiency

  • Poor linear growth; delayed bone age.
  • Plot growth velocity; endocrine testing.
  • Prescribed somatropin; rotate injections.
  • Monitor growth/glucose; report headache, vision change or hip pain.

Hypoparathyroidism

  • Low PTH → low calcium, high phosphate.
  • Tingling, cramps, tetany or seizures.
  • Monitor calcium, phosphate and ECG.
  • Calcium + active vitamin D as ordered; urgent treatment for symptomatic hypocalcemia.

Congenital hypothyroidism

  • Often detected on newborn screen before obvious signs.
  • May have prolonged jaundice, poor feeding, hypotonia, constipation.
  • Confirm TSH/free T4; start levothyroxine promptly.
  • Protect brain development and growth; frequent lab follow-up.

Hyperthyroidism

  • Excess thyroid hormone: heat intolerance, tachycardia, tremor, weight loss.
  • Usually Graves disease in children.
  • Methimazole and symptom control as ordered.
  • Fever/sore throat on antithyroid drug: urgent assessment.

2 / 3 Metabolic disorders and medicines

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Phenylketonuria

  • Cannot adequately break down phenylalanine.
  • Lifelong controlled phenylalanine intake + medical formula.
  • Avoid aspartame; monitor blood levels.
  • NOT a zero-protein diet.

Galactosemia

  • Cannot properly metabolize galactose.
  • Milk exposure can cause vomiting, jaundice, poor growth or sepsis.
  • Use prescribed lactose/galactose-free feeding.
  • NOT avoidance of all carbohydrates.

Type 2 diabetes

  • Insulin resistance with progressive beta-cell dysfunction.
  • May have polyuria, thirst or acanthosis nigricans.
  • Glucose/A1c testing; evaluate symptoms urgently.
  • Family-based nutrition/activity plan.
  • Metformin, insulin or other age-approved medicine as prescribed.

Medications and too much / too little

  • GH deficiency: somatropin; report severe headache or limp.
  • Hypothyroidism: levothyroxine; excess causes hyperthyroid symptoms.
  • Hyperthyroidism: methimazole; overtreatment can cause hypothyroidism.
  • Hypoparathyroidism: calcium/calcitriol; excess can cause hypercalcemia.
  • PKU: medical formula; sapropterin for eligible patients.
  • Galactosemia: dietary treatment; specialist-directed supplements.
  • Diabetes: excess insulin can cause hypoglycemia; monitor glucose.

Read the original shorthand carefully

The source crosses out “protein” and “carbs.” PKU requires controlled phenylalanine with adequate nutrition; galactosemia restricts galactose/lactose, not every carbohydrate. The added notes make those distinctions explicit.

3 / 3 Type 1 diabetes

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Cause

  • Autoimmune beta-cell destruction → little/no insulin.
  • Lifelong insulin replacement.

Assessment

  • Polyuria = much urination.
  • Polydipsia = much thirst.
  • Polyphagia, weight loss, fatigue.
  • Vomiting + deep fruity breathing: evaluate for DKA.

Diagnosis

  • Elevated blood glucose; A1c assesses chronic exposure.
  • Check ketones/acidosis if ill.
  • Autoantibodies help classify diabetes.

Treatment

  • Basal + mealtime insulin or pump.
  • Glucose/CGM monitoring, carbohydrate matching.
  • Teach injection sites and hypoglycemia care.

Sick days

  • Do not omit basal insulin.
  • Check glucose/ketones more often; follow fluid and call instructions.

Exercise

  • Check glucose; carry fast carbohydrate.
  • Adjust food/insulin per plan.
  • Avoid exercise with significant ketones; hypoglycemia may be delayed.

Hypoglycemia

  • Sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion.
  • Fast carbohydrate if safe to swallow; recheck per plan. Severe: glucagon/help.

Hyperglycemia

  • Thirst, urination, fatigue.
  • Check glucose/ketones; follow correction plan.

Client teaching

  • Medical ID; school/emergency plan.
  • Rotate sites; safe sharps disposal.
  • Know when to call for vomiting, ketones or persistent high/low glucose.

DKA

  • Insulin deficiency → ketones + metabolic acidosis; dehydration.
  • IV fluids and insulin under pediatric protocol.
  • Frequent glucose, electrolytes, neurologic and cardiac monitoring.
  • Potassium may fall during treatment.
  • Worsening headache/mental status: urgent escalation.
