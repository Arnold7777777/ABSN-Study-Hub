1 / 4 Upper respiratory illnesses

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Nasopharyngitis

  • Viral: congestion, runny nose, cough.
  • Saline + gentle suction; fluids and rest.
  • Antibiotics do not treat a cold.

Allergic rhinitis

  • Itchy nose/eyes, sneezing, clear drainage.
  • Reduce triggers; prescribed antihistamine or nasal steroid.
  • Usually no fever.

Bacterial epiglottitis

  • Drooling, tripod posture, stridor, toxic appearance.
  • AIRWAY emergency: keep calm; call airway team.
  • Do not inspect throat with tongue blade.

Pharyngitis

  • Viral: often cough/runny nose.
  • Strep: fever, sore throat, tender nodes; test as ordered.
  • Confirmed strep: complete antibiotics; fluids and pain relief.

Croup

  • Barking cough, hoarseness, inspiratory stridor.
  • Keep calm; corticosteroid as ordered.
  • Stridor at rest/severe distress: urgent assessment; nebulized epinephrine as ordered.

Tonsillectomy

  • Watch for frequent swallowing, bright-red bleeding, tachycardia.
  • Report suspected hemorrhage immediately.
  • Airway, hydration, prescribed analgesia; follow diet/activity instructions.

Otitis media

  • Ear pain, fever, irritability; otoscopic diagnosis.
  • Pain relief; antibiotics when indicated.
  • Upright feeding, smoke avoidance; reassess persistent symptoms.

COVID-19

  • Fever, cough, sore throat; variable severity.
  • Testing and isolation per current policy.
  • Support hydration; assess breathing and oxygenation.
  • Escalate distress or dehydration.

2 / 4 Respiratory assessment and lower airway illness

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Respiratory assessment

  • Rate, rhythm, effort and breath sounds.
  • Retractions, flaring, grunting, stridor or wheeze.
  • SpO2, color, alertness, feeding and urine output.
  • Apnea, exhaustion or cyanosis = urgent escalation.

Nursing interventions

  • Position for easier breathing; minimize distress.
  • Suction nasal secretions when needed.
  • Oxygen and hydration as prescribed.
  • Frequent reassessment, intake/output, infection precautions.
  • Avoid oral feeds if breathing makes swallowing unsafe.

Medications

  • Albuterol: bronchospasm, not routine RSV treatment.
  • Corticosteroids: asthma/croup when ordered.
  • Antibiotics: bacterial infection.
  • Antivirals: selected influenza/COVID cases.
  • Antipyretics: weight-based orders; no aspirin for viral illness.

Pneumonia

  • Fever, cough, tachypnea, crackles; possible hypoxemia.
  • Support oxygenation, fluids and comfort.
  • Antibiotics if bacterial; monitor work of breathing and response.

Influenza A and B

  • Abrupt fever, aches, fatigue and cough.
  • Fluids, rest; prescribed antiviral for eligible patients.
  • Annual vaccination; respiratory precautions.

Bronchiolitis / RSV

  • Infant: congestion progresses to tachypnea, wheeze/crackles, poor feeding.
  • Young infants may have apnea.
  • Supportive care: suction, oxygen if hypoxemic, safe hydration.
  • Bronchodilators, steroids and antibiotics are not routine.
  • Monitor apnea, fatigue and intake/output.

3 / 4 Asthma

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Cause

  • Inflammation + bronchospasm.
  • Triggers: allergens, virus, exercise, smoke.

Assessment

  • Wheeze, cough, prolonged expiration.
  • Retractions, dyspnea, reduced activity.
  • Silent chest or exhaustion can mean severe obstruction.

Diagnosis

  • History + variable airflow limitation.
  • Spirometry when age permits.
  • Peak flow tracks control; compare with personal best.

Treatment

  • Reliever for symptoms; controller for prevention.
  • Follow the written asthma action plan.

Bronchodilator

  • Albuterol relaxes airways.
  • Use spacer correctly.
  • Monitor tremor and fast pulse.

Anti-inflammatory

  • Inhaled steroid controls inflammation.
  • Use consistently; rinse mouth.
  • Not a rescue drug.

Education

  • Teach inhaler/spacer technique and trigger reduction.
  • Peak flow: green 80–100%, yellow 50–79%, red <50% of personal best.
  • Follow action plan; seek urgent help for severe distress or poor response.

Complications

  • Status asthmaticus; hypoxemia; respiratory failure. Escalate promptly.

4 / 4 Cystic fibrosis

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Cause

  • CFTR defect (autosomal recessive) → thick mucus, salty sweat.

Assessment

  • Chronic cough; recurrent chest infections.
  • Poor growth, bulky greasy stools.
  • Digital clubbing; dehydration risk.

Diagnosis

  • Newborn screen prompts confirmatory testing.
  • Sweat chloride test + CFTR genetic assessment.

Pulmonary effects

  • Thick mucus obstructs airways.
  • Infection and inflammation damage lungs.

GI effects

  • Blocked pancreatic ducts → enzyme deficiency.
  • Fat/protein malabsorption, steatorrhea, poor growth.

Endocrine effects

  • Pancreatic injury can cause CF-related diabetes.
  • Monitor growth and glucose as directed.

Pulmonary treatment

  • Airway clearance; prescribed inhaled medicines/antibiotics.
  • CFTR modulator if eligible.

GI treatment

  • Pancreatic enzymes with every meal/snack.
  • High-energy nutrition; fat-soluble vitamins and salt per plan.
  • Do not crush enzyme beads.

Endocrine treatment

  • Insulin for CF-related diabetes as ordered.
  • Coordinate glucose care with nutrition and illness needs.
