A nurse is teaching a child who has asthma how to use a peak flow meter. Which of the following information should the nurse include in the teaching?

Record the average number of attempts
Perform three attempts
Deliver a long, slow breath into the meter
Sit in a chair with feet on the floor

Answer: b
Rationale: When teaching a child with asthma to use a peak flow meter, the nurse should instruct the child to perform three attempts and record the highest of the three readings—not the average. This ensures the most accurate measurement of peak expiratory flow, which helps monitor asthma control. The child should stand (if possible), take a deep breath in, place the mouthpiece in the mouth, and then blow out as hard and fast as possible—not a long, slow breath, which would be incorrect for this device. Sitting in a chair is acceptable if standing is not possible, but it is not the preferred or required method unless specified.

A nurse is providing discharge teaching for a child who has cystic fibrosis. Which of the following should the nurse include? 

Administer pancreatic enzymes with meals and snacks.
Implement a fluid restriction during times of infection.
Restrict physical activity.
Provide a low-calorie, low protein diet. 

Answer- a 
Rationale- Children with cystic fibrosis (CF) often have pancreatic insufficiency, meaning their pancreas does not produce enough enzymes to properly digest food. To support digestion and nutrient absorption, pancreatic enzymes must be administered with all meals and snacks. This helps prevent malnutrition and supports growth and development.
The other options are incorrect:
Implement a fluid restriction during times of infection: This is inappropriate. Children with CF are at risk for dehydration, especially during illness, so adequate fluid intake is essential, not restriction.
Restrict physical activity: Physical activity is actually encouraged for children with CF, as it helps improve lung function and overall well-being.
Provide a low-calorie, low-protein diet: Children with CF typically require a high-calorie, high-protein diet to meet their increased energy needs and support growth, due to malabsorption and chronic illness.

A nurse in the ED is assessing a newly-admitted infant. Which of the following findings is an early indication of hypoxemia?
Nonproductive cough
Hypoventilation
Tachypnea
Stridor 

Answer- c
Rationale- Tachypnea (increased respiratory rate) is an early sign of hypoxemia in infants. When oxygen levels begin to drop, the body attempts to compensate by increasing the respiratory rate to improve oxygen delivery to tissues. Recognizing this early sign is critical to preventing further respiratory compromise.
Incorrect options:
Nonproductive cough: This is a nonspecific symptom and not a reliable early indicator of hypoxemia. It can occur with many respiratory conditions, including viral infections or allergies.
Hypoventilation: This is typically a late sign of respiratory failure, indicating poor respiratory effort or fatigue, which can follow prolonged hypoxemia if unaddressed.
Stridor: Stridor is a high-pitched, inspiratory sound indicating upper airway obstruction, such as croup or foreign body aspiration. While it is a concerning respiratory finding, it is not a general early sign of hypoxemia.

An infant weighs 8 pounds and requires IV maintenance fluids. What would the hourly IV rate be for this client based on a daily fluid requirement of 100 ml/kg/day?

Correct Answer: 15 mL/hr
Rationale: To calculate the hourly IV maintenance fluid rate for an infant based on a requirement of 100 mL/kg/day, follow these steps:
Convert the infant's weight from pounds to kilograms:
8 lb÷2.2=3.636363 kg 
Calculate the total daily fluid requirement:
100 mL/kg/day×3.63636 kg=363.636363 mL/day
Divide by 24 hours to find the hourly rate:
363.636363÷24=15.15151515 mL/hr
Round to the nearest whole number, if appropriate:
≈15 mL/hr
Therefore, the correct IV maintenance fluid rate is 15 mL/hr.
