Congenital Heart Defects
Disorder Definition Symptoms Diagnosis Treatment
Atrial Septal Defect
Acyanotic
Opening in the atrial septum allowing blood
to shunt from the left to right atria.
s/s of L-sided HF: increased WOB
(tachypnea, dyspnea, crackles),
poor weight gain
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
No tx if asymptomatic
management of symptoms:
furosemide
surgical closure: occlusion via
cardiac cath
Ventricular Septal Defect
Acyanotic
Opening in the ventricular septum allowing
blood flow from the left to the right ventricle
s/s of L-sided HF: increased WOB
(tachypnea, dyspnea, crackles),
poor weight gain
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
No treatment if asymptomatic
Medication symptom management:
furosemide
Some types closed via cardiac cath
Others closed through a median
sternotomy 
Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)
Acyanotic
Opening allowing blood flow from the aorta
to the pulmonary artery.
s/s of L-sided HF: increased WOB
(tachypnea, dyspnea, crackles),
poor weight gain
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
Medicinal closure: Indomethacin
(prostaglandin inhibitor)
Additional medication
management: furosemide
Surgical closure (PDA ligation or
occlusion via cardiac cath
Coarctation of the Aorta (COA)
Can be cyanotic or acyanotic based on severity
 Localized narrowing of the aorta at the
ductus arteriosus, restricting systemic blood
flow (specifically to lower extremities)
◦ Upper extremities: Elevated BP
(commonly in right arm), bounding
pulses
Lower extremities: Hypotension.
weak/absent pulses, cool skin
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
Surgical repair-balloon angioplasty
(repair may be delayed based on
severity)
*** Symptoms and treatment vary based on severity of the defect
***Blood always takes the path of least resistance (goes from high pressure to low pressure)

Tricuspid Atresia
Cyanotic
 Failure of tricuspid valve to develop= no
communication between RA and RV
(shunting from right to left)
Cyanosis
Chronic hypoxemia in older
children
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
Surgical repair
Prior to surgery: continuous
infusion of prostaglandin E1- keep
the PDA open!
Tetrology of Fallot (TOF)
Cyanotic
Four defects: VSD, pulmonary stenosis,
overriding aorta, R ventricular hypertrophy.
Cyanosis
Hypercyanotic “Tet” spells
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-ray,
ECG
Surgical repair
Prior to surgery: continuous
infusion of Prostaglandin E1- keep
PDA open!
Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA)
Cyanotic
Pulmonary artery and aorta switched
positions, causing systemic circulation of
oxygen-poor blood (no communication
between right and left side)
Profound cyanosis
Upper extremities SpO2 < lower
extremities
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-Ray,
ECG
Surgical repair
Prior to surgery: continuous
infusion of Prostaglandin E1- keep
PDA open!
Acquired Heart Disease
Kawasaki Disease  Generalized vasculitis of all blood vessels
and coronary arteries
Initial: prolonged fevers unrelieved
by antipyretics
REDNESS (eyes, mucous
membranes, strawberry tongue)
Chapping/peeling to lips/hands/
feet
Cervical lymphadenopathy
Cardiac: dysrhythmias,
myocarditis
Clinical evaluation,
Echocardiogram,
Chest X-Ray
ECG
Laboratory data
IV Gamma Globulin (IVIG), 
ASA, 
Hospitalization during acute phase
Rheumatic Fever
 Autoimmune response due to group A beta-
hemolytic strep (GABHS) leading to
inflammatory lesions in the heart, blood
vessels, brain, and joints
Jones criteria: carditis (most
serious), polyarthritis, chorea,
macular rash, subcutaneous
nodules, fever, arthralgia
Clinical evaluation
Echocardiogram
Chest X-Ray
Laboratory data
◦ Penicillin (treat GABHS)
◦ NSAIDs (reduce
inflammation/joint pain)
◦ Phenobarbital and diazepam (to
reduce chorea)
