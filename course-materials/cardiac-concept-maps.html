1 / 3 Congenital defects: pulmonary flow and mixing

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

PDA and medications

  • Aorta–pulmonary artery connection; left-to-right shunt.
  • Open: prostaglandin E1 if duct-dependent.
  • Close: ibuprofen/indomethacin in selected preterm infants; catheter/surgery.

ASD

  • Opening between atria → usually left-to-right shunt.
  • Often asymptomatic; murmur/fixed split S2.
  • Larger defects may need closure.
  • Left-sided HF clues: tachypnea, crackles, feeding fatigue and pulmonary congestion.

VSD

  • Opening between ventricles → usually left-to-right shunt.
  • Murmur; large shunt causes pulmonary overcirculation, tachypnea, poor feeding/growth.
  • Small defects may close; HF treatment and repair when indicated.

Tricuspid atresia

  • Absent tricuspid opening restricts right-heart flow.
  • Survival requires mixing and a route to lungs.
  • Cyanosis; prostaglandin if duct-dependent.
  • Staged surgical palliation.

Transposition of great arteries

  • Aorta from RV; pulmonary artery from LV → parallel circuits.
  • Blood must mix through ASD/VSD/PDA.
  • Severe neonatal cyanosis.
  • Prostaglandin, possible atrial septostomy; arterial switch repair.

Tetralogy of Fallot

  • Four: VSD, pulmonary outflow obstruction, overriding aorta, RV hypertrophy.
  • Cyanosis depends on obstruction.
  • Hypercyanotic spell: calm child, knee-chest position, urgent help/O2 and ordered treatment.
  • Repair; prostaglandin only when pulmonary flow is duct-dependent.

2 / 3 Obstruction, heart failure and procedures

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Coarctation of aorta

  • Narrow aorta → high arm BP, weak femoral pulses, lower leg BP.
  • Compare upper/lower extremity pulses and pressures.
  • Critical newborn: shock as duct closes.
  • Prostaglandin if duct-dependent; catheter/surgical repair.

Pulmonary stenosis

  • Obstruction from RV to pulmonary artery.
  • RV workload rises; murmur, exertional symptoms.
  • Severe disease can cause cyanosis.
  • Balloon valvuloplasty or surgery; critical newborn may need prostaglandin.

Aortic stenosis

  • Obstructed LV outflow; increased LV workload.
  • Murmur; exertional syncope/chest pain are concerning.
  • Critical newborn may develop low output/shock.
  • Activity advice and catheter/surgical treatment by cardiology.

Cardiac medications

  • Diuretics: reduce congestion; check weight, electrolytes and urine.
  • ACE inhibitors: reduce afterload; monitor BP, renal function and potassium.
  • Digoxin: prescribed contractility support; apical pulse and toxicity monitoring.
  • Prostaglandin E1: maintains ductal flow; monitor apnea and BP.
  • Use individualized pediatric dose/hold parameters.

Nursing interventions for HF

  • Small, frequent high-energy feeds; rest breaks.
  • Daily weight; strict intake/output.
  • Monitor work of breathing, perfusion and oxygenation.
  • Cluster care; oxygen as prescribed.
  • Follow fluid/sodium and medication plan; teach worsening signs.

Cardiac catheterization

  • Before: consent, fasting, baseline pulses and allergies.
  • After: monitor access site, bleeding, distal pulses/color/temp.
  • Keep affected limb positioned as ordered; check vitals and urine.
  • Report bleeding or impaired limb perfusion immediately.

3 / 3 Acquired inflammatory cardiac conditions

🔎 Open full size

Completed study notes · suggested additions

Kawasaki disease

  • Systemic vasculitis; coronary artery aneurysm risk.
  • Persistent fever with red eyes, red/cracked lips or strawberry tongue, rash, extremity changes and cervical node enlargement.
  • Incomplete presentations also occur.
  • Echocardiography and inflammatory labs support evaluation.
  • IV immunoglobulin + aspirin under specialist direction.
  • Monitor cardiac status, hydration and response.
  • Follow cardiology/echo plan; discuss live-vaccine timing after IVIG.

Rheumatic fever

  • Immune complication after group A streptococcal infection.
  • Major Jones manifestations: carditis, migratory polyarthritis, chorea, erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules.
  • Diagnosis uses criteria plus evidence of preceding infection.
  • Eradicate strep; anti-inflammatory treatment and cardiac care.
  • Long-term antibiotic prophylaxis may prevent recurrence.
  • Teach completion of antibiotics and follow-up.
  • Main concern: lasting valve damage.
