Suggested completion of discussion prompts

Congenital / neonatal hypothyroidism

  • Primary hypothyroidism typically has elevated TSH and low free T4. Maternal antithyroid medicine can cause transient neonatal hypothyroidism.
  • Early levothyroxine protects brain development and growth. Monitor thyroid labs, growth and development.
  • Give the exact prescribed preparation/dose consistently. A tablet may be crushed in a small approved volume; do not hide it in a full bottle. Separate interfering calcium/iron/soy according to treatment instructions.

✓ Reveal answer & explanation

Suggested completion of unlabeled comparison

Thyroid hormone: too much, just right, too little

  • Too much thyroid hormone: tachycardia, heat intolerance, tremor, sweating, weight loss and irritability.
  • Just right: age-appropriate growth/activity and thyroid tests in the treatment target range.
  • Too little thyroid hormone: fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, slowed growth and weight gain.
  • Medicine direction matters: excess levothyroxine can cause excess hormone; excess methimazole can lower hormone too far.

7 / 13 Name the disorder

🔎 Open full size
✓ Reveal answer & explanation

Suggested completion of discussion prompts

Possible growth hormone deficiency

  • Poor height velocity and delayed bone age prompt assessment; short stature alone does not confirm deficiency.
  • Evaluation: growth chart/velocity, bone age, IGF-1 and endocrine-directed stimulation testing; investigate other causes.
  • Somatropin is given as prescribed; rotate injection sites. Monitor growth and glucose; report headache/vision change or hip/knee pain/limp.
  • Support adherence with a family routine, calendar and teach-back.

9 / 13 Name the disorder…

🔎 Open full size
✓ Reveal answer & explanation

Suggested completion of discussion prompts

Diabetes insipidus

  • High-volume dilute urine, thirst, sodium 148, urine specific gravity 1.002 and low urine osmolality favor DI. Normal glucose argues against osmotic diuresis from diabetes mellitus.
  • Central DI: insufficient ADH; nephrogenic DI: kidneys resist ADH. Primary polydipsia is another differential.
  • Track intake/output, weight, sodium and hydration. Maintain access to water; specialist-directed fluids.
  • Desmopressin treats central DI. Follow fluid/dose instructions to avoid water intoxication and hyponatremia.

Hyperthyroidism

  • Tachycardia, tremor, heat intolerance and weight loss despite appetite suggest excess thyroid hormone.
  • Methimazole is commonly used in children; a beta blocker may control symptoms. Radioiodine or surgery is selected individually.
  • Fever or sore throat during antithyroid therapy needs urgent evaluation for agranulocytosis; jaundice also requires prompt review.
