SLOW

STEP 1 · WHY GIVE IT

Spot the slow heart that is actually in trouble — the key word buys the drug.

🐢 Indication — symptomatic bradycardia

Atropine is given to speed up a slow heart rate with symptomatic bradycardia.

🔑 Key word: Symptomatic — the exam clue is the word, not just the number.

🧠 Drug name trick: Atro-PINE 🌲 — picture a PINE tree: tall & upright. Atropine stands a slumping heart rate back up.

🚩 Signs: symptomatic bradycardia — mental status changes

  • 1. Agitation 😤
  • 2. Irritability 😠
  • 3. Confusion 😵‍💫
🧠 A slow pump makes a grumpy brain — A·I·C: Agitated · Irritable · Confused.
💡

SWITCH

STEP 2 · HOW IT WORKS

One drug, one flick: brake off, gas on.

💡 MOA — like flicking a light switch

Atropine acts to increase the heart rate by blocking the action of the vagus nerve to block the PNS (parasympathetic nervous system) — REST & DIGEST — and turns ON the SNS (fight & flight) in the heart, like flicking a light switch.

🧠 Block the brake → the gas takes over. Vagus on vacation 🏖️ = heart rate climbs.
🐇

SPEED

STEP 3 · DID IT WORK?

Effectiveness lives on the strip AND in the patient — you need both.

📈 Key points — atropine is effective when we see normal sinus rhythm and reversal of the symptoms

They will show you normal sinus rhythm like this & no more hypoxic symptoms, like confusion, agitation, hypotension or syncope.

✅ Reversal of the symptoms — what disappears

No more hypoxic symptoms:

  • 😵‍💫 Confusion
  • 😤 Agitation
  • 📉 Hypotension
  • 😵 Syncope
🧠 When atropine works, the symptoms CASH out: Confusion · Agitation · Syncope · Hypotension — all gone.

⚡ Side note — if drugs do not work

If drugs do not work to fix the problem, then we have to put the patient on external pacing.

Correct sequence:

1
💊 Atropine — 1st: try the drug
2
⚡ External pacing — 2nd: when the drug fails
🧠 No pace from the pill? Pace with the pads. Drug first, pacer second.

📝 Common NCLEX Question NCLEX TIP

Atropine for a client with a heart rate of 38, bp of 88/65, reports confusion and dizziness. Which ECG strip would show medication effectiveness?

✅ CORRECT: the Normal Sinus rhythm strip — rate lands in 60–100.

🧮 Count it: R peak × 10 → 8 × 10 = 80 ✅

🧠 Don't pick the strip that just looks “nicer” — count the R peaks, × 10, and check it sits in 60–100.

SYMPTOMATIC — symptomatic bradycardia.
Q2: Name the 3 mental status changes of symptomatic bradycardia.
Agitation, Irritability, Confusion (A·I·C).
Q3: HR 38, BP 88/65, confusion & dizziness — which ECG strip shows the med worked?
Normal Sinus rhythm, 60–100 — R peak × 10: 8 × 10 = 80.

External pacing. Sequence: 1st Atropine, 2nd External pacing.
