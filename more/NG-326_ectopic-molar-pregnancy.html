🩸 Ectopic & Molar Pregnancy

Early pregnancy bleeding sorted by where the pregnancy is. Shoulder pain means rupture; very high hCG means a mole.

🧬 Ectopic pregnancy

A fertilised ovum implants outside the uterus — about 90% in a fallopian tube. The tube cannot stretch, so it eventually ruptures.

This is the leading cause of maternal death in the first trimester.

SignDetail
Classic triadMissed period + unilateral stabbing pain + spotting
Shoulder painRupture — blood in the abdomen irritates the diaphragm and refers to the shoulder
hCGRises more slowly than in a normal pregnancy
ShockTachycardia then hypotension; often more bleeding than is visible

Shoulder pain in early pregnancy is a ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise. It is a surgical emergency.

Risk factors: previous ectopic, PID or salpingitis, tubal surgery, endometriosis, IUD in place, smoking.

🩺 Care

  • Unruptured — may be treated with methotrexate. Teach: avoid folic acid supplements and alcohol, and no intercourse until hCG is undetectable
  • Ruptured — two large-bore IVs, type and crossmatch, oxygen, straight to theatre
  • RhoGAM if Rh negative
  • Acknowledge the loss — this is a pregnancy loss as well as an emergency

🍇 Hydatidiform mole

Abnormal placental tissue grows instead of a foetus — grape-like vesicles filling the uterus.

SignDetail
hCGExtremely high — far above expected for dates
UterusLarger than dates
BleedingDark brown “prune juice”, may pass grape-like vesicles
Foetal heartAbsent; no foetus on ultrasound
OtherSevere nausea and vomiting; preeclampsia before 20 weeks

Preeclampsia before 20 weeks is not normal preeclampsia — it points to a molar pregnancy. That detail is asked directly.

🩺 After evacuation

  • Serial hCG levels for a full year — they must fall to zero and stay there
  • Reliable contraception for that year

She must not become pregnant for a year. A rising hCG is the marker for choriocarcinoma - and a new pregnancy would raise hCG and hide it.

Molar pregnancy carries a real risk of choriocarcinoma, which is why the follow-up is so strict.

🎯 NCLEX traps

  • Shoulder pain = ruptured ectopic
  • Very high hCG + uterus large for dates + no foetal heart = mole
  • No pregnancy for one year after a mole, with monthly hCG
  • Preeclampsia before 20 weeks suggests a mole
  • RhoGAM after any early pregnancy loss if Rh negative
