🩸 Ectopic & Molar Pregnancy

Early pregnancy bleeding sorted by where the pregnancy is. Shoulder pain means rupture; very high hCG means a mole.

🧬 Ectopic pregnancy

A fertilized ovum implants outside the uterus — about 90% in a fallopian tube. The tube cannot stretch, so it eventually ruptures.

This is the leading cause of maternal death in the first trimester.

SignDetail
Classic triadMissed period + unilateral stabbing pain + spotting
Shoulder painRupture — blood in the abdomen irritates the diaphragm and refers to the shoulder
hCGRises more slowly than in a normal pregnancy
ShockTachycardia then hypotension; often more bleeding than is visible

Shoulder pain in early pregnancy is a ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise. It is a surgical emergency.

Risk factors: previous ectopic, PID or salpingitis, tubal surgery, endometriosis, IUD in place, smoking.

🩺 Care

  • Unruptured — may be treated with methotrexate. Teach: avoid folic acid supplements and alcohol, and no intercourse until hCG is undetectable
  • Ruptured — two large-bore IVs, type and crossmatch, oxygen, straight to theater
  • RhoGAM if Rh negative
  • Acknowledge the loss — this is a pregnancy loss as well as an emergency

🍇 Hydatidiform mole

Abnormal placental tissue grows instead of a fetus — grape-like vesicles filling the uterus.

SignDetail
hCGExtremely high — far above expected for dates
UterusLarger than dates
BleedingDark brown “prune juice”, may pass grape-like vesicles
Fetal heartAbsent; no fetus on ultrasound
OtherSevere nausea and vomiting; preeclampsia before 24 weeks

Preeclampsia before 24 weeks is not normal preeclampsia — it points to a molar pregnancy. That detail is asked directly.

🩺 After evacuation

  • A baseline pelvic exam and an ultrasound scan of the abdomen, straight after evacuation — that is ATI’s wording for the baseline.
  • Serum hCG weekly for 3 weeks, then monthly for 6 months, up to a year, to detect gestational trophoblastic disease
  • Reliable contraception as part of follow-up care
  • Rho(D) immune globulin after the procedure if she is Rh-negative
  • A hysterectomy may be considered if she does not want future fertility, or if the risk of GTN is high

ATI lists the baseline as a pelvic exam and abdominal ultrasound. Some question banks key a baseline chest X-ray instead — the lungs are where choriocarcinoma spreads first, so a film gives something to compare against later. If a stem offers it, take it; just know it is not the wording in your chapter. ATI lists no thyroid test at all, so a thyroid option is not an ATI answer.

She must not become pregnant for a year. A rising hCG is the marker for choriocarcinoma - and a new pregnancy would raise hCG and hide it.

Molar pregnancy carries a real risk of choriocarcinoma, which is why the follow-up is so strict.

🎯 NCLEX traps

  • Shoulder pain = ruptured ectopic
  • Very high hCG + uterus large for dates + no fetal heart = mole
  • No pregnancy for one year after a mole. The schedule is weekly for 3 weeks, then monthly — an option saying only “monthly” has dropped the weekly phase.
  • Preeclampsia before 24 weeks suggests a mole
  • RhoGAM after any early pregnancy loss if Rh negative
