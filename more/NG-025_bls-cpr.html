STOP

STEP 1 · WHY THE HEART STOPS

Cardiac arrest has many triggers — commonly described as the Hs & Ts.

🧨 Causes — “Hs & Ts”

Caused by a variety of factors:

  • 💨 Hypoxia — no oxygen on board
  • 🫁 Respiratory failure — the lungs quit first, the heart follows
  • ☠️ Toxins
  • 🩸 Blood clots
  • ⚡ Electrolyte imbalances — Na⁺ · Cl⁻ the heart's wiring salts
  • ➕ & others
🧠 AIR · POISON · PLUMBING · WIRING — no air (hypoxia, respiratory failure), poison (toxins), blocked plumbing (blood clots), bad wiring (electrolytes). All roads end in CARDIAC ARREST.

🚨 Why compressions can't wait — #1 Immediate CPR with high quality compressions

🙌 Chest compressions = you become the pump

START

STEP 2 · PUMP & BREATHE

The adult BLS sequence — the order IS the exam question.

🪜 Adult CPR — the priority ladder

1
🙌 Chest compressions IMMEDIATELY. If NO caregivers are around to help, start compressions BEFORE calling for help — you are the only caregiver! NCLEX TIP
“Most students get this wrong on exams.”
▼
2
🎯 Hands in center of chest — lower half of sternum
▼
3
💪 High quality compressions: rate 100–120/min, depth at least 2–2.4 in (5–6 cm) NCLEX TIP
▼
4
🫁 Breaths: manual = 30 compressions & 2 rescue breaths · intubated = every 6 sec without interruption
▼
5
🖐️ Every 2 min: pause compressions to assess pulse — 10 seconds
🧠 “PUSH FIRST, PHONE SECOND.” Alone with an arrest = hands on the chest before the phone. Then the Twin 2s: 2 inches deep · 2 breaths per 30 · pulse check every 2 min.

🎯 Where to push — center of chest, lower half of sternum

SHOCK

STEP 3 · AED & DEFIB

Pads on, everyone clear, shock — then hands straight back on the chest.

🔌 AED pads — where they go (8 years & older)

⚡ How to SHOCK an adult — in order

1
🔌 Defibrillator pads are placed
2
📣 Call out & look to make sure everyone is clear
3
🙌 Continue chest compressions immediately after the shock
🧠 “Pads · People clear · Push again.” P-P-P — the shock is a comma, not a period: hands go right back on the chest.

❌ Defibrillation is NOT cardioversion

❌ NO IV sedation needed.

❌ NO synchronized button.

That is for cardioversion — an arrested heart has no rhythm to sync to, and an unconscious client needs no sedating.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: You're the ONLY caregiver and your client goes into cardiac arrest. First action?
Start high-quality chest compressions immediately — BEFORE calling for help. (Most students get this wrong!)
Q2: Compression rate and depth for an adult?
100–120/min, at least 2–2.4 inches (5–6 cm) deep — hands on the lower half of the sternum.
Q3: The client is intubated. How do you deliver breaths?
Every 6 seconds, without interrupting compressions. (Manual = 30 compressions : 2 rescue breaths.)
Q4: For defibrillation, do you sedate the client and press the synchronized button?
NO and NO — no IV sedation, no sync button. The synchronized button is for cardioversion.
