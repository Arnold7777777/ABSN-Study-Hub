Pregnancy physiology · Seven key signals

Pregnancy hormones: who does what?

Remember one job first, then add the detail. Prolactin makes milk; oxytocin moves milk.

🧠 The milk distinction

Prolactin → production.

Oxytocin → ejection.

After placental delivery, estrogen and progesterone fall. This enables the transition to copious milk production; the two milk hormones have different jobs.

Read it in small chunks

🧪

01 hCG — pregnancy-test signal

Human chorionic gonadotropin supports the corpus luteum early.

This helps sustain progesterone while the placenta develops its hormone-producing role. Pregnancy tests detect hCG. In your nursing sign categories, a positive hCG test is a probable sign, not a definitive fetal finding.

🛡

02 Progesterone — maintains

Maintains the uterine lining and supports uterine relaxation.

The corpus luteum provides early progesterone; the placenta later becomes the major source.

🌱

03 Estrogen — grows

Supports uterine growth and breast duct development.

Placental estrogen production becomes important during pregnancy.

🔋

04 hPL — shifts fuel

Human placental lactogen helps increase maternal insulin resistance.

This supports fuel availability for the fetus. hPL is a placental hormone and also supports mammary development; it is one contributor to pregnancy’s metabolic changes.

↔

05 Relaxin — loosens

Helps relax pelvic ligaments and soften the cervix.

Sources include the corpus luteum and placenta. The diagram highlights supporting tissues; it does not depict the bones separating widely.

🥛

06 Prolactin — makes milk

Stimulates milk production in the breast.

Produced by the anterior pituitary. Levels rise during pregnancy; the drop in placental hormones after birth permits copious milk production.

💧

07 Oxytocin — contracts and moves milk

Stimulates uterine contractions and milk ejection (let-down).

Made in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. It helps move milk through ducts; it is not the hormone that makes the milk.

One short cue per hormone

hCG signals · Progesterone maintains · Estrogen grows · hPL shifts fuel · Relaxin loosens · Prolactin produces · Oxytocin contracts and ejects.

These are memory cues for major functions, not the only effects of each hormone.
