NG-321

🔴 Postpartum Hemorrhage

The leading cause of maternal death worldwide. Call for help early, feel the fundus, and work out which of the four Ts is causing it.

🧬 What counts, and why it happens

Blood loss over 500 mL after a vaginal birth, or 1000 mL after a cesarean — or any loss causing signs of hypovolemia.

The uterus stops bleeding by contracting. A uterus that will not contract cannot clamp the vessels where the placenta was.

⭐ The 4 Ts

CauseRecognize it byFix
Tone — atony (most common)Boggy, soft uterusMassage and uterotonics
Trauma — laceration, hematomaFIRM uterus but still bleeding — a bright red steady trickle; or severe perineal or rectal pain and swelling (hematoma)Find and repair; a hematoma may need evacuating

If the uterus is FIRM and she is still bleeding, it is not atony. Assess for trauma (laceration or hematoma), retained tissue and a clotting problem — a firm fundus does not prove a laceration. More massage will not fix any of them, and that is a favorite exam question.

Who is at risk

  • Overdistended uterus — twins, big baby, polyhydramnios
  • Prolonged or very rapid labor; oxytocin augmentation
  • Grand multiparity — the muscle is tired
  • Retained placental fragments; previous PPH
  • Chorioamnionitis; magnesium sulfate (it relaxes smooth muscle)

🩺 What to do — urgently, and all at once

This is not a ladder. Help is called at the start, and these happen together:

  • Call for help and notify the provider early — in parallel, not after everything else
  • Assess the fundus. Boggy: massage it, with the other hand supporting the lower uterine segment. Firm: do not keep massaging — look for another cause
  • Empty the bladder — void or catheter
  • IV access and fluids; uterotonics per order
  • Quantify the blood loss — weigh pads and drapes rather than estimating
  • Vital signs and shock signs — pulse, blood pressure, skin, level of consciousness, urine output

If the bleeding does not stop, prepare for theater and blood products.

⭐ The bladder clue

A uterus displaced to the RIGHT and sitting above the umbilicus means a full bladder. A distended bladder physically prevents the uterus from contracting down.

Boggy + deviated right = empty the bladder

For a boggy uterus, massage and emptying the bladder happen right away — while help is coming and the uterotonic is being drawn up, not instead of them.

👀 Assessment and lochia

Fundal assessment

  • Should be firm, midline, at or just below the umbilicus right after birth
  • Descends about 1 cm per day
  • If it is boggy, massage it — support the lower uterine segment with one hand while massaging with the other — otherwise you risk inverting the uterus

🚨 Signs she is decompensating

Tachycardia comes first. Blood pressure falls late. A young healthy postpartum woman can compensate for a large loss and then crash suddenly.

  • Rising pulse, then falling BP
  • Cool clammy skin, pallor
  • Restlessness and anxiety — cerebral hypoxia
  • Urine output below 30 mL/hr

Do not be reassured by a normal blood pressure in a bleeding postpartum patient. Watch the pulse.

🎯 NCLEX traps

  • Call for help early — at the same time as everything else
  • Boggy fundus → massage, lower segment supported. Massage is for a boggy uterus, not every bleed
  • Firm uterus + still bleeding = not atony — check for trauma, retained tissue and a clotting problem
  • Deviated right + boggy = full bladder
  • Methylergonovine + hypertension = no. Carboprost + asthma = no
  • Tachycardia is the early sign; hypotension is late
