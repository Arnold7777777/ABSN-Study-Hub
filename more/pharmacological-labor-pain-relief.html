Labor comfort · Compare the options

Pharmacological pain relief in labor

Match each method to what it does and what the nurse watches. Analgesia reduces pain; anesthesia blocks sensation.

🧠 Quick associations

  • Epidural/spinal: maternal blood pressure and fetal response.
  • Systemic opioid near birth: maternal and newborn breathing.
  • Nitrous: self-held mask; avoid the opioid/sedative combination.
  • Pudendal/local: perineal pain, not contraction pain.

Read it in small chunks

🩺

01 Epidural

A catheter provides ongoing regional pain relief.

Often combines local anesthetic and opioid. Watch maternal blood pressure and fetal heart rate, bladder function, and leg strength. Follow prescribed monitoring and assist mobility until function is assessed.

⚡

02 Spinal / CSE

Spinal relief is rapid and time-limited.

CSE means combined spinal–epidural: the spinal component starts relief, while an epidural catheter allows continued medication. Monitor blood pressure, breathing and block level; report a headache that changes with posture.

💊

03 Systemic opioids

IV or IM medicine reduces pain throughout the body.

These medicines can cross the placenta. Watch maternal sedation and respirations, and prepare to assess newborn breathing. Medication, dose, route and proximity to birth affect planning.

😷

04 Nitrous oxide

The client holds the inhalation mask.

Nitrous oxide mixed with oxygen helps coping, with rapid onset and offset. Watch for dizziness, nausea and sedation. Do not co-administer with systemic opioids or sedative/hypnotic medicines.

🎯

05 Pudendal / local anesthetic

Targets the perineal area for birth or repair.

A pudendal block targets the nerve supply; local infiltration numbs a small tissue area. These approaches do not relieve uterine contraction pain.

Choice + monitoring

Discuss preferences, allergies, contraindications, clinical status and available monitoring. The client can change their mind. This is a comparison aid—not a dosing guide, a complete contraindication list or a replacement for unit policy.

Sources and study notes
  • ACOG: Medications for Pain Relief During Labor and Delivery — Options, routes, systemic opioid and local/pudendal effects.
  • American Society of Anesthesiologists: Epidurals — Epidural versus spinal/CSE and relevant adverse effects.
  • ACOG: Nitrous oxide interaction advisory — Avoid combining inhaled nitrous with systemic opioids or sedative/hypnotic medicines.
