NSAIDs 🔥

Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs

NG-256 PHARMACOLOGY ADHD-friendly visual edition

NSAIDs block the COX enzyme to stop prostaglandins from being made — that kills pain, fever, and inflammation. The catch: prostaglandins also protect the stomach, kidneys, and platelets, so blocking them broadly causes the three big NSAID dangers — GI bleed, renal injury, and cardiovascular risk. See also NG-100 Anti-inflammatory Agents (overview) and NG-232 Acetaminophen, the drug NSAIDs get swapped for when these risks are too high.

🚫 Block COX-1 & COX-2↓ prostaglandins = ↓ pain, fever, inflammation — but also ↓ stomach/kidney protection.
🩸 #1 risk = GI bleedCOX-1 protects the stomach lining. Nonselective NSAIDs strip that protection.
💧 #2 risk = kidneysRenal blood flow depends on prostaglandins too — watch Cr & urine output.
🚨 Triple whammyNSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic = the classic nephrotoxic combo — the single highest-yield NSAID fact.
🎯

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · COX-1 vs COX-2

One enzyme, two jobs — protecting the body and causing the pain. NSAIDs can't fully tell the difference.

⚖️ COX-1 (the protector) vs COX-2 (the inflamer)

MECHANISM Nonselective NSAIDs block BOTH COX-1 and COX-2 — that's why they work fast but also cause GI/renal side effects.

🧠 "COX-1 keeps you safe, COX-2 makes you hurt." Nonselective NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, ketorolac, aspirin) block both = pain relief AND stomach/kidney risk. COX-2 selective agents were built to spare COX-1 — but trade GI safety for cardiovascular risk.

💊 Know the drug names

🎯 Indications

  • 🔥 Anti-inflammatory & antipyretic (fever reducer)
  • 🦴 Rheumatoid arthritis & osteoarthritis — relieves joint pain/swelling
  • 🦶 Gout — effective; a client stating "I can use ibuprofen or naproxen for gout pain" shows correct understanding
  • 🩹 Mild-to-moderate pain, dysmenorrhea, headache
🧠 "AIF" — Analgesic, Inflammation-fighter, Fever-reducer. Three jobs, one drug class.
🚨

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · THE 3 BIG DANGERS

GI, renal, cardiovascular — and the interaction that combines two of them into one kidney emergency.

🚨 The "Triple Whammy" — NSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB + Diuretic

The single most-tested NSAID interaction. Each drug reduces kidney blood flow/pressure by a different route — stack all three and the kidney can lose perfusion fast.

🧠 "Triple Whammy" = NSAID + ACE-I/ARB + Diuretic. Teach clients: "I will call my HCP before taking ibuprofen" if they're on a blood pressure pill and a water pill — that statement is a CORRECT understanding on an exam.

🩸 GI bleed risk

Loss of COX-1's protective mucus/bicarbonate layer → erosion, ulceration, bleeding.

  • ✅ Take NSAIDs with food — do NOT take on an empty stomach
  • ⚠️ Higher risk: elderly, prior PUD, combined with aspirin, corticosteroids, or anticoagulants
  • 👀 Watch for: coffee-ground emesis, melena (black tarry stool), ↓ Hgb/Hct
🧠 A client on ranitidine (an H2 blocker acid reducer) plus an NSAID for their acid reflux is being managed correctly — the acid reducer protects the stomach while the NSAID is used.

💧 Renal risk

Prostaglandins keep the afferent arteriole dilated — block them and kidney perfusion drops, especially with long-term use or in an already-volume-depleted client.

  • 🧪 Rising creatinine above the normal range
  • 🚽 Oliguria — urine output trending toward < 30 mL/hr, a classic oliguria threshold
  • 📞 Client teaching gold standard: "I will call my HCP before taking ibuprofen" if they have kidney disease, heart failure, or are on ACE-I/ARB + diuretic
🧠 NSAIDs are "NOT good for the body" in one line: kidneys, heart failure, asthma, GI, and clotting — five systems, one drug class.

❤️ Cardiovascular risk — worse with COX-2 selective agents

  • 📈 Worsening HTN and CHF — NSAIDs cause fluid retention and blunt the effect of antihypertensives
  • 🫀 COX-2 selective NSAIDs (e.g. celecoxib) carry the highest cardiovascular risk — increased MI/stroke risk
  • 🩸 Thrombosis risk — "sticky blood," increased clot risk with COX-2 selective agents specifically (unlike aspirin, which is antiplatelet)
  • ⚠️ Use caution or avoid in clients with known CHF or HTN
🧠 "Swelling heart, HTN worsening" — remember NSAIDs both raise blood pressure AND make the heart work against more fluid. Doubly bad for a CHF client.

⚠️ More NSAID-specific traps

TrapWhat to know

🧠 "A-A-A" — Avoid kids' Aspirin = Avoid Reye's. And if the ears start ringing, the aspirin dose is too high.

STEP 3 · SAFE USE

Correct client statements the NCLEX rewards — and the ones that should trigger more teaching.

✅ Client statements that show correct understanding

1
🍽️ "I don't take ibuprofen on an empty stomach." — take with food
2
📞 "I will call my HCP before taking ibuprofen" — if on ACE-I/ARB + diuretic, or has kidney/heart disease
3
🦴 "I can use ibuprofen or naproxen for gout pain." — NSAIDs are appropriate for gout flares
4
🩹 "I take occasional ibuprofen for my knees." — appropriate PRN use for mild joint pain
🧠 These four statements are the ones an NCLEX question rewards as "the teaching was effective" — memorize the pattern, not just the words.

🧠 Every item on this list either strips the stomach's protection or stacks a bleed/kidney risk.

🧪 Labs/values to track

  • 🧪 Creatinine — rising above baseline = renal injury
  • 🚽 Urine output — trending down toward oliguria
  • 🩸 Hgb/Hct — dropping = possible GI bleed
  • 👂 Tinnitus — early clinical sign of aspirin/salicylate toxicity, report immediately
🧠 "Cr up, urine down, Hgb down, ears ringing" — four red flags, four different organ systems, one drug class.

🔀 When NSAIDs get swapped for acetaminophen

Acetaminophen has minimal effect on platelets and no significant GI erosion risk, so it is preferred over NSAIDs in:

  • 🩸 Clients with hemophilia or other bleeding disorders
  • 🫙 Clients with peptic ulcer disease
  • 🧒 Children with viral illness (flu) — avoids Reye's risk from aspirin

Tylenol and NSAIDs can be used interchangeably to reduce fever — when one reaches its max daily dose, the other can be alternated in. See NG-232 Acetaminophen for the liver-toxicity side of that trade-off.

🧠 "Bleeder or ulcer? Reach for Tylenol, not the NSAID."

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Why do NSAIDs cause both pain relief and stomach/kidney damage?
Nonselective NSAIDs block both COX-1 (protective — stomach lining, renal blood flow, platelets) and COX-2 (inflammatory) — the same mechanism causes the benefit and the harm.
Q2: What three drug classes together create the "triple whammy" nephrotoxic combo?
NSAID + ACE inhibitor (or ARB) + diuretic — each reduces renal perfusion by a different mechanism.
Q3: A child has the flu. Which OTC pain/fever reducer should be avoided, and why?
Aspirin — risk of Reye's syndrome in children with a viral illness. Use acetaminophen instead.
Q4: What early symptom signals aspirin/salicylate toxicity and should be reported to the HCP?
Tinnitus (ringing in the ears).
