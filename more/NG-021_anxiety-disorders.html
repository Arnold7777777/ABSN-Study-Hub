🧠 "Generally Sad, Panicky & Separated — Phobic, Obsessed, Traumatized." One sad sentence = GAD · SAD · Panic · Separation · Phobias · OCD · PTSD.

⭐ Exam favorite: Separation anxiety disorder

📝 HESI Question — Client states… "every time I need to leave the house for work … Mom becomes extremely anxious and cries that something terrible is going to happen to me." … supports which psychiatric diagnosis?

✅ Separation anxiety disorder

🎯 How to spot it fast

The anxiety is glued to the goodbye — distress fires when the person leaves, not at the workplace or the crowd.

🧠 "Terrible things happen when you LEAVE" = separation anxiety. The trigger is the door, not the destination.
⚡

SNS SWITCH

STEP 2 · FEEL IT

Pathophysiology — panic in the mind flips the fight-or-flight switch in the body.

🏃 Fight or flight — what the SNS does to the body

During severe anxiety the mind goes into a state of panic & so the body goes into fight or flight mode, turning on the SNS — sympathetic nervous system.

🧠 MEMORY TRICK — "SNS — Sssspeeds Up the Vital Signs!" Increasing the HR, BP, RR, sugar levels, & dilating the pupils!

🧪 ATI Question — physiological signs of anxiety

Which of the following are physiological signs of anxiety? Select all that apply — all five!

  • ✅ Increased pulse rate 📈
  • ✅ Hyperglycemia 🍬
  • ✅ Dilated pupils 👁️
  • ✅ Dilated bronchioles 🫁
  • ✅ Peripheral vasoconstriction 🥶

🩸 The shunt story

The SNS tells the body to shunt blood flow away from the extremities & toward the core of the body for the vital organs — and to increase the vital signs.

🧠 "Core gets the blood, hands get the cold." Everything that helps you run goes UP — everything at the edges gets clamped DOWN.
🥤

LEVELS

STEP 3 · GRADE IT

Grade anxiety like a drink order — the exam question is almost always "which level is this?"

🥤 Small → EXTRA Large

🧠 Order it like a soda: Small = Mild · Medium = Moderate · Large = Severe · EXTRA Large = Panic Attacks! Same first letters — Small-Mild is the only mismatch to memorize.

📊 Mild vs Moderate vs Severe — what you'll see

Level👀 What you see🧠 Perception
😤 Mild Restless, irritability intact
😟 Moderate Increased RR & HR · pacing back & forth Slightly reduced perception
😱 Severe Increased RR & HR "hyperventilation" · pacing back & forth · feeling of "Impending Doom" Perception is GREATLY reduced: can NOT respond to directions
🧠 The 3 D's of SEVERE: Doom, Death-talk, Deaf to directions. If perception is gone and they can't follow you — it's severe.

🚨 Exam alert — every one of these clients is SEVERE

  • 📝 ATI: "The client is pacing the hallway and tells the nurse he has a feeling of impending doom." → Severe
  • 📝 HESI Q1: patient tells the nurse, "I feel like I am going to die." → Severe
  • 📝 HESI Q2: male patient is running & keeps repeating, "They are coming!" — neither follows staff directions nor responds to verbal efforts to calm him → Severe

Can't follow directions = severe — every time.
