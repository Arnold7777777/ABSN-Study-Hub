🧱 The skin stack — 3 layers

  • 1️⃣ Epidermis — the top coat
  • 2️⃣ Dermis — the working middle
  • 3️⃣ Subcutaneous tissue — “that fatty bubble looking tissue”

Under the skin we find fascia, muscle & bone.

⭐ Why the layers matter

Depth describes the tissue injured. Partial-thickness burns involve some of the dermis; full-thickness burns destroy the entire dermis, and deeper structures may be injured too. The layers are an anatomy order, not one layer per degree.

🧠 “Every Day Someone Finds My Burns” = Epidermis · Dermis · Subcutaneous · Fascia · Muscle · Bone — top to bottom.

NUR 258 · MODULE 4 · BURN ASSESSMENT

Burn depth: read the layers, then the clues

Depth describes injured tissue. Appearance, blanching and sensation help you assess it together; a single picture or pain score cannot establish depth.

Go to the readable comparison ↓

PANEL 1 · FIRST DEGREE

Superficial

Depth: epidermis only.

Typical clues: dry surface, redness, blanching and pain. Blisters suggest a deeper injury.

PANEL 2 · SECOND DEGREE

Superficial partial-thickness

Depth: epidermis and upper dermis.

Typical clues: moist surface, blisters, brisk blanching and marked tenderness.

PANEL 3 · ALSO SECOND DEGREE

Deep partial-thickness

Depth: farther into the dermis, but not through its entire thickness.

Typical clues: paler or mottled areas, a drier surface, less blanching and reduced sensation.

PANEL 4 · THIRD DEGREE

Full-thickness

Depth: epidermis and the entire dermis.

Typical clues: dry, firm or leathery tissue; white, brown or black areas; markedly reduced sensation in the center. Nearby burns can still hurt.

Memory hook: “1 is red, 2 is wet, 3 is dry & dead” — and if it blanches, blood still branches; full-thickness won’t blanch. Deep partial-thickness sits between 2 and 3: paler, drier, less painful.

What about fourth degree? Injury extends beyond skin into deeper structures, such as muscle or bone. It does not mean every case reaches bone. Full-thickness skin injury does not, by itself, mean all underlying fat is destroyed.

Three things to remember
  • Less pain can mean deeper injury. Never use pain alone to label a burn minor.
  • TBSA for burn resuscitation: count partial- and full-thickness burns; exclude superficial burns.
  • Reassess: mixed depths are common, and depth can evolve. Suspected deep burns need specialist consultation; full-thickness injury warrants immediate consultation with consideration of transfer.
Quick self-check

Are panels 2 and 3 different degrees? No. Both are second-degree burns; the amount of dermis injured differs.

Does a white, less-painful center prove a mild burn? No. Those findings may indicate deeper injury and require assessment.

CARE

STEP 3 · PREHOSPITAL · MINOR BURNS

Three C's, in order — and two traps the exam loves.

✅ Prehospital care — the 3 C's

C
🚿 Cool water — cool the burn with cool running water.
❌ NO ice, butter, creams or ointment on the fresh burn
Side note: NHS and RCH guidance say 20 minutes of running water, started within 3 hours, keeping the rest of the person warm.
C
🩹 Cover the area — “Clean dry cloth”
C
💍 Clothing & jewelry removal — only if NOT adhered to the skin
🧠 Cool it, Cover it, Clear it off — 3 C's, in that order. Cool the burn, never the whole patient.

⛔ Never-do list

  • ❌ NO ice on the burn
  • ❌ NO creams
  • ❌ NO antibiotic ointment on the fresh burn 💊🚫 (later topical antimicrobials follow the wound-care plan)
  • ❌ Don't pull off anything stuck to the burn

Cool water only — soothe it, don't smother it.

⭐ Stuck to the skin? Step away NCLEX TIP

HCP removes anything sticking to the skin.

You remove clothing & jewelry that is not adhered — if it's melted in or stuck on, it stays until the HCP takes it off.

🧠 “If it's stuck, it stays — the HCP peels, not you.”

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A burn is “red, moist, shiny” with fluid-filled vesicles and painful blisters. What degree?
Second-degree: superficial partial-thickness.
Q2: First C of prehospital burn care — and what do you NEVER apply?
Cool the burn with cool running water — NO ice, butter, creams or ointment on the fresh burn. Later dressings and topical medicines follow the wound-care plan.
Q3: The client's shirt is stuck to the burn. Do you remove it?
NO — only remove clothing/jewelry that is not adhered. The HCP removes anything sticking to the skin.
Q4: Dry, waxy white, leathery or charred black, and it does NOT blanch. What degree?
Third-degree (full-thickness) — non-blanchable. Less pain does not make it minor.
