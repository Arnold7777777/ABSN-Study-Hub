STEP 2 · READ THE DEPTH

Look at the burn, name the floor it reached — dry & red, wet & blistered, or waxy & dead.

🕳️ Burn depth map — how far down did it go?

🌡️ Degree vs degree — the look test

DegreeThicknessWhat you see 👀
1st° ☀️SuperficialDry with blanchable redness
2nd° 💧Partial thickness“Red, moist, shiny fluid filled vesicles” · painful blisters
3rd° 🤍Full-thicknessDry waxy white, leathery, or charred black · non-blanchable
4th° 🦴Full-thicknessDeepest — down where fascia, muscle & bone live
🧠 “1 is red, 2 is wet, 3 is dry & dead.” And the blanch test: if it blanches, blood still branches — 3rd° won't blanch.

CARE

STEP 3 · PREHOSPITAL · MINOR BURNS

Three C's, in order — and two traps the exam loves.

✅ Prehospital care — the 3 C's

C
🚿 Cool water — briefly soak the area.
❌ NO ice, creams, or antibiotic ointment to open skin
C
🩹 Cover the area — “Clean dry cloth”
C
💍 Clothing & jewelry removal — only if NOT adhered to the skin
🧠 Cool it, Cover it, Clear it off — 3 C's, in that order. Cool the burn, never the whole patient.

⛔ Never-do list

  • ❌ NO ice on the burn
  • ❌ NO creams
  • ❌ NO antibiotic ointment to open skin 💊🚫
  • ❌ Don't pull off anything stuck to the burn

Cool water only — soothe it, don't smother it.

⭐ Stuck to the skin? Step away NCLEX TIP

HCP removes anything sticking to the skin.

You remove clothing & jewelry that is not adhered — if it's melted in or stuck on, it stays until the HCP takes it off.

🧠 “If it's stuck, it stays — the HCP peels, not you.”

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A burn is “red, moist, shiny” with fluid-filled vesicles and painful blisters. What degree?
Second-degree (partial thickness).
Q2: First C of prehospital burn care — and what do you NEVER apply?
Cool water, briefly soak the area — NO ice, creams, or antibiotic ointment to open skin.
Q3: The client's shirt is stuck to the burn. Do you remove it?
NO — only remove clothing/jewelry that is not adhered. The HCP removes anything sticking to the skin.
Q4: Dry, waxy white, leathery or charred black, and it does NOT blanch. What degree?
Third-degree (full-thickness) — non-blanchable.
