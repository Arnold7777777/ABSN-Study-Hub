🧠 The fundus tells you most

Immediately after birth it should be firm, midline, at or just below the umbilicus.

  • Boggy → massage → empty bladder → reassess
  • Deviated to the right and high → full bladder
  • Not descending → think retained fragments or infection

Always support the lower uterine segment with one hand while massaging with the other, or you risk inverting the uterus.

Homans sign is no longer recommended as a DVT test - it can dislodge a clot. Assess for unilateral pain, warmth, swelling and redness instead.

Severe perineal pain with a firm uterus is a haematoma, not normal soreness. That distinction is a favourite question.

💐 The three mood disorders

 Baby bluesPostpartum depressionPostpartum psychosis
OnsetDays 3–5Weeks to monthsUsually first 2 weeks
DurationUp to 2 weeksMonths if untreatedEmergency
PictureTearful, mood swings, still functioningHopeless, guilty, cannot function, may not bondHallucinations, delusions, thoughts of harming self or baby
TreatmentSupport, reassurance, restTherapy, medication, screeningPsychiatric emergency, hospitalisation

Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency. The infant must not be left alone with her, and she needs immediate psychiatric care.

Ask directly about thoughts of harming herself or the baby. Asking does not plant the idea, and it is the only way to find out.

Baby blues is normal and self-limiting. The distinguishing question is always duration and function: still coping and improving by two weeks = blues; worsening or unable to function = depression.

🎯 NCLEX traps

  • Fever after 24 hours = infection. In the first 24 h, suspect dehydration
  • Severe perineal pain + firm uterus = haematoma
  • Do not use Homans sign
  • Blues resolve by 2 weeks; beyond that, screen for depression
  • Psychosis = emergency, never leave her alone with the baby
