Scoliosis

Uneven shoulders/hips and rib prominence on forward bend.

⚖️ Treatment by curve size, and how adults differ (NG-422)

← Back to Module 10Open the picture full sizeRead the study notes

Study notes

🚨 Priority

New neurologic symptoms need prompt assessment.

⚙ Pathophysiology

  • The spine curves sideways with rotation, often developing during growth.

🩺 Nursing actions

  • Assess posture, function and prescribed imaging.
  • Support brace use, skin care and body image.

💊 Treatment

  • Observation, bracing or surgery depends on curve size and remaining growth.

🏠 Patient & family education

  • Follow brace and follow-up schedules.
  • Report new weakness, bowel/bladder changes or severe pain.

🏥 After spinal fusion (rods)

Keep the spine straight and watch the legs, the head, the lungs and the belly.

  • 🔄 Log roll to turn her: shoulders and hips move together, a pillow between the knees. She does not twist or turn herself the first days, and nobody lifts her by the shoulders and hips.
  • 🦵 Neuro checks of both legs: movement, feeling, color, warmth, pulses. New numbness, tingling or weakness → report now (spinal cord injury).
  • 🧠 Headache that is worse sitting up, or clear fluid on the dressing → report: possible CSF leak. Never chart it as routine post-op pain.
  • 🫁 Lungs: incentive spirometer, cough and deep breathe. Watch for atelectasis and pneumothorax.
  • 🍽 Belly: nothing by mouth until bowel sounds return (ileus). Repeated vomiting once eating starts → think superior mesenteric artery syndrome.
  • 🩸 Blood loss: falling blood pressure, rising heart rate, hemoglobin, urine output.
  • 💊 Pain: expected and big; scheduled or PCA analgesia so she can turn and breathe deeply.
