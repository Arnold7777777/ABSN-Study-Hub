ABO compatibility for blood products

Start with the patient’s blood type. Read across to the product being transfused. These are standard ABO-compatible donor groups; the blood bank selects the unit.

Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) vs. packed red blood cells (pRBCs)

RecipientMay receive plasma / FFP fromMay receive red cells from
OO, A, B, ABO
AA, ABA, O
BB, ABB, O
ABABAB, A, B, O

Why the rule flips: With red cells, think about donor antigens. With plasma, think about donor antibodies. AB plasma lacks anti-A and anti-B, so it is ABO-compatible with every group. Group O red cells lack A and B antigens. Example: a type A patient can receive type A or AB plasma, but type A or O red cells.

Use ABO-identical components when available. RhD matters for red cells; it does not usually determine plasma selection. Emergency release of low-titer group A plasma, platelet substitutions, and cryoprecipitate selection follow transfusion-service policy.
