4 Rules for Antidepressants 📜

SSRI · SNRI · TCA · MAOI · atypicals — four rules that apply to all of them

NG-010 MENTAL HEALTH · Pharmacology ADHD-friendly visual edition

There are five antidepressant classes and dozens of drug names — but four rules cover almost every antidepressant question you will ever see. Learn these four and you can answer a question about a drug you have never heard of. This is the umbrella page: each class has its own deep-dive page linked at the bottom.

1️⃣ Suicide risk goes UP firstEspecially the first few weeks. Notify the provider of any suicidal thought.
2️⃣ Slow on, slow off4–6 weeks for full effect · NEVER stop abruptly — taper.
3️⃣ NEVER mixSSRI + St. John's wort · MAOI + any antidepressant → serotonin syndrome.
4️⃣ Weight + BP changeALL psych drugs shift weight · they decrease BP → slow position changes.

THE 4 RULES

STEP 1 · LEARN THESE, ANSWER ANYTHING

Start with the synapse — every rule below is a consequence of what happens in this one gap.

🔬 The synaptic cleft — what every antidepressant is doing down there

EXAM TIP Nearly all antidepressants raise the amount of neurotransmitter sitting in the gap between two neurons. SSRIs and SNRIs block the reuptake pump; TCAs block the same pumps plus several other receptors; MAOIs destroy the enzyme that breaks the transmitter down. Different doors, same result: more transmitter in the cleft. And that is also why combining two of them is so dangerous.

One mechanism, three outcomes: too little transmitter (depression), enough (treatment), far too much (serotonin syndrome).

🧠 "Fill the gap — don't flood it." Every antidepressant fills the synaptic gap. Two at once floods it. That single image gives you Rules 1, 2 and 3.

1️⃣ Increased risk of suicide — the first few weeks are the danger window

Antidepressants can increase suicidal thoughts in the first few weeks of treatment. The classic explanation: energy and motivation return before the mood does — so a client who was too depressed to act now has enough drive to act, while still feeling hopeless.

  • 📞 Notify the provider of ANY suicidal thought — this is not a "monitor and re-assess" item.
  • ❓ Clarify any new prescription — check it against everything else the client takes before the first dose.
  • 👀 Ask directly. "Are you having thoughts of hurting or killing yourself?" Asking does not plant the idea.
  • 📅 Short prescription quantities and frequent follow-up in the early weeks are deliberate safety measures.
🧠 "Energy comes back before hope does." That one sentence explains the entire black box warning — and it is the reason the answer is almost never "reassure the client."

2️⃣ Slow onset & slow taper — NEVER stop abruptly

  • ⏳ Full therapeutic effect takes about 4–6 weeks — some improvement earlier, but do not judge the drug a failure at 2 weeks.
  • 🗣️ Teach: "Keep taking it even when you feel better" — feeling better is the drug working, not a reason to stop.
  • 📉 Taper on a schedule set by the provider. The client should never run out and never self-discontinue.
  • ⚠️ Discontinuation symptoms are not addiction — antidepressants are not addictive; the brain simply needs time to re-adapt.
🧠 "Slow on, slow off." Four words, two rules. Weeks to start working, weeks to come off.
⚠️

RULE 3: NEVER MIX

STEP 2 · SEROTONIN SYNDROME

This is the single most tested antidepressant fact in the entire curriculum.

🚨 The forbidden combinations

  • ❌ SSRI + St. John's wort — the herb is serotonergic. Ask about supplements every time.
  • ❌ MAOI + ANY antidepressant — TCA, SSRI, SNRI. This is the classic lethal pairing.
  • ❌ MAOI or SSRI + other serotonergic drugs: triptans, tramadol, linezolid, buspirone, lithium, dextromethorphan.
  • ⏳ Switching between an SSRI and an MAOI requires a washout period — typically about 2 weeks in either direction, and longer (about 5 weeks) after fluoxetine because of its long half-life. Stopping one drug "the day before" starting an MAOI is never safe.
🧠 "A HOT, SHAKY, SWEATY, JUMPY patient." Agitation · Hyperthermia · Clonus/hyperreflexia · Diaphoresis · Tremor. This exact five-item list appears on the SSRI, SNRI/TCA and MAOI pages — memorize it once.

4️⃣ ALL psych drugs cause weight changes — and antidepressants decrease BP

🧠 "Every psych drug moves the scale and drops the pressure." Two vitals to check at every visit: weight and orthostatic BP.

TEACH & TEST

STEP 3 · THE MOST-MISSED QUESTIONS

Three question types from the source — work through the reasoning, not the memorizing.

🎯 Missed question #1 — "Which medications have the most potential risk for injury?" (SATA)

READ THE STEM "Risk for injury" in a psych stem almost always means which drugs are SEDATING → falls.

OptionPick it?Why
1. Amitriptyline✅ YESTCA — strongly sedating + anticholinergic + orthostatic hypotension. Triple fall risk.
2. Diphenhydramine✅ YESSedating antihistamine, anticholinergic — a classic fall/confusion drug in older adults.
3. Colace (docusate)❌ noA stool softener. No sedation, no CNS effect.
4. Alprazolam✅ YESBenzodiazepine — CNS depressant, sedation, impaired balance.
5. Buspirone❌ noNon-sedating anxiolytic — that is its whole selling point vs a benzo.
🧠 "Sedation = the floor." Scan the list for anything that makes a person drowsy or dizzy. Stool softeners and buspirone don't.

🎯 Missed question #2 — "Which drug combinations should the nurse question?" (SATA)

CombinationQuestion it?Why
Sertraline + selegiline✅ YESSSRI + MAOI — the forbidden pairing. Serotonin syndrome.
Alprazolam + citalopram❌ noA benzo with an SSRI is commonly prescribed together; monitor for additive sedation, but it is not a "question the order" combination.
Buspirone + phenelzine✅ YESBuspirone is serotonergic; phenelzine is an MAOI. Serotonin syndrome + hypertensive risk.
Lithium + ketorolac✅ YESNSAIDs reduce lithium excretion → rising level → lithium toxicity.
St. John's wort + buspirone✅ YESTwo serotonergic agents stacked → serotonin syndrome.

PATTERN Count the serotonin sources in each option. Two or more serotonergic agents = question it. The one exception on this list — a benzodiazepine — does not act on serotonin at all.

🧠 "MAOI is the poison-ivy of pharmacology — nothing touches it." Phenelzine, selegiline, isocarboxazid, tranylcypromine. If one of those names appears next to any other psych drug, question it.

🎯 Missed question #3 — "Which of these should concern the nurse?"

Statement / situationConcern?Reasoning
1. Amitriptyline prescribed to treat fibromyalgia pain❌ noA recognized off-label use of TCAs for chronic pain — not a red flag by itself.
2. Headache while on phenelzine🚨 YESAn MAOI + a new severe headache is the classic first sign of a hypertensive crisis from tyramine. Check the BP now.
3. Taking St. John's wort with sertraline🚨 YESTwo serotonergic agents → serotonin syndrome. Rule 3.
4. Discontinuing escitalopram the day before starting isocarboxazid🚨 YESAn SSRI → MAOI switch needs a washout (commonly about 2 weeks). One day is nowhere near enough.
5. Peanut butter & jelly sandwich while on selegiline❌ noPeanut butter and jelly are not high-tyramine foods. The MAOI danger list is aged cheese, cured/smoked meats, fermented foods, tap/draft beer, soy sauce, overripe fruit.
6. Sore throat, fatigue & low-grade fever on clozapine🚨 YESInfection signs on clozapine = possible agranulocytosis. Hold, get a CBC with differential, notify the provider immediately.
🧠 Two "safe" distractors hide in every one of these lists — usually a legitimate off-label use and a harmless food. Don't over-flag; read for the actual mechanism.

✅ The teaching script — what you actually say

1
"It takes 4 to 6 weeks to feel the full benefit. Don't judge it in the first two weeks."
2
"If you have any thought of hurting yourself, call us the same day — do not wait for your appointment."
3
"Never stop it suddenly, even if you feel great. We will taper it together."
4
"Tell every provider and pharmacist you take this — including about herbals like St. John's wort."
5
"Stand up slowly — sit on the edge of the bed for a minute first. And expect some change in weight; we'll track it."
6
"No alcohol — it worsens depression and adds sedation."
🧠 "WAIT · WARN · WEAN · WATCH." Wait 4–6 weeks · Warn about suicidality · Wean, never stop · Watch for interactions, weight and BP.

🎯 Cover & check — 6 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Name the 4 rules.
1) Increased risk of suicide, especially in the first few weeks. 2) Slow onset and slow taper — never stop abruptly. 3) Never mix — SSRI + St. John's wort, or MAOI + any antidepressant. 4) All psych drugs cause weight changes and decrease BP (slow position changes).
Q2: List the 5 signs of serotonin syndrome.
Agitation, hyperthermia, clonus/hyperreflexia, diaphoresis, tremor. Often also hypertension, tachycardia and muscle rigidity.
Q3: A client says "I've taken it 12 days and I feel no different." Response?
First assess for suicidal thoughts, worsening depression or unusual behavior — this is the high-risk window. Then teach that full effect takes about 4–6 weeks and that stopping now is not safe.
Q4: Why is stopping an SSRI "the day before" starting an MAOI unsafe?
The SSRI is still in the body. A washout period is required — commonly about 2 weeks, and longer (about 5 weeks) after fluoxetine — or the overlap can cause serotonin syndrome.
Q5: Client on phenelzine reports a new severe headache. Priority?
Check the blood pressure immediately — headache on an MAOI is the classic warning of hypertensive crisis from tyramine. Notify the provider.
Q6: Sore throat, fatigue and low-grade fever in a client on clozapine — what are you worried about?
Agranulocytosis. Hold the dose, obtain a CBC with differential, and notify the provider immediately.
