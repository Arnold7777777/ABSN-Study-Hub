Aminosalicylates 🎯

5-ASA drugs — mesalamine · balsalazide · olsalazine · sulfasalazine

NG-016 GI · Pharmacology ADHD-friendly visual edition

Aminosalicylates are the first-line anti-inflammatory class for inflammatory bowel disease. What makes them different from every other IBD drug: they exert a TOPICAL anti-inflammatory effect in the bowel — the drug has to physically land on the inflamed lining to work. The exact mechanism of action is unknown. Because the effect is topical, the entire pharmacology of this class is really a delivery problem: how do we get the drug to the diseased segment and no further?

🎯 TOPICAL, not systemicIt works where it touches. That's why the formulation (delayed-release, enema, suppository) matters as much as the drug.
🩹 Treats UC & Crohn'sActive disease and maintenance of remission — proctitis and proctosigmoiditis too.
🚫 Sulfa & sulfite allergyContraindicated. Also: intestinal obstruction and children under 2 years.
📣 Report 3 thingsAbdominal distention · fever · abdominal pain — the deterioration triad.

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · TOPICAL ANTI-INFLAMMATORY

Look at the bowel wall first. Once you see which layer is inflamed, the whole class makes sense.

🔬 Bowel wall in cutaway — inflamed vs. healed on aminosalicylate

EXAM TIP In ulcerative colitis the inflammation is confined to the mucosa and submucosa and it is continuous from the rectum upward. That superficial, continuous pattern is exactly why a drug that sits on the surface can work — a topical anti-inflammatory can reach mucosal disease, but it cannot reach through a full-thickness inflammatory tract.

Gold dots = neutrophils packing the crypts (crypt abscesses), the histologic hallmark of active disease. Green = the 5-ASA drug sitting on the mucosal surface, exactly where it must be.

🧠 "It's a paint, not a pill." Think of 5-ASA as anti-inflammatory paint for the bowel lining. Paint only works on the surface you can reach — which is why an enema exists for the left colon and a suppository for the rectum.

🗺️ Delivery is the whole game — where each formulation releases

🧠 "Low disease, low route." Rectum only → suppository. Rectum + sigmoid → enema. Whole colon or small bowel → oral, in the coating that opens at the right pH/time.

🧠 "Balsalazide is the sprinter (8 weeks), olsalazine is the marathoner (maintenance)." Mesalamine is the all-rounder with the most formulations. Sulfasalazine is the one that also does joints.

🆚 Topical action vs systemic immunosuppression

This is the differentiator the exam wants. A 5-ASA works on the lining. A corticosteroid or biologic works through the bloodstream, dampening immunity everywhere.

🎯 Aminosalicylate (this page)💉 Systemic IBD drugs
Acts topically in the bowel — must contact the mucosa Act body-wide through the circulation
Adverse effects are mostly GI + headache/dizziness Adverse effects are infection, glucose, bone, growth
Used for mild–moderate disease and to hold remission Used to rescue a severe flare or when 5-ASA fails
No routine "monitor for infection" teaching Infection precautions are central
🧠 "Surface first, systemic second." IBD therapy climbs a ladder — the topical rung is always tried before you accept whole-body immunosuppression.

EXAM TIP Abdominal pain, nausea and diarrhea are also the symptoms of the disease. That is why the source insists you question the patient about the type and intensity of symptoms BEFORE the first dose — without a baseline you cannot tell a drug reaction from a flare.

🧠 "You can't judge the change if you never measured the start." Baseline symptom assessment is the whole reason this is a nursing-management question, not a pharmacology one.

🚫 Contraindications

  • ❌ Known hypersensitivity
  • ❌ Hypersensitivity to sulfonamides and sulfites — ask about a sulfa allergy before every dose, especially with sulfasalazine
  • ❌ Intestinal obstruction — a drug that must travel to the colon cannot be given into a blocked bowel
  • ❌ Children younger than 2 years

Caution: aminosalicylates are pregnancy category B — except olsalazine, which is category C — and all are used with caution during pregnancy and lactation (safety has not been established).

🧠 "SOB-2" — Sulfa/sulfite allergy · Obstruction · Below age 2. Three checks before you hand over the tablet.

⭐ NCLEX TIP — the obstipation trap: loose stool that is actually constipation

A client with hypoactive bowel sounds and severe obstipation can leak liquid stool around a hard fecal mass — and it presents as loose stool. If you read that as diarrhea and treat it as a flare, you are treating the exact opposite of the real problem. That patient needs completely different drug therapy.

🧠 "Quiet gut, liquid stool = a plug, not a flare." Listen before you believe the stool. Hypoactive bowel sounds flip the entire care plan.

STEP 3 · NURSING MANAGEMENT

This is a nursing-process class: baseline, assess, report, replace fluids.

✅ Nursing management, in order

1
Review the chart for the course of treatment and find the reason the drug was prescribed — active flare or maintenance changes everything you teach.
▼
2
Question the patient about the type and intensity of symptoms — pain, discomfort, diarrhea or constipation — to create the baseline for evaluating effectiveness.
▼
3
Assess for relief of symptoms and monitor vital signs.
▼
4
Monitor fluid intake & output — chronic diarrhea is a fluid and electrolyte problem before it is anything else.
▼
5
🚨 REPORT: abdominal distention · fever · abdominal pain.
🧠 "Baseline → Relief → Report." Three verbs. Every aminosalicylate nursing question is one of those three.

🚨 Why "abdominal distention" is on the report list

Worsening distention with fever and pain in a colitis client can signal severe colonic dilation — a surgical emergency, not a drug side effect. Never dismiss a newly distended, tender abdomen as "just the IBD."

🧠 "Big belly + hot + hurting = phone, not chart."

💧 Chronic diarrhea — what to actually offer

If diarrhea is chronic, encourage increased fluid intake. The source names the exact options:

🍵Weak tea 
💧Water 
🍲Bouillonsalty broth
🧃Pedialyteelectrolytes
🥤Gatoradeelectrolytes
  • 📋 Monitor fluid intake & output — the number, not the impression.
  • ⚖️ Daily weight is the fastest fluid-loss detector.
  • 🧪 Ongoing diarrhea also risks hypokalemia — watch for muscle weakness and cramps.
🧠 "Water alone won't fix a salt problem." Broth and electrolyte drinks replace what water can't.

🌼 Herbal consideration — chamomile

Chamomile is one of the most popular teas in Europe and is on the FDA list of herbs generally recognised as safe (GRAS). Traditional uses include a mild sedative and treatment of digestive upset, menstrual cramps and stomach ulcers; topically for skin irritation and inflammation. As an infusion it appears to produce an antispasmodic effect on GI smooth muscle and to protect against the development of stomach ulcers.

SAFETY The infusion is prepared from pollen-filled flower heads. In people hypersensitive to ragweed, asters and chrysanthemums it has caused reactions ranging from mild contact dermatitis to severe anaphylaxis. Always ask about ragweed/daisy-family allergy before endorsing chamomile tea.

🧠 "Chamomile is a daisy." If ragweed makes them sneeze, the tea can make them swell. Same botanical family (Asteraceae).

🎯 Cover & check — 6 rapid-fire questions
Q1: How do aminosalicylates work?
They exert a topical anti-inflammatory effect in the bowel. The exact mechanism of action is unknown — say that, it's the expected answer.
Q2: A client on mesalamine has hypoactive bowel sounds and is passing small amounts of loose stool. What are you thinking?
Severe obstipation with overflow — liquid stool leaking around a hard fecal mass. It looks like diarrhea but the client is constipated, and needs very different drug therapy. Do not give an antidiarrheal.
Q3: Four interactions and their consequences?
Digoxin — reduced digoxin absorption. Methotrexate — increased risk of immunosuppression. Oral hypoglycemics — increased blood glucose. Warfarin — increased risk of bleeding.
Q4: Which allergy must be screened before sulfasalazine?
Sulfonamide (sulfa) and sulfite hypersensitivity — a contraindication for the class.
Q5: Three things you report immediately?
Abdominal distention, fever, and abdominal pain.
Q6: Your client with ragweed allergy asks about chamomile tea for cramps. Response?
Caution — chamomile is made from pollen-filled flower heads of the daisy family. People hypersensitive to ragweed, asters or chrysanthemums have had reactions from contact dermatitis to severe anaphylaxis. Discuss with the provider first.
