💉 Childhood Immunisations

Which vaccines are live, who cannot have them, and what is a true contraindication versus a myth.

🚨 Live vaccines — the ones with rules

Live vaccines contain weakened organisms, so they carry restrictions the others do not.

  • MMR — measles, mumps, rubella
  • Varicella (chickenpox)
  • Rotavirus (oral)
  • Live attenuated influenza (nasal spray)
  • MMRV, yellow fever, oral typhoid

Live vaccines are not given in pregnancy, or to a severely immunocompromised child. Give MMR and varicella postpartum, and advise avoiding pregnancy for 4 weeks after.

A child living with an immunocompromised person CAN still have most live vaccines — in fact vaccinating the household protects the vulnerable person. Only specific situations require avoiding it, which is why this is asked.

A runny nose and a temperature of 100F is not a reason to delay a vaccine. That is one of the most commonly missed questions.

Egg allergy is no longer a contraindication to influenza vaccine for most children.

💉 Administration and aftercare

Where and how

  • Infants: IM into the vastus lateralis — the largest muscle mass
  • Toddlers and older: deltoid once muscle mass allows
  • Different vaccines go in different sites, at least 1 inch apart
  • Document site, lot number and expiry — legally required

Never aspirate before an IM vaccine, and never give a vaccine into the dorsogluteal site in a small child - sciatic nerve risk and poor immune response.

✅ What to tell parents

  • Expected: soreness, redness, low-grade fever, fussiness for 1–2 days
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen for comfort — not routinely before, as it may blunt the response
  • A cool compress on the site; move the limb normally
  • Never aspirin - Reye syndrome
  • Report: high fever, a seizure, persistent inexplicable crying over 3 hours, or any difficulty breathing
  • Stay 15 minutes after for observation

  • Mild illness is NOT a reason to delay
