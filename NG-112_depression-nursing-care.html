Depression: Nursing Care 🛟

Suicide risk assessment · therapeutic communication · milieu safety

NG-112 MENTAL HEALTH ADHD-friendly visual edition

Once you know a client is depressed, the nursing job shifts to one priority: is this client safe? That means asking about suicide directly, recognizing the paradox where a sudden lift in mood can mean rising danger, and building a safe, structured, low-demand environment while therapy and medication take weeks to work.

🚨 Ask directly“Do you have thoughts of hurting yourself? Do you have a plan?” — never dance around it.
⚠️ Calmer + more energyafter being severely depressed = INCREASED risk, not improvement.
👁️ 1:1 continuousobservation is the priority intervention for active suicidal ideation with a plan.
🗣️ Sit. Listen.Simple, direct language + your presence — therapeutic communication is an intervention.
🧨

CAUSE

STEP 1 · WHAT KIND, AND WHY

Depression isn't one thing — the type changes the teaching. The mechanism is the same low-neurotransmitter picture underneath all of them.

🗓️ Four faces of depression — same low mood, different timeline

🧠 “The Big 4” — MDD hits hard & leaves, Dysthymia stays low forever, SAD comes back every winter, Peripartum shows up around baby. Same low mood, four different clocks.

🧠 Mechanism, in one card

Depression is linked to low levels of serotonin, norepinephrine & dopamine in the brain — everything runs low and slow: mood, energy, sleep, appetite, concentration.

🔗 Full neurotransmitter diagram & the 9-symptom "SIG E CAPS" breakdown live on NG-031 Depression: Signs & Symptoms — this page assumes that baseline and focuses on what the nurse does about it.

🧠 Low & slow = the whole disease in three words.

⚠️ Who's at higher risk

  • 👪 Family history of depression or suicide
  • 🔁 Prior suicide attempt — the single strongest predictor
  • 🍺 Substance use disorder
  • 🏥 Chronic or terminal illness, chronic pain
  • 😔 Recent loss — job, relationship, bereavement
  • 🧍 Social isolation, lack of support system
🧠 A prior attempt is the single biggest red flag in the whole chart — always screen history before you screen today's mood.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT THE RISK

Symptoms tell you the client is depressed. Risk clues tell you whether they are about to act on it — a completely different question.

🧾 SIG E CAPS — the 9 symptoms (recap)

  • Sleep changes
  • Interest lost (anhedonia)
  • Guilt / worthlessness
  • Energy loss (anergia)
  • Concentration poor
  • Appetite/weight change
  • Psychomotor slowing or agitation
  • Suicidal ideation

+ depressed mood most of the day, most days. Diagnostic threshold and full detail on NG-031.

🚨 A PRIORITY: suicide risk — the warning signs that override everything else

  • 🎁 Giving away cherished/valued possessions
  • 💬 Statements: “I can't go on,” “I do not want to live,” “I won't be a problem much longer,” “This will all be over soon”
  • 📈📉 A sudden, abrupt change in energy — in either direction
  • 🗒️ Making final arrangements, settling affairs, saying goodbye
🧠 “Gifts + goodbyes = red flags.” A depressed client who suddenly starts giving things away or sounds like they're wrapping things up is telling you something — take it literally, not figuratively.

⚠️ The classic NCLEX trap — calmer or more energetic can mean MORE danger, not less

Early in treatment, physical energy and psychomotor drive can return before the depressed, hopeless mood lifts. The client now has both the intent (still hopeless) and the energy (newly restored) to act on a plan — this is one of the highest-yield NCLEX points in psych. A client who suddenly seems calm, smiling, and "cured" after being severely depressed needs closer observation, not less.

🧠 “Energy comes back before hope does.” Never chart a sudden mood lift as reassuring without reassessing suicidal ideation first.

🗣️ Ask it directly — the exact assessment questions

  • “Have you had any thoughts of hurting yourself?”
  • “Do you have a plan to end your life?”
  • “Do you have access to the means to carry out that plan?”
  • “Do you want to die?”

Asking directly does not plant the idea and does not increase risk — it is the single most important, evidence-based nursing action. Vague or indirect questions get vague, unreliable answers.

🧠 “Plan + means + intent” = the three things every suicide risk assessment must uncover. The more specific and immediate the plan and access, the higher the risk level and the more restrictive the intervention.
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · KEEP THEM SAFE

Least restrictive environment that is still safe — then presence, structure, and time while treatment works.

🚨 Safety interventions — the priority ladder

1
Continuous 1:1 observation — active plan, means, and intent = highest risk, arm's-length supervision at all times
2
Room close to the nurses' station, not an isolated private room
3
Remove harmful objects from the room and environment per unit protocol (means restriction)
4
Supervise during meals and personal care
5
Reassess every shift — thoughts can escalate or de-escalate quickly
🧠 Risk level sets the observation level. The more specific the plan and the more available the means, the further right on this staircase the client goes.

🗣️ Therapeutic communication

  • ✅ Use simple, direct language — depressed clients have low concentration/energy for complex conversation
  • ✅ Sit with the client, even in silence — your presence is an intervention
  • ✅ “I'd like to hear about how you are feeling now” — open, non-judgmental
  • ✅ Reflect and validate feelings without minimizing them

Never use empty reassurance — “everything will be fine,” “you have so much to live for” — it shuts the conversation down and feels dismissive.

🧠 Presence > advice. The best therapeutic response is almost always an invitation to keep talking, not a solution.

✅ Milieu & activity — invite, don't force

  • Offer a structured program of activities the client can choose to join
  • Invite and encourage group participation — do not command it
  • Assist with ADLs — offer to help the client get dressed, sit with them, walk with them
  • Look for improvement clues: talking about future plans, goals, family, holidays — a genuine sign of progress vs. the false calm of a fixed plan
🧠 A client who says “I talked with my family about ways we can celebrate the holidays together” is showing real improvement — future-oriented language is reassuring in a way sudden calm is not.

🍽️ Nutrition

Depression commonly causes appetite and weight change (usually loss) — poor intake is a real medical risk, not just a symptom to note.

  • Small, frequent, high-calorie / high-protein meals and snacks
  • Stay with the client during meals
  • Weekly weighing — the most reliable way to evaluate whether nutrition interventions are working
🧠 Trend the weight, not the tray. A client can pick at food and still be losing ground — the weekly weight is the objective outcome measure.

QUICK RECALL

SAY IT OUT LOUD
🎁 Giving things away+ goodbye statements = act on it, don't dismiss it

👁️ Highest risk= continuous 1:1 observation

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A client with major depression and suicidal ideation is suddenly calmer and more energetic. What should the nurse consider?
This can mean INCREASED suicide risk — the client may now have the energy to act on a plan. Increase the level of observation/precautions and reassess immediately, don't assume improvement.
Q2: A client says he has no reason to continue living. What should the nurse ask first?
Ask directly and specifically: "Do you have any plans to end your life right now?" — assess for plan, means, and intent before anything else.
Q3: A previously depressed client admits to a plan for suicide. What is the priority action?
Provide continuous one-to-one observation/supervision — the highest level of safety precaution.
Q4: A client is crying alone, refusing breakfast and morning care. What's the best intervention?
Offer to sit with the client and help them get dressed — presence, simple direct language, and gentle assistance with ADLs, not pressure or lectures.
