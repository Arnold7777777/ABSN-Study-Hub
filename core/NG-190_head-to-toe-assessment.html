HEAD TO TOE, IN ORDER

STEP 2 · THE WALK-THROUGH

Same direction every time — head/neck, chest, abdomen, extremities/circulation. Consistency is what lets you spot when something's off.

🧍 The eight-stop sequence

🧠 "HEN-CAB-E" — Head, Eyes/ENT, Neck, Chest, Abdomen, Breast, Extremities. Same order, every client, every time — that consistency is what makes a change stand out.

🧑‍⚕️ Head & neck

  • Hair — clean, well-kept?
  • Cranial nerves assessed (if in program scope)
  • Eyes — PERRLA, sclera white, conjunctiva pink/moist, no debris/drainage
  • Nares — check patency, occlude one nostril at a time
  • Mouth — oral mucosa pink & moist, no caries/redness/lesions; tongue midline
  • Jaw/neck — palpate for swollen or painful lymph nodes
🧠 PERRLA = Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light & Accommodation.

🫁 Chest — heart, lungs, breast

  • Heart: 5-point auscultation (Aortic → Pulmonic → Erb's → Tricuspid → Mitral)
  • Lungs: RR should be even & unlabored; auscultate anterior + posterior ladder
  • Breast (if in scope): check for lumps, drainage, nipple size/color changes
FULL DETAIL NG-082 Assessment Locations — the exact 5 heart points and lung auscultation ladder.

🫃 Abdomen

  • Shape: flat, non-pulsating, even
  • Bowel sounds: active in all 4 quadrants; if absent, listen 5 min before charting "absent"
  • Order: Inspect → Auscultate → Percuss → Palpate
  • Ask about pain on palpation
FULL DETAIL NG-082 Assessment Locations — the four-quadrant map.

🦵 Extremities & circulation

  • Peripheral pulses: radial, brachial, femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibialis (never palpate both carotids at once)
  • Capillary refill: <3 sec
  • Skin turgor: returns <3 sec; skin warm, dry, intact
  • Temperature equal in all extremities
  • ROM in all joints + DTRs (if in program scope)
FULL DETAIL NG-082 Assessment Locations — every pulse landmark, illustrated.
📌 Program note: not every nursing program teaches DTRs, cranial nerve exams, or breast exams as part of the routine head-to-toe — check your school's skills manual and your state's nurse practice act before performing/omitting them.

NORMAL vs RED FLAG

STEP 3 · KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

Same eight stops — here's what "normal" looks like next to what should make you stop and reassess.

📋 System-by-system: normal vs red flag

SystemNormal findingRed flag
EyesPERRLA, sclera whiteUnequal/nonreactive pupils
NeckNo swollen/painful nodesTender, enlarged lymph nodes
LungsEven, unlabored RR 12–20Crackles, wheezes, rhonchi, labored breathing
HeartRegular rate & rhythm at all 5 pointsNew murmur, irregular rhythm
AbdomenFlat, non-pulsating, active bowel sounds x4Rigid/board-like, absent sounds x5min, pulsating mass
Pulses2+ bilateral, equalAbsent, markedly unequal, thready/weak
SkinWarm, dry, intact; cap refill & turgor <3sCool/clammy, tenting, cap refill >3s, cyanosis

🚨 Priority combo: rigid belly + fever + tachycardia

Board-like abdomen, fever >100.3°F, rebound tenderness, restlessness, tachycardia/tachypnea = suspected peritonitis — report to the HCP as a priority.

⚠️ NCLEX trap: skipping the sequence

Jumping around the body (e.g., checking a pulse, then eyes, then coming back to the chest) increases the chance of missing a finding. Stick to head-to-toe order every time.

🩻 IAPP — the one order that flips for the abdomen

Touching the belly before listening can stir up bowel sounds and give a false reading — so auscultation jumps ahead of percussion and palpation for this one system only.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What six things happen before the nurse touches the client?
Introduce yourself, provide privacy, wash hands, obtain consent, assess for allergies, obtain a psychosocial assessment.
Q2: What is the correct order for abdominal assessment, and why does it differ from other systems?
Inspect, Auscultate, Percuss, Palpate — palpating first can artificially stimulate bowel sounds and give a false reading.
Q3: The client has a rigid, board-like abdomen, a fever, and tachycardia. What's the priority?
Suspect peritonitis — report to the HCP immediately; this is a priority finding.
