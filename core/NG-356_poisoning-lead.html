☠️ Poisoning & Lead Exposure

What to do first, what never to induce, and why lead damages the nervous system silently.

🚨 Acute poisoning — the first actions

Toddlers explore by mouth, so poisoning peaks around age 2.

1Assess the child — ABC first
2Remove any remaining substance from the mouth
3Call Poison Control — before giving anything
4Take the container with you to hospital
5Treat as directed — activated charcoal, antidote, supportive care

🚨 The absolute nevers

  • Never induce vomiting. Syrup of ipecac is no longer recommended.
  • Never induce vomiting with a caustic substance - lye, drain cleaner, oven cleaner. It burns the oesophagus a second time on the way back up.
  • Never induce vomiting with a petroleum product - petrol, paraffin, furniture polish. Aspiration causes chemical pneumonitis.

Take the container. Identifying the substance and its concentration changes the treatment entirely, and a frightened parent’s description is often wrong.

SubstanceAntidote / treatment
Paracetamol (acetaminophen)N-acetylcysteine — most effective within 8 hours
OpioidsNaloxone
IronDeferoxamine; iron tablets look like sweets and are a classic paediatric poisoning
BenzodiazepinesFlumazenil (used cautiously)
OrganophosphatesAtropine; look for SLUDGE and pinpoint pupils

✅ Prevention teaching

  • Locked cupboards, high shelves — child-resistant is not child-proof
  • Keep everything in its original container — never in a drinks bottle
  • Do not call medicine “sweets” to get a child to take it
  • Handbags and visitors’ bags are a common source — tablets in a pocket
  • Poison Control number saved in the phone and on the fridge

🧲 Lead exposure

Lead damages the developing nervous system, and it does it silently. Most children with raised levels have no symptoms at all — which is why screening exists.

The commonest source is deteriorating lead paint in housing built before 1978 - chips and, more importantly, the dust.

🧠 Where it comes from

  • Old paint — chips, and dust from friction on windows and doors
  • Contaminated soil near old buildings or busy roads
  • Old plumbing — run the tap and use cold water for drinking and formula
  • Imported pottery, cosmetics, some traditional remedies, some toys
  • A parent’s work or hobby — brought home on clothing

Pica — eating non-food items — raises the risk considerably.

When there are symptoms 
NeurologicalIrritability, poor attention, developmental delay or regression, learning problems
GIAbdominal pain, vomiting, constipation, poor appetite
BloodMicrocytic anaemia — lead interferes with haem production
SevereSeizures, altered consciousness, encephalopathy

✅ Management

  • Find and remove the source — this is the definitive treatment; treating the child while they return to the same house achieves nothing
  • Chelation for high levels — succimer, EDTA, dimercaprol. Ensure good hydration and monitor renal function
  • Iron and calcium-rich diet — a well-nourished gut absorbs less lead. Deficiency increases absorption
  • Frequent wet mopping and damp dusting; wash hands and toys often
  • Developmental follow-up — effects can appear later as school difficulties

There is no known safe blood lead level. Prevention is the only reliable protection.

🎯 NCLEX traps

  • Never induce vomiting — especially caustics and petroleum products
  • Take the container to hospital
  • Call Poison Control before giving anything
  • Paracetamol antidote is N-acetylcysteine
  • Lead is usually asymptomatic — screening finds it
  • Remove the source; good iron and calcium reduce absorption
