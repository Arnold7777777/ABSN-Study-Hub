Scoliosis 🌀

Lateral Spinal Curvature · Screening · Bracing vs. Spinal Fusion

NG-211 Musculoskeletal ADHD-friendly visual edition

Scoliosis is a lateral (sideways) curvature of the spine that bends it into an "S" or "C" shape instead of straight. Most often caught during adolescent growth spurts, it's screened with the Adam's forward bend test and staged by the Cobb angle on X-ray. Treatment tracks severity: watch it, brace it, or fuse it.

CAUSE

STEP 1 · A SPINE THAT CURVES SIDEWAYS

Most cases have no single known cause — but growth spurts and a handful of underlying conditions raise the risk.

🌀 Normal spine vs. scoliotic spine

Scoliosis — say the word: S for lateral curvature, an "S"-shaped spine instead of straight. Curves can also be single "C" shapes depending on where along the spine they occur.

🧠 "S is for Scoliosis, S is for S-shaped spine." The name tells you the shape.

🧬 Pathophysiology & risk factors

  • Most idiopathic cases have no single identified cause; one theory points to a possible defect of the intervertebral discs — the shock absorbers between vertebrae
  • Strongly associated with periods of rapid growth, especially the adolescent growth spurt
  • Most common in females, ages 10–12
  • Also seen with cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy, and Marfan syndrome (neuromuscular or connective-tissue causes)
🧠 "Growth spurt, spine curves out." The faster the bones grow, the more likely an already-vulnerable spine curves before it can be caught.

🩺 What the curve does to the body

  • Mild to severe back pain
  • Stiffened spine that limits movement
  • Severe, untreated cases can cause a deformity of the chest cavity, which can affect breathing & cardiac function
🧠 A curve isn't just cosmetic — severe curves can compress the chest and crowd the heart & lungs.

CLUES

STEP 2 · SCREEN IT, MEASURE IT

One bedside test screens for it; one X-ray measurement stages it and drives every treatment decision.

🙇 Adam's forward bend test — the screening tool

Client bends forward at the waist, arms hanging, feet together. The examiner looks from behind for asymmetry — one side of the rib cage or back rising higher than the other (a "rib hump"). A positive test is followed by X-ray.

📐 Cobb angle — measuring severity

Lines are drawn along the most-tilted vertebrae at the top & bottom of the curve on X-ray; the angle between them is the Cobb angle — the number that stages the curve.

📊 General severity thresholds

General treatment ranges — exact cutoffs vary by provider & growth status.

Cobb angleTypical approach
<10°Normal variant / not scoliosis
10–25°Mild — observation, repeat X-rays
25–40°Moderate — bracing, esp. if still growing
>40–50°Severe — surgical candidate (spinal fusion)
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · MATCH TREATMENT TO SEVERITY

Watch mild curves, brace moderate ones during growth, and fuse severe curves — then protect the spine after surgery.

👀 Mild: observation

  • Periodic X-ray & Cobb angle rechecks to track progression
  • No brace or surgery needed if the curve stays small

🩹 Moderate: bracing — the Boston brace

Used for moderate curves, especially while the client is still growing. Wear a cotton shirt underneath the brace at all times to protect the skin from irritation/breakdown. Bracing doesn't reverse the curve — it aims to prevent progression.

🔪 Severe: spinal fusion surgery

Reserved for severe curves (generally >40–50°) or curves that keep progressing despite bracing. Rods & instrumentation are placed to straighten & permanently fuse the affected vertebrae.

Post-fusion nursing priorities:

  • 🪵 Log-roll to turn — keep the spine in one straight aligned line, use a draw sheet with adequate staff
  • 🖐️ Regular neurovascular checks of the lower extremities (pulses, movement, sensation) — same skill as the 6 P's on the Fracture page in this series
  • 💊 Aggressive pain control to support early mobilization
  • 🚶 Early ambulation per surgeon protocol, once cleared
  • ❌ Avoid twisting or bending the fused spine during recovery
🧠 "One log, one line." Log-rolling keeps every vertebra moving together so the new fusion isn't stressed by a twist.

💬 Psychosocial & activity teaching

  • Encourage continued social interaction — visiting friends, normal activities — bracing shouldn't isolate an adolescent
  • Avoid participating in contact sports while braced or during post-fusion recovery, per provider guidance
  • Body image support — a back brace during adolescence can be emotionally difficult; validate and involve the client in care

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What test screens for scoliosis, and what does a positive result look like?
Adam's forward bend test — the client bends forward at the waist and the examiner looks for asymmetry, such as a rib hump on one side.
Q2: What measurement on X-ray determines the severity and treatment path?
The Cobb angle — the angle between the most-tilted vertebrae at the top and bottom of the curve.
Q3: What is essential teaching for a client wearing a Boston brace?
Wear a cotton shirt underneath the brace at all times to protect the skin; also avoid contact sports while braced.
Q4: A client is recovering from spinal fusion surgery. What two nursing priorities protect the new fusion and the legs?
Log-roll the client to keep the spine in one straight line when turning, and perform regular neurovascular checks of the lower extremities.
