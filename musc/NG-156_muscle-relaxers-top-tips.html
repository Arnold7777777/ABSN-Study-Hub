SNAPSHOTS

STEP 1 · MEET THE DRUGS

Four named drugs, four separate personalities — one memory hook each.

🔥 Dantrolene — the muscle-fiber blocker

Used for:

  • 🌡️ Malignant hyperthermia — acute IV treatment
  • 🧩 Chronic spasticity: spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis

Watch for:

  • 😴 Sedation / drowsiness
  • 🧪 Hepatotoxicity — monitor liver function
  • ❌ Contraindicated: active hepatic disease, lactation, spasm from rheumatic disorders
🧠 "Calcium CONTRACTS, dantrolene BLOCKS." Less calcium release = less contraction. And: Dantrolene = treats the FIRE (malignant hyperthermia) but can BURN the liver — check LFTs.

😵 Cyclobenzaprine — the TCA look-alike

Used for: acute muscle spasm — short-term only (max 2–3 weeks).

Watch for:

  • 😴 Sedation, anticholinergic effects (structurally similar to tricyclic antidepressants)
  • ❌ Contraindicated: recent MI, cardiac conduction disorders, hyperthyroidism
  • ❌ Within 14 days of an MAOI — risk of high fever & convulsions
🧠 "CycloBACKzzaprine" — relaxes the back, but makes you zzz's. And: it's a cousin of TCAs — so it inherits the classic anticholinergic list ("can't see, can't spit, can't pee, can't poop") plus the cardiac cautions TCAs carry.

🚫 Baclofen — GABA-B agonist, never stop abruptly

MOA: enhances GABA (an inhibitory neurotransmitter) at GABA-B receptors — "makes everything low & slow."

Used for: decreased flexor/extensor spasticity — spinal cord injury, MS, cerebral palsy.

Watch for:

  • 😵 Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness on position change — expected, not a hold parameter
  • 🤢 Nausea
  • 🚨 Never abruptly stop — rhabdomyolysis, multi-organ failure, seizures, rebound spasticity
  • ❌ Contraindicated: spasm from rheumatic disorders
🧠 "Baclofen — back off slowly." This is the single highest-yield safety point in the whole muscle relaxant class: taper, never stop cold. Dizziness with position change is expected, not a reason to withhold the dose.

🔒 Carisoprodol — controlled substance, abuse potential

Used for: acute muscle spasm — e.g., after surgery on open fractures.

Watch for:

  • 🔒 Controlled substance — risk of dependence and abuse
  • 😴 Drowsiness & sedation
  • 🛑 Taper off — do not stop abruptly
  • ❌ Contraindicated: hypersensitivity to meprobamate
🧠 "Carisoprodol → meprobamate." The metabolite is itself a controlled sedative-hypnotic — that's exactly why carisoprodol carries abuse potential and is a controlled substance.

NCLEX TRAPS

STEP 2 · TOP MISSED QUESTIONS

The traps test-writers reuse across this class — spot the pattern, not just the answer.

📝 Missed-question pattern: newly prescribed cyclobenzaprine

A classic SATA stem: "When providing education to a patient newly prescribed cyclobenzaprine, which instructions should be included?" Options mix real teaching with disguised distractors. Practice spotting the pattern rather than memorizing this exact stem:

✅ Likely correct: Report excessive/unusual drowsiness, especially before driving or hazardous tasks. Do not stop the medication abruptly after regular use. Avoid alcohol entirely.
❌ Classic distractor: "Limit wine to 3–4 glasses per day" — any answer that quantifies "a little alcohol is fine" is wrong. The real teaching is avoid, not limit.
❌ Classic distractor: An oddly specific, low fever cutoff (e.g., "notify provider of temp above 99.5°F") — normal fever thresholds you're taught elsewhere are higher; be suspicious of numbers that don't match standard teaching.
🧠 Trap-spotting rule: "moderation" answers and off-standard numbers are usually wrong. On any CNS-depressant medication question, the safe teaching is almost always the absolute one (avoid, never, do not stop) — not a hedged, permissive version.

🚨 Dantrolene dual identity trap

Test writers love testing whether you know dantrolene has two separate uses: emergency IV treatment of malignant hyperthermia AND oral use for chronic spasticity. Don't assume a dantrolene question is automatically about MH — read the scenario.

🧠 One drug, two hats: 🔥 MH = acute IV rescue · 🧩 spasticity = chronic oral therapy.

🚨 Baclofen "dizziness" trap

A client reports dizziness with position changes on baclofen. This is NOT a reason to withhold the dose or call it a contraindication — orthostatic dizziness is an expected side effect. Teach slow position changes instead.

🧠 Expected ≠ emergency. Save your "hold the dose" instinct for abrupt discontinuation, not routine orthostatic dizziness.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Which two conditions does dantrolene treat, and how do the routes differ?
Malignant hyperthermia (acute IV treatment) and chronic spasticity from spinal cord injury/MS/cerebral palsy (oral). Watch liver function — hepatotoxicity risk.
Q2: Why does cyclobenzaprine carry anticholinergic side effects?
It's structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), so it inherits dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, and tachycardia — plus TCA-style cardiac cautions.
Q3: A client on baclofen wants to stop today because they feel fine. What do you teach?
Never stop abruptly — taper under provider guidance. Sudden discontinuation risks rebound spasticity, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, and multi-organ failure.
Q4: Why is carisoprodol a controlled substance?
It's metabolized in the liver to meprobamate, an active sedative-hypnotic metabolite, giving carisoprodol dependence and abuse potential (Schedule IV).
