Rheumatoid Arthritis 🖐️

RA — Chronic Autoimmune Joint Disease

NG-163 Musculoskeletal · Group D ADHD-friendly visual edition

RA is an autoimmune disorder — the body attacks its OWN joints, causing major inflammation & deformity. Mainly the small hand joints, but it's systemic — it can hit the skin, eyes, and lungs too. Bilateral. Symmetric. Body-wide.

CAUSE

STEP 1 · THE IMMUNE ATTACK

The body mistakes its own joint lining for a threat — and the attack doesn't stay in the joint.

🧬 Autoimmune attack on the synovium

RA is thought to be an autoimmune disorder — the immune system targets the synovium (joint lining), causing chronic inflammation. Over time inflamed synovial tissue thickens into pannus, which erodes cartilage and bone — this is what produces the classic hand deformities.

🧠 "Pannus eats the joint." Pannus = the overgrown, angry synovial tissue that literally chews through cartilage and bone — that's why RA damage is often irreversible if untreated.

🌍 It's systemic — not just joints

Because RA is an autoimmune, body-wide disease, it can cause collateral damage in:

  • 🖐️ Joints — mainly small hand joints
  • 🩹 Skin — rheumatoid nodules
  • 👁️ Eyes — dryness, scleritis
  • 🫁 Lungs — pulmonary nodules/fibrosis
🧠 RA is systemic; OA is not — this is the single highest-yield line examiners quote directly.

⚠️ Risk factors

  • 👩 Female sex — much higher incidence
  • 🧬 Genetic susceptibility (family history)
  • 🚬 Smoking
  • 🎂 Peak onset ages 30–60
🧠 RA doesn't "wear out" a joint — it attacks one. Wear-and-tear thinking belongs to osteoarthritis, not RA.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT THE PATTERN

Symmetric small-joint pain, systemic symptoms, stiffness that lingers, and the labs that confirm it.

🖐️ Signature pattern: symmetric, small joints, systemic

Early signsWhat they mean
Fatigue, anorexia, weight lossSystemic inflammatory symptoms — the whole body is involved
Symmetrical pain & swelling, small joints of the handsClassic RA distribution — both hands, same joints
Morning joint stiffnessLasts >30–60 min — inflammatory, not mechanical
Low-grade feverSystemic inflammation marker
🧠 "RA is a mirror." Whatever happens to one hand happens to the other — symmetry is the tell.

⏱️ RA vs OA morning stiffness — do NOT mix these up

Rheumatoid ArthritisOsteoarthritis (see NG-168)
Autoimmune — systemicWear-and-tear — local
Stiffness >30–60 minStiffness <30 min
Symmetric, small joints (hands)Asymmetric, weight-bearing joints (knees, hips)
Fatigue, low-grade fever, nodulesNo systemic symptoms
🧠 "Long stiff = autoimmune gift; short stiff = joints that just got stiff from use." If the question says stiffness lasting over an hour, think RA — not OA.

🖐️ Classic hand deformities

🧠 "Swan reaches UP, buttonhole caves IN." A swan's neck curves up and out; a boutonniere (buttonhole) sinks the middle joint in.

🧪 Labs — all trend UP in active RA

🧠 "RF and anti-CCP point AT RA; ESR and CRP just say something's inflamed." Anti-CCP is the more RA-specific antibody test.

🔬 Diagnostics

  • Synovial fluid aspiration — analyzes joint fluid for inflammation
  • Arthroscopy — direct visualization when X-ray/MRI are inconclusive
  • Blood tests: RF (rheumatoid factor), ESR, CRP — plus anti-CCP for higher specificity
🧠 Confirming RA takes labs + imaging + fluid analysis together — no single test rules it in or out alone.

CARE

STEP 3 · PROTECT THE JOINT

Heat and cold, low-impact movement, a medication ladder that ends in DMARDs, and one classic "never."

💊 Medication ladder — symptom control → disease control

1
NSAIDs — pain/inflammation relief, does not slow disease
2
Steroids — the "-sone" drugs, e.g. prednisone, for flares/bridge therapy
3
DMARDs — methotrexate first-line; see the DMARDs page (NG-114) for full dosing & safety
🧠 "NSAIDs and steroids calm the fire; DMARDs put it out." Only DMARDs actually modify/slow the disease course.

🔥❄️ Heat & cold, alternated

  • Alternate applying heat and cold to affected joints
  • Low-impact exercise — swimming is ideal (non-weight-bearing)
  • Warm shower or bath before bed to ease morning stiffness
🧠 "Heat loosens, cold calms." Heat before activity to loosen stiff joints; cold after activity to calm an inflamed one.

🚨 Classic never: don't prop the knees

Do NOT elevate the knees with pillows at night — it feels comfortable in the moment but encourages flexion contractures, permanently limiting extension over time.

🧠 Comfortable now ≠ safe long-term — a flexed knee that never extends fully is a permanent mobility loss.

🎯 Whole-person care plan

  • Assess and treat pain levels regularly
  • Pain relief WITH activity, but MORE pain at rest — expect this pattern in RA, unlike OA where activity worsens pain
  • Watch for joint contractures from prolonged immobility
  • Coordinate PT/OT for joint protection and adaptive devices
🧠 "Rusty hinge rule." RA joints hurt more at rest and loosen with gentle motion — like a rusty hinge that eases once it starts moving.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What is the key difference between RA and osteoarthritis?
RA is a systemic autoimmune disease; osteoarthritis is a local wear-and-tear disease. RA morning stiffness lasts >30–60 min vs OA's <30 min.
Q2: What labs confirm rheumatoid arthritis?
RF (rheumatoid factor) and anti-CCP (more RA-specific), plus ESR and CRP for general inflammation — alongside synovial fluid aspiration and arthroscopy.
Q3: Why should you never put a pillow under an RA patient's knees at night?
It promotes flexion contracture — the knee loses the ability to fully extend over time.
Q4: What's the first-line DMARD for RA, and why is it added?
Methotrexate — added when NSAIDs/anti-inflammatories alone can't control pain and immobility; DMARDs slow the actual disease process, not just symptoms.
