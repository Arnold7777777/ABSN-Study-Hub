Atypical Antidepressants 🧩

Trazodone · Bupropion (Wellbutrin) · Mirtazapine — the ones that don't fit the SSRI/SNRI/MAOI mold

NG-101 MENTAL HEALTH ADHD-friendly visual edition

"Atypical" just means none of these three work through the standard SSRI/SNRI/MAOI mechanism — each has its own quirky target, and each earns its spot on the exam through one signature side effect: trazodone's priapism, bupropion's seizure risk, mirtazapine's weight gain.

😴 Trazodone = sleepSedating → used off-label as a sleep aid; take at night.
⚡ Bupropion = seizuresLowers seizure threshold — avoid in seizure or eating disorder history.
🍽️ Mirtazapine = appetiteSedation + weight gain — good pick for poor sleep/appetite.
🚨 PriapismErection lasting hours on trazodone = go to the ER now.
💊

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · MECHANISM

Three drugs, three different targets — none of them are classic serotonin reuptake blockers.

🧠 Three different mechanisms, one drug class label

🧠 “SARI sleeps, NDRI energizes, NaSSA feeds.” Match the mechanism to the side-effect profile and you'll never mix these three up.

😴 Trazodone — the sleep-aid antidepressant

Indication: depression; very commonly used off-label as a sleep aid at lower doses because of its sedating effect.

Mnemonic: TraZZZadone / TRANCE-adone — sleepy and sedated.

🧠 If the question describes a depressed client with severe insomnia getting a low-dose bedtime medication that isn't a benzodiazepine — think trazodone.

🚭 Bupropion (Wellbutrin) — depression + smoking cessation

Indication: depression and as an aid to stop smoking (marketed as Zyban for that indication, same drug).

Comes in SR (sustained-release) and XL (extended-release) formulations.

🧠 “Bupropion helps you quit and stay energized.” It's the antidepressant of choice when a client also wants to stop smoking or is worried about sexual side effects/weight gain.

🍽️ Mirtazapine — sedation + appetite, on purpose

Indication: depression, especially useful when the client also has significant insomnia and poor appetite/weight loss — the side effects become the therapeutic benefit.

🧠 “Mirtaz-appetite.” Picture a fork and a pillow — this drug makes people eat and sleep, which is exactly what an underweight, sleepless depressed client needs.
⚠️

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · SIDE EFFECTS

Each drug has one signature danger sign the exam loves to test.

🚨 Trazodone — priapism is a medical emergency

  • 😴 Sedation, drowsiness
  • 📉 Orthostatic hypotension — dizziness with position changes
  • 🚨 Priapism — rare but serious: an erection that persists for hours unrelated to sexual activity

Priapism is a urologic emergency — untreated, it risks permanent tissue damage. Teach the client to go to the emergency department immediately if this occurs.

🧠 Rare ≠ ignorable. Priapism is the one trazodone side effect tested over and over precisely because students forget to mention it's an emergency.

⚡ Bupropion — lowers the seizure threshold

Avoid bupropion in clients with:

  • 🌀 A seizure disorder or history of seizures
  • 🍽️ A current or past eating disorder (anorexia or bulimia nervosa) — electrolyte shifts from purging further lower the seizure threshold
🧠 “Bu-PRO-pion, PRO the seizures? No — PRO-tect against giving it in seizure/eating-disorder history.” Seizure risk is the #1 safety flag for this drug.

😵 Bupropion — activating side effects

  • 😴➡️😳 Insomnia (opposite of trazodone/mirtazapine)
  • 🤕 Headache
  • ⚖️ Weight loss — unlike most antidepressants
  • 💚 No significant sexual side effects — a real advantage over SSRIs (see NG-205)
🧠 If the question says a client switched antidepressants because of sexual dysfunction, bupropion is a classic answer choice.

⚖️ Mirtazapine — sedation & weight gain

  • 😴 Significant sedation — dose at bedtime
  • ⚖️ Weight gain and increased appetite (the flip side of bupropion)
  • 😵 Dizziness, dry mouth

Low sexual side-effect profile, similar advantage to bupropion, but the mechanism and target population differ.

🧠 Bupropion and mirtazapine are opposites on sleep and weight — insomnia/weight loss vs. sedation/weight gain — pick based on what the client needs more of.

🧠 One glance, one drug: droopy face + warning = trazodone, alert face + down arrow = bupropion, droopy face + up arrow = mirtazapine.

TEACH

STEP 3 · PATIENT EDUCATION

Formulation rules, timing, and what to do if a warning sign shows up.

🚫 Bupropion SR/XL — never crush, chew, or cut

🚫Never crush
🚫Never chew
🚫Never cut
🚫Never double up on a missed dose

Crushing/chewing an XL (extended-release) or SR (sustained-release) tablet dumps the full dose at once — this spikes blood levels and raises seizure risk sharply.

🧠 Extended-release + seizure-risk drug = never crush, ever. The whole point of the SR/XL coating is to prevent exactly the kind of dose spike that could trigger a seizure.

🌙 Trazodone — timing & interactions

  • 💊 Take at night — sedation is expected and useful for sleep
  • Avoid alcohol and other sedatives (benzodiazepines, antihistamines) — additive CNS depression
  • 🧍 Teach slow position changes to reduce orthostatic hypotension/fall risk
  • 🚨 Go to the ED for any erection lasting more than a few hours

🚭 Bupropion — smoking cessation teaching

  • Can be started while the client is still smoking, with a target quit date set during treatment
  • Take earlier in the day — insomnia is common if dosed at night
  • Report new or worsening seizures, tremor, or agitation immediately

🍽️ Mirtazapine — set expectations

  • Take at bedtime — sedation is the point, not a problem to fight
  • Expect increased appetite and possible weight gain — monitor weight over time
  • Avoid driving/operating machinery until the sedating effect is known
🧠 Mirtazapine and trazodone both sedate, but only mirtazapine reliably drives appetite up — that's the differentiator.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A client on trazodone reports an erection lasting several hours with no sexual activity. What should the nurse instruct?
Go to the emergency department immediately — priapism is a urologic emergency.
Q2: Which client would be a poor candidate for bupropion?
A client with a seizure disorder or an active/past eating disorder (anorexia or bulimia) — bupropion lowers the seizure threshold.
Q3: A client is prescribed bupropion XL. Which teaching point is essential?
Never crush, chew, or cut the tablet, and never double up on a missed dose — both spike blood levels and raise seizure risk.
Q4: Which antidepressant would best fit a depressed client with insomnia and poor appetite/weight loss?
Mirtazapine — its sedating and appetite-stimulating side effects directly address both problems.
