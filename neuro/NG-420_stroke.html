🧠 Stroke

Recognise it, tell the two kinds apart, predict what it took away, and keep her safe afterwards. Four questions, and every stroke item on an exam is one of them.

⭐ The one idea

Nothing happens until the brain is imaged. Blocked and bleeding look identical from the doorway and are treated in opposite directions — so the treatment question is never answerable from symptoms alone.

And the thing that decides what she gets offered is when she was last seen normal. Not when she was found. Last known well is the single most useful sentence you can put in a report, and it is the one families most often cannot answer.

Symptoms that resolve still get the full work-up. A TIA leaves no damage and is a warning shot; the risk of a completed stroke is highest in the days right after.

Do not let her drive herself, and do not give food, drink or aspirin before she is assessed. She may not be able to swallow, and if the stroke is a bleed, aspirin makes it worse.

🩸 2 · Blocked or bleeding

🧯 Ischemic — a clot blocks an artery

The commonest kind. Blood flow stops, and the tissue downstream starts to die.

Eligible people may be offered clot-dissolving medicine or thrombectomy — eligibility depends on imaging, timing and clinical criteria, which is exactly why the last known well time matters so much.

🩸 Hemorrhagic — a vessel ruptures

Blood escapes into or around the brain, and the pressure itself becomes the injury.

Care is aimed at controlling the bleeding and the blood pressure, and reversing anticoagulation when that applies. Thrombolytics are not given for a brain hemorrhage.

Which is it? You cannot tell from the bedside, and neither can the provider. Urgent imaging decides the treatment path — that is the whole reason the CT happens before anything is given.

🧠 3 · Side of brain, side of body

The motor pathways cross. So the damage is on one side of the brain and the weakness is on the other side of the body. That crossing is what most of these questions are actually testing.

⬅️ Left brain
  • Often right-sided weakness or sensory loss
  • Language difficulty (aphasia) may occur — trouble finding words, understanding, reading or writing
  • She may be slow, cautious and aware of the deficit, which is its own kind of distress
➡️ Right brain
  • Often left-sided weakness or sensory loss
  • Neglect — she may ignore the left side of space entirely, including her own left arm and the left half of the plate
  • Impulsive, poor safety awareness, may deny anything is wrong

Aphasia is not a loss of intelligence. She understands far more than she can say. Speak to her as an adult, at normal volume, and give her the time to answer.

And these are patterns, not guarantees. Brainstem and cerebellar strokes can look completely different — vertigo, double vision, difficulty swallowing, ataxia — with no classic one-sided weakness at all.

✅ 4 · Afterwards: safety and independence

  • Swallow screen before anything by mouth — food, fluids and medicines. This is the first thing, every time, and it comes before the breakfast tray.
  • Support the weak limbs. Never pull on the affected arm — the shoulder subluxes easily once the muscles cannot hold it. Use the prescribed transfer method.
  • One idea at a time when you speak, then stop and wait. Silence is her processing, not her failing.
  • Approach from her unaffected side at first if she has neglect, then work on teaching her to scan toward the neglected side.
  • Prevent the next one: the prescribed medicines taken, blood pressure managed, smoking stopped, and the other risks addressed.

New or worsening stroke symptoms at any point — call 911. Recovery is not a reason to wait and see.

Rehabilitation goals are individual, and progress can keep coming for a long time. Central axons regrow poorly, but the brain reorganises — limited regrowth does not mean no recovery.

⭐ High yield

  • BE-FAST: balance, eyes, face, arms, speech, time — and last known well, not when she was found
  • Check the glucose. Hypoglycaemia mimics a stroke exactly
  • Urgent brain imaging before treatment — blocked and bleeding are opposite
  • No thrombolytics for a hemorrhagic stroke, and no aspirin before imaging
  • Left brain → right body + aphasia. Right brain → left body + neglect
  • Aphasia = language. Dysarthria = the speech muscles, words intact
  • Swallow screen before food, fluids or medicines. Every time, first
  • Never pull the affected arm
  • Symptoms that resolve are a TIA — still an emergency work-up
