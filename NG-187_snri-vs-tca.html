SNRI vs. TCA

Two older or dual-acting antidepressant classes — one modern & broad, one older & risky

NG-187 PSYCH PHARM ADHD-friendly visual edition

SNRIs block reuptake of two neurotransmitters — serotonin AND norepinephrine — and double as neuropathic pain drugs. TCAs are an older class that also boosts serotonin & norepinephrine, but drags a heavy anticholinergic side-effect profile and a genuinely dangerous overdose behind it. Same neurotransmitter targets, very different safety margins — that's the whole point of this comparison.

🎯 SNRI = dual + painSerotonin AND norepinephrine reuptake blocked — also treats neuropathic/fibromyalgia pain.
💀 TCA = narrow marginCardiotoxic in overdose — QT prolongation, arrhythmia. A small extra dose can kill.
🥴 TCA = anticholinergic"Can't see, can't pee, can't spit, can't poop" — the classic tetrad.
🚨 Serotonin syndromeAgitation, hyperthermia, clonus/hyperreflexia, diaphoresis, tremor — same 5 signs, same risk, both classes.

SIDE A · SNRI

STEP 1 · DUAL & MODERN

Rung 2 of the ladder — same serotonin trick as an SSRI, plus norepinephrine, plus a second job treating chronic pain.

🔬 Blocks TWO reuptake pumps at once

MOA Like an SSRI, an SNRI blocks the serotonin reuptake pump — but it also blocks the norepinephrine reuptake pump, so both neurotransmitters build up in the synapse. That second target is what gives SNRIs their extra job: treating neuropathic pain, not just mood.

🧠 “SNRI = double duty.” One drug, two pumps blocked, two jobs done: mood and pain.

💊 Know the name — Duloxetine

Duloxetine — think "DUAL-oxetine": dual mechanism, dual purpose.

  • 😔 Depression
  • 🔥 Neuropathic pain — diabetic peripheral neuropathy
  • 🦴 Fibromyalgia pain
🧠 "If a fibromyalgia patient on duloxetine says they're not depressed, they need education" — it's still the right drug. It's treating their pain, not (only) their mood.

⭐ Key point — pain AND sleep

Duloxetine helps with chronic pain and improves sleep quality in fibromyalgia patients — a two-for-one benefit worth pointing out in patient teaching.

🧠 SNRIs are often reached for second, after an SSRI trial, or first when chronic pain is part of the picture.
🔵

SIDE B · TCA

STEP 2 · OLDER & RISKIER

Rung 3 of the ladder — an older class that hits more receptors than it needs to, and that's exactly the problem.

🥴 The anticholinergic tetrad — "dry body"

TCAs don't just block serotonin/norepinephrine reuptake — they also block acetylcholine receptors, causing a predictable dry-everything picture.

🧠 "Amy trips on things" — Amitriptyline causes orthostatic hypotension: teach slow position changes to avoid a dizzy fall. Imipramine — "Inhibit my PEEing" = urinary retention is its stand-out anticholinergic effect.

💊 Know the names

  • Amitriptyline — orthostatic hypotension
  • Imipramine — urinary retention

Indications: depression, anxiety, and neuropathic pain (diabetics & fibromyalgia) — TCAs treat pain too, like SNRIs, just with a rougher side-effect trade-off.

🧠 Two drugs, two classic teaching points: Amitriptyline = slow rise. Imipramine = slow stream.

💀 Cardiotoxicity — the reason TCAs fell out of favor

Narrow safety margin — in overdose, TCAs cause QT prolongation and life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias. Unlike SSRIs/SNRIs, a TCA overdose is a genuine cardiac emergency.

🧠 "TCAs can stop a heart before they stop a headache." A small dose miscalculation or an overdose attempt with a narrow-margin drug like this is a true cardiac emergency — assess for arrhythmia, monitor ECG.

TELL THEM APART

STEP 3 · SIDE BY SIDE

Same neurotransmitter targets, very different risk profile — this is the table the "vs." title is asking for.

⚖️ SNRI vs. TCA — head to head

🟣 SNRI🔵 TCA
Mechanism: blocks serotonin + norepinephrine reuptake, selectively Mechanism: blocks serotonin + norepinephrine reuptake, PLUS acetylcholine & histamine receptors
Drug: DuloxetineDrugs: Amitriptyline, Imipramine
Signature side effects: nausea, dry mouth (mild), ↑BP at high doses Signature side effects: full anticholinergic tetrad — can't see, pee, spit, poop
Overdose risk: relatively wide safety margin Overdose risk: narrow margin — cardiotoxic, QT prolongation, arrhythmia
Shared use: neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia Shared use: neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia
Ladder position: often 2nd-line, sometimes 1st Ladder position: older, generally reserved when newer agents fail
🧠 "Same targets, different manners." Both hit serotonin + norepinephrine — the TCA just also grabs receptors it didn't need to, and that's where all its side effects and its overdose danger come from.

🚨 Both interact with MAOIs the same dangerous way

Never combine an SNRI or a TCA with an MAOI — risk of serotonin syndrome. A 2-week washout period is required when switching to or from an MAOI.

🧠 "MAOI = Anti... depressant STOP." Whichever antidepressant a patient is switching away from or onto an MAOI, count two weeks first. See NG-154 for the full MAOI washout rule.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A fibromyalgia patient on duloxetine says they aren't depressed and asks why they're on an antidepressant. What do you teach?
Duloxetine is an SNRI that also treats neuropathic and fibromyalgia pain — it's the right drug for their pain, not only for mood.
Q2: What four things can't a patient do on a TCA?
Can't see (blurred vision), can't pee (urinary retention), can't spit (dry mouth), can't poop (constipation) — the anticholinergic tetrad.
Q3: Why is a TCA overdose more dangerous than an SSRI or SNRI overdose?
TCAs have a narrow safety margin and are cardiotoxic — overdose can cause QT prolongation and life-threatening arrhythmias.
Q4: A patient wants to switch from imipramine to an MAOI. What has to happen first?
A 2-week washout period, to avoid serotonin syndrome from combining serotonergic antidepressant classes.
