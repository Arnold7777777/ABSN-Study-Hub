Cardiomyopathy 🫀

Three different diseases of the heart muscle itself — dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive

NG-197 CARDIO ADHD-friendly visual edition

Cardiomyopathy is disease of the heart muscle itself, and it comes in three shapes: Dilated (a stretched, floppy muscle), Hypertrophic (a huge, thickened muscle that can block outflow), and Restrictive (a rock-hard, stiff muscle that can't relax). All three end the same way — LESS cardiac output, LESS oxygen-rich blood out to the body.

🎈 DilatedDistended, stretched chambers — weak floppy pump, S3, cardiomegaly.
🏆 HypertrophicHuge trophy-like muscle can block the aorta — sudden death in young athletes.
🧱 RestrictiveRock-hard muscle can't refill — emboli are common.
🚫 NO DDD in HCMNo Dilators, Digoxin, Diuretics — they worsen the outflow block.
🧨

CAUSE

STEP 1 · THREE SHAPES, ONE PROBLEM

Same end result — less cardiac output — but the muscle gets there three completely different ways. The shape tells you everything else.

🫀 Heart-shape comparison: how each chamber geometry differs from normal

EXAM TIP All three cardiomyopathies drop cardiac output — but they get a very different-shaped heart to do it.

🧠 "Distended, Trophy, Rock-hard." Dilated = Distended thin balloon. Hypertrophic = Huge trophy-like muscle in the septum. Restrictive = Rock-hard, can't RE-fill. Say the shape, get the disease.

🎈 Dilated cardiomyopathy — "distended heart muscle"

Fibrosis (stiff, hard scarring) of the myocardium & endocardium plus dilated chambers — a stretched, floppy muscle that struggles to squeeze oxygen-rich blood out.

Common causes: ischemic heart disease, viral myocarditis, chronic alcohol use, peripartum, idiopathic.

🧠 A worn-out rubber band stretched too many times — it still moves, but the snap (squeeze) is gone.

🏆 Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy — "huge trophy-like muscle"

The muscle — especially the septum — grows huge and thick, which can obstruct the aortic valve/outflow tract, blocking oxygenated blood from reaching the body.

ObstructiveNon-obstructive
Blocks the aortic valve outflowDoes not block outflow
🧠 Mostly genetic (autosomal dominant) — the #1 reason to screen family members of a young sudden cardiac death victim.

🧱 Restrictive cardiomyopathy — "rock-hard muscle"

The heart muscle becomes stiff and can't RE-fill during diastole — REstrictive. Emboli (blood clots) are common because blood stagnates in a chamber that won't relax and empty well.

Common causes: amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, hemochromatosis, endomyocardial fibrosis.

🧠 A rock doesn't stretch to catch water — a stiff ventricle doesn't stretch to catch returning blood.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT IT

A murmur that breaks the normal rules, a lab that grades how bad the failure is, and the sign that makes hypertrophic the deadliest of the three.

🚨 Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy — sudden cardiac death in young athletes

Typically asymptomatic — no s/s — until heavy exercise, when the thickened septum obstructs outflow during high demand and the client can die suddenly. This is why HCM is the classic cause of sudden collapse in young, seemingly healthy athletes.

🧠 "Fine until the finish line." A perfectly healthy-looking teen athlete who collapses during a game is the textbook HCM story — screen family history before it happens, not after.

🔊 The murmur that breaks the rules — louder with Valsalva/standing

Most murmurs get quieter when preload drops (Valsalva strain, standing) because there's less blood to make noise. HCM is the opposite: less blood in the ventricle lets the thick septum obstruct the outflow tract more, so the murmur gets LOUDER. Squatting or lying down (more preload) makes it softer.

🧠 "HCM plays by opposite rules." If a murmur gets LOUDER with Valsalva or standing, and softer with squatting/lying flat — that's hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, not a normal flow murmur.

🧪 BNP — grades how bad the failure is

B-type (brain) natriuretic peptide is released when ventricles stretch/strain — the higher it climbs, the worse the heart failure.

🧠 Bigger number = bigger stretch. BNP is the heart shouting how hard it's straining to keep up.

📉 General signs across all three

  • 😮‍💨 Low oxygen, fatigue, dizziness, syncope
  • 😵 Restlessness, agitation, altered LOC (poor perfusion)
  • 🫁 Left-sided failure → lung fluid (crackles)
  • 💧 Right-sided failure → edema, ascites, JVD
  • 🔊 New murmur (often S3), cardiomegaly
🧠 Same heart-failure playbook you already know — cardiomyopathy is just the muscle problem causing it.

🧪 Diagnostics that separate the three

  • 📷 Chest X-ray & MRI — chamber size & wall thickness
  • 🫀 Echocardiogram — measures ejection fraction (EF): 55–70% normal, ≤40% = heart failure range
  • 🩻 Angiography — highlights coronary arteries, rules out ischemic heart disease as the cause
🧠 EF tells you the pump strength; angiography tells you why. Rule out a blocked artery before calling it a primary cardiomyopathy.

CARE

STEP 3 · PROTECT THE PUMP

Pharmacology flips for hypertrophic — know the "NO DDD" rule cold, and check three numbers before every digoxin dose.

💊 Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy pharmacology

✅ Give — reduce contractility, slow HR, let it fill❌ NO DDD — worsens the obstruction
Beta blockersDilators
Calcium channel blockersDigoxin
Diuretics
🧠 "BC, no DDD." Beta blockers & Calcium channel blockers slow the heart and give it more time to fill around the obstruction. Dilators, Digoxin, and Diuretics all shrink ventricular volume or boost contractility — both make the outflow obstruction worse.

🚨 Digoxin — hold if any of these three

1
💓 Apical pulse below 60 bpm
2
🧪 Potassium (K+) below 3.5 — raises risk of dig toxicity
3
🧪 Digoxin level over 2.0 — toxic

Toxicity signs: vision changes (halos, yellow-green tint), nausea/vomiting, fatigue, dizziness.

🧠 "Pulse, Potassium, Peak level" — the 3 P's to check before you push dig. Any one abnormal = hold and notify.

✅ Nursing interventions

  • NO strenuous activity — especially HCM, can worsen aortic outflow blockage
  • ⚖️ Daily weights, strict I&O for fluid status
  • 🧂 Sodium/fluid restriction as ordered for HF symptoms
  • 🚨 Teach family of HCM clients: screening & sudden death risk with exertion
🧠 "Rest the trophy." A hypertrophied heart already fights its own outflow tract — heavy exertion is the thing most likely to push it over the edge.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Which cardiomyopathy is the classic cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes, and why?
Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy — the thickened septum can obstruct the aortic outflow tract, especially during heavy exertion, causing sudden collapse in someone who was often asymptomatic.
Q2: What happens to the HCM murmur with Valsalva or standing, and why is that the opposite of most murmurs?
It gets LOUDER. Less ventricular volume lets the thick septum obstruct the outflow tract more. Most other murmurs get softer with less blood volume.
Q3: What three numbers must be checked before giving digoxin?
Apical pulse (hold if <60), potassium (hold/notify if <3.5, raises toxicity risk), and digoxin level (hold if >2.0, toxic).
Q4: What does "NO DDD" mean for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy?
Avoid Dilators, Digoxin, and Diuretics — they reduce ventricular volume or increase contractility, both of which worsen the outflow obstruction.
