Course overview

ADHD — THREE PRESENTATIONS, ONE DIAGNOSIS

  • It is one disorder that shows up three ways. What you monitor is the same either way.

🚨 A sudden drop off the growth curve, or a resting heart rate that keeps climbing, is reported before the next dose.

THE THREE PRESENTATIONS
  • Inattentive (was “ADD”): Loses things, does not finish, looks like daydreaming
  • Hyperactive–impulsive: Cannot stay seated, blurts, cannot wait a turn
  • Combined (both): most common
⭐ WHAT MAKES IT A DIAGNOSIS
  • 6+ symptoms, under age 17
  • 5+ symptoms, 17 and older
  • 6 months or longer
  • Before 12: some symptoms present
  • 2 settings: home AND school — not just one

💡 Two settings is the one students miss.

💊 STIMULANTS — WHAT YOU MONITOR
  • Methylphenidate, amphetamine salts. Schedule II.
  • Give after breakfast, last dose before 4 pm — or no sleep.
  • Appetite suppression: weigh, and plot height every visit.
  • Check heart rate and BP at each visit.
  • Atomoxetine is the non-stimulant — watch mood, suicidality.
  • Drug holidays only if the prescriber directs them.
🏫 WHAT ACTUALLY HELPS
  • One instruction at a time.
  • Written routine she can see.
  • Seat near the teacher, away from windows.
  • Break long tasks into short pieces.
  • Praise the effort, not just the result.
  • Medication + therapy beats either alone.
