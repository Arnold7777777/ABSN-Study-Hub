Spinal cord injury

Course labels and notes from the original graphic, arranged into readable sections. The illustration is schematic.

Course overview

FIND THE LEVEL, THEN LOOK DOWNWARD

  • Everything below the injury is affected. Everything above it is normal. Two numbers do most of the work.
📏 THE LEVELS WORTH MEMORISING

C1–C4

  • all four limbs, and the DIAPHRAGM
  • C3, 4 and 5 keep the diaphragm alive. At or above C4 she cannot breathe unaided — ventilator.

C5–C8

  • tetraplegia, with some arm function
  • The diaphragm works, but the cough is weak. Watch for retained secretions.

T1–T6

  • paraplegia; poor trunk control
  • At or above T6, autonomic dysreflexia becomes possible.
  • T6–T12
  • paraplegia, better trunk control
  • Independent wheelchair use is realistic.

L1–S5

  • leg weakness; bowel, bladder, sexual function
  • Bracing and walking may be possible.
  • C4 — above this she cannot breathe — VENTILATOR
  • T6 — at or above this, AUTONOMIC DYSREFLEXIA
  • If a question asks what she can do, find the level and read downward. That is nearly always the whole method.
🌀 THE TWO SHOCKS THAT GET CONFUSED

🌀 THE TWO SHOCKS THAT GET CONFUSED

SPINAL SHOCK · NEUROGENIC SHOCK

What it is

SPINAL SHOCK
a temporary loss of ALL cord function below the injury
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
loss of sympathetic tone below the injury

When

SPINAL SHOCK
immediately, and it lasts days to weeks
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
immediately, with injuries at T6 and above

Reflexes

SPINAL SHOCK
ABSENT — flaccid, areflexic
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
may return

Blood pressure

SPINAL SHOCK
usually normal
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
HYPOTENSION

Pulse

SPINAL SHOCK
usually normal
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
BRADYCARDIA — this is the giveaway

Skin

SPINAL SHOCK
unremarkable
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
warm, dry, flushed below the injury

Treatment

SPINAL SHOCK
supportive; wait for it to resolve
NEUROGENIC SHOCK
fluids, vasopressors, atropine for the slow rate

Hypotension WITH bradycardia is the pairing. Every other shock gives hypotension with a FAST heart rate.
