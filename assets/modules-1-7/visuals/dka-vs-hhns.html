Course overview
  • DKA vs HHNS — SAME EMERGENCY, TWO DIFFERENT PICTURES
  • Both are dangerously high glucose. Ketones and pH are what tell them apart.

⭐ THE TWO THAT DECIDE IT — ketones and pH

💡 Acidosis with ketones is DKA. A normal pH and no ketones, however high the glucose, is HHNS.

🧪 DKA — type 1, ketones, acidosis
  • No insulin at all, so fat is burned for fuel.

Labs

  • Glucose >250 mg/dL: high
  • pH <7.35: ACIDIC
  • Bicarbonate <18: low
  • Ketones: PRESENT

What you see

  • Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, abdominal pain.
  • Hours to develop. Often the first sign of type 1.
🌊 HHNS — type 2, no ketones, dehydration
  • Some insulin left, so fat is not burned. Just water loss.

Labs

  • Glucose >600 mg/dL: extreme
  • pH normal: normal
  • Bicarbonate normal: normal
  • Ketones: ABSENT

What you see

  • Profound dehydration, altered mental status, seizures.
  • Days to develop. Older adults. Higher mortality.
✅ TREATMENT — SAME ORDER FOR BOTH
  • 1. FLUIDS first: 0.9% saline. Volume, not sugar, is what kills.
  • 2. Insulin drip: Regular insulin, IV only.
  • 3. POTASSIUM: Insulin drives K+ in, so it will fall.
  • 4. Add dextrose: At a glucose of 250, to avoid a crash.
