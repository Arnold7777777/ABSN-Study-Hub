THE FAMILY

STEP 1 · MEET EVERY PLAYER

Four ways to fight acid — neutralize it, block its release two different ways, or coat the wound so it can't reach it.

🫙 Four strategies, one stomach

🧠 “Neutralize, Block-A, Block-B, Coat.” Antacid neutralizes acid already made · H2 blocker & PPI both reduce production (different mechanisms) · mucosal protectant coats the ulcer and never touches acid levels at all.

🧂 Antacids — immediate, temporary

  • Sodium bicarbonate (Alka-Seltzer)
  • Calcium carbonate (Tums, Rolaids)
  • Aluminum hydroxide
  • Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia)

Immediately neutralizes acid — but ONLY temporary, not long-lasting.

💩 Antacid cation memory trick

🪨Aluminumconstipation
🦴Calciumconstipation
💧Magnesiumdiarrhea
🎈Sodiumfluid retention — avoid in HF
🧠 “Al & Ca CLOG, Mg goes.” Aluminum and Calcium clog you up (constipation) — Magnesium is mellow and moves things along (diarrhea). Sodium swells — never for a heart-failure patient.

STEP 3 · HESI-STYLE TRAPS

Every acid drug shares the same spacing rule — the traps are in the details that are different.

🚨 HESI-style trap: sodium bicarbonate + heart failure

NOT for heart failure patients — sodium bicarbonate antacid adds a sodium load that promotes fluid retention. Sodium = swells.

🕐 Universal spacing rule

Antacids can interfere with the absorption of other oral drugs. Separate any other medication by 1 hour before or after an antacid — this rule repeats across every acid-drug page in this family.

🧠 “Anti-Acid = Anti-Mixing.” The whole point of the name doubles as the safety rule: don't mix it with other meds too closely in time.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Rank the three acid-suppressing classes from fastest onset to slowest.
Antacid (minutes) → H2 blocker (~30–60 min) → PPI (slowest, days for full effect).
Q2: Rank them from lowest potency to highest.
Antacid (lowest) → H2 blocker (moderate) → PPI (highest).
Q3: Which antacid ingredient is contraindicated in heart failure, and why?
Sodium bicarbonate — the sodium load promotes fluid retention.
Q4: What's the one rule that applies to every drug in this family?
Space it 1–2 hours apart from other oral medications, especially antacids, which can interfere with absorption.
