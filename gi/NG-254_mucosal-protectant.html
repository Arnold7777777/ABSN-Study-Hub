WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · BUILD A SHIELD

Two drugs, two completely different jobs — one coats the wound, the other rebuilds the mucus that protects it.

🩹 Sucralfate — a bandage that needs acid to stick

EXAM TIP Sucralfate (Carafate) is given to treat and prevent stomach and duodenal ulcers. It forms a thick, sticky paste that physically coats the ulcer crater, shielding it from stomach acid and enzymes — it does not neutralize or reduce acid.

🧠 “No acid, no armor.” Sucralfate is the opposite of every other drug on this page — it actually needs stomach acid around to turn into its sticky, protective form. Raise the pH too early (antacid, H2 blocker, PPI) and it never activates.

🌸 Misoprostol — replaces what NSAIDs destroy

A synthetic prostaglandin that increases the protective mucous layer inside the stomach. NSAIDs work by blocking prostaglandins — which is exactly why chronic NSAID use erodes the stomach lining. Misoprostol replaces that missing prostaglandin.

🧠 “Miso replaces what NSAIDs erase.” Given specifically to prevent NSAID-induced gastric ulcers in high-risk, long-term NSAID users.

✅ Indications, both drugs

  • Sucralfate — treat & prevent gastric and duodenal ulcers
  • Misoprostol — prevent NSAID-associated gastric ulcers
🧠 Neither drug is a first-line therapy on its own for most patients — they're used alongside acid reducers or specifically for NSAID-risk prevention.
⚠️

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · SAFETY

Sucralfate's biggest risk is a missed dose window. Misoprostol's biggest risk is a missed pregnancy test.

😐 Sucralfate — adverse effects & cautions

  • 💩 Constipation — most common (aluminum-containing salt)
  • 🩺 Caution in renal impairment — aluminum can accumulate with reduced kidney clearance

🚨 Misoprostol — major adverse effect: MISCARRIAGE RISK

Reliable birth control is required for anyone of childbearing potential taking misoprostol.

🧠 “Miso starts labor — that's the danger.” The exact reason misoprostol is used in obstetrics for cervical ripening/labor induction is the exact reason it's dangerous in an unintended pregnancy — same drug, same mechanism.

TEACH

STEP 3 · TIMING IS THE WHOLE LESSON

Get the clock right and this drug class works exactly as designed.

⏱️ Sucralfate timing — the whole teaching point

🧠 “Taken LATE, SucralfATE.” Give it on an empty stomach — roughly 1 hour before meals and at bedtime — and keep a 2-hour buffer from antacids or other meds so nothing raises the pH too soon.

🩺 Nursing management — sucralfate

  • 💧 Give with a full glass of water
  • 📋 Monitor bowel pattern — watch for constipation
  • 🩺 Use cautiously with renal impairment
  • 🕐 Do not give within 2 hr of an antacid or another acid-changing drug

🚫 Nursing management — misoprostol

  • 🚨 Confirm negative pregnancy test before starting in a person who could become pregnant
  • 💊 Ensure reliable contraception is in place throughout therapy
  • 🕐 Space away from antacids
  • 🗣️ Teach the patient exactly why — cervical ripening & uterine contractions

🔗 See it in context — the acid-reducer family

Mucosal protectants are the odd one out on the potency ladder — they don't reduce acid at all, so they don't compete for "strongest" or "fastest." They're a completely separate strategy: physically shielding the wound while the acid-reducing drugs do the acid-lowering work.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Why must sucralfate be given on an empty stomach, separate from antacids?
Sucralfate needs an acidic environment to activate and form its protective paste. An antacid (or H2 blocker/PPI given too close in time) raises stomach pH and can prevent it from binding to the ulcer.
Q2: How does misoprostol protect the stomach?
It's a synthetic prostaglandin that replaces the protective mucous-stimulating prostaglandins that NSAIDs block — used to prevent NSAID-associated gastric ulcers.
Q3: What is the major, absolute safety concern with misoprostol?
Miscarriage risk from cervical ripening and uterine contractions — a negative pregnancy test and reliable birth control are required.
Q4: What's the mnemonic for sucralfate timing?
"Taken LATE & SucralfATE" — give about 1 hour before meals and at bedtime, on an empty stomach, with a 2-hour gap from other acid-changing drugs.
