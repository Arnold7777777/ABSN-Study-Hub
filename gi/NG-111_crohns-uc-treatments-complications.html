COMPLICATIONS

STEP 1 · WHAT GOES WRONG

Crohn's digs tunnels through the wall. UC balloons the colon out. Both roads can end in peritonitis.

⚖️ Crohn's vs UC — the complication & cure table

👑 Crohn's🩸 UC
Location 📍Mouth to anus, skip lesions, mainly small intestineColon & rectum only, continuous
Depth 🔬Transmural — through every layerMucosal — surface layer only
Signature complicationFistulas, strictures, abscesses, obstructionToxic megacolon, severe bleeding
Cancer riskIncreased, but lower than UCHigher colon cancer risk with long-standing disease
Surgical cure? 🔪NOT curative — disease can recur anywhere in the GI tractColectomy IS curative — removing the colon removes the disease
🧠 "You can cut UC out — you can't cut Crohn's out." UC lives only in the colon, so removing the colon removes the disease. Crohn's can pop up anywhere from mouth to anus, so surgery only treats the current complication, not the disease itself.

🕳️ Crohn's: tunnels through the wall

Deep, transmural inflammation burrows all the way through the bowel wall, creating:

  • 🕳️ Fistulas — abnormal tunnels connecting bowel to bladder, vagina, skin, or other bowel loops
  • 🪢 Strictures — scarred, narrowed segments → obstruction
  • 🫙 Abscesses — pockets of infection
🧠 Deep = damage that lasts. Transmural inflammation means Crohn's leaves permanent structural changes — strictures and fistulas don't heal shut on their own.

🎈 UC: the colon balloons — toxic megacolon

Toxic megacolon — severe inflammation paralyzes colon motility, the colon distends massively, and the wall can rupture, spilling bowel contents and causing peritonitis — a deadly infection of the peritoneal cavity.

🧠 Toxic megacolon = UC's emergency. Fistulas/strictures are Crohn's signature; toxic megacolon → rupture → peritonitis is UC's.

PHARMACOLOGY

STEP 2 · CLIMB THE LADDER

Treatment steps up in intensity as disease gets harder to control — cheapest/safest drugs first.

🪜 The treatment ladder

1
🧴 Aminosalicylates (5-ASA): Sulfasalazine — "STOPS the body attacking itself" — first-line, especially for UC
2
🔥 Corticosteroids: Prednisone — "soothes the swelling" — induces remission fast, not for long-term use
3
🧬 Immunomodulators: azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine — maintain remission
4
🎯 Biologics (anti-TNF): infliximab, adalimumab — for moderate–severe disease; screen for TB/infection first
5
🔪 Surgery — for complications or disease that won't respond
🧠 "Soothe, then Stop, then Suppress, then Surgery." Steroids soothe symptoms short-term, 5-ASA/immunomodulators stop the ongoing attack, biologics suppress the immune trigger, surgery is the last step.

💊 Symptom-control drugs

DrugMemory clue
Loperamide (Imodium)"LOW bowel movements"
Dicyclomine"Dry Cycle" — antispasmodic, dries up cramping/spasm
🧠 Caution: antidiarrheals can be risky during a severe flare — slowing the bowel can contribute to toxic megacolon. Give as prescribed and monitor closely.

🔪 Surgery — resection with ostomy

Most clients get a colostomy or ileostomy after a bowel resection, where the diseased segment of bowel is removed.

🧠 Surgery in Crohn's treats the complication in front of you (a stricture, fistula, abscess) — it does not remove the disease.

CARE

STEP 3 · SUPPORT THE WHOLE PERSON

Fluid & electrolytes, nutrition, and psychosocial support — the day-to-day nursing that keeps a flare from becoming an emergency.

💧 Fluid & electrolyte monitoring

  • 📋 Strict I&O monitoring — closely, every shift
  • 💧 Encourage fluids — roughly 2 L/day, more with diarrhea to prevent dehydration
  • 🧪 Hypokalemia — K⁺ 3.5 mEq/L or less — from ongoing diarrhea, monitor for weakness & dysrhythmias
  • 💊 Daily multivitamin containing calcium — malabsorption + steroid use raise osteoporosis risk
Low K⁺3.5 mEq/LNormal5.0High K⁺
🧠 Diarrhea drains potassium. Any IBD client with active diarrhea is a hypokalemia risk until proven otherwise — check the K⁺.

🍽️ Diet

  • 🥩 High protein & high calorie — healing & malabsorption needs
  • 🥣 Low fiber during flares — rest the irritated bowel
  • 🍽️ Small, frequent meals
  • 📓 Keep a food journal to spot personal triggers
  • ❌ Avoid alcohol; reduce caffeine (coffee, tea) — both irritate the gut
🧠 "High protein, low fiber, small & often." Feed the healing, rest the lining.

💊 Comfort & psychosocial care

  • 💊 Give analgesics as prescribed for pain
  • 🧠 Encourage clients to discuss feelings — chronic disease, body image with an ostomy
  • 😌 Stress reduction — stress is a known flare trigger (see NG-030)
🧠 IBD is chronic and relapsing — psychosocial support is not optional extra care, it's core to preventing the next flare.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Which disease is cured by a colectomy, and why?
Ulcerative colitis — disease is confined to the colon, so removing the colon removes the disease. Crohn's can recur anywhere in the GI tract, so surgery is not curative.
Q2: Name Crohn's signature complications versus UC's signature complication.
Crohn's: fistulas, strictures, abscesses (transmural damage). UC: toxic megacolon (can rupture → peritonitis).
Q3: What's the first-line drug class for IBD, and its memory clue?
Aminosalicylates (5-ASA), e.g. sulfasalazine — "stops the body attacking itself."
Q4: Why are IBD clients with diarrhea at risk for hypokalemia?
Ongoing diarrhea drains potassium — monitor K⁺ (concern at 3.5 mEq/L or less) for weakness and cardiac dysrhythmias.
