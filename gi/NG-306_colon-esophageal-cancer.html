Colon & Esophageal Cancer 🎗️

Risk Factors, Screening & Early Clues

NG-306 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

Colorectal cancer = cancer of the colon (large intestine) & rectum, the "exit door" of the GI tract. Esophageal cancer = cancer of the "food tube" carrying food from mouth to stomach. Both share the same three modifiable risk factors — Obesity, Alcohol, Tobacco — and both are caught early with screening, not waiting for symptoms.

🔤 "OAT" risk factorsObesity · Alcohol · Tobacco — shared by both cancers.
🔥 GERD → Barrett's → cancerChronic untreated reflux is the esophageal cancer pathway.
🔭 Colonoscopy at 45Average-risk screening starts age 45, repeat every 10 years.
📉 Change in bowel habits + low H&H= colonoscopy is the expected diagnostic test.
🧨

RISK FACTORS

STEP 1 · WHO'S AT RISK

Two different organs, the same three lifestyle risks, plus organ-specific ones.

🗺️ Where each cancer lives

🔤 "OAT" — shared risk factors

  • O — Obesity (BMI over 30)
  • A — Alcohol
  • T — Tobacco (cigarettes, cigars, chewing tobacco)
🧠 "Eat your OATs — or don't." Obesity, Alcohol, Tobacco raise the risk of BOTH colorectal and esophageal cancer — and they're the same three things you teach the client to change.

⚠️ Colorectal cancer — extra risks

  • 🕳️ Diverticulosis
  • 🔥 Ulcerative colitis (long-term, chronic inflammation)
  • 🧬 Family history of colorectal cancer
🧠 Long-standing colon inflammation (UC, Crohn's) + family history = earlier & more frequent screening than average risk. Most colorectal cancer follows this polyp → cancer sequence over years — which is exactly why screening catches it early.

⚠️ Esophageal cancer — extra risk

  • 🔥 Chronic GERD / acid reflux — the #1 organ-specific risk
  • 📈 Untreated reflux → esophagitis → Barrett's esophagus → cancer
🧠 Barrett's = the "burnt" esophagus. It's a precancerous change (normal cells replaced by intestinal-type cells) — the reason GERD is treated seriously, not just as heartburn.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT IT EARLY

Early cancer is often silent — these are the clues that finally show up, plus the exact exam scenario.

🔤 "ABC" — colorectal cancer clues

  • A — Abdominal pain
  • B — Bleeding: blood in stools, low hemoglobin
  • C — Change in bowel habits

Unexplained weight loss is also a major red-flag clue for both cancers.

🧠 "ABC — and don't forget the C" (weight loss). Any client with new bowel habit changes + unexplained weight loss + falling H&H needs a colonoscopy, not reassurance.

🖼️ Esophageal cancer symptoms

  • 🍽️ Dysphagia — progressive difficulty swallowing (solids → liquids)
  • ⚖️ Unexplained weight loss
  • 😖 Chest/retrosternal pain
  • 🍽️ Odynophagia — painful swallowing
🧠 Esophageal cancer symptoms often start subtle and progress — "food gets stuck" is the classic early complaint.

🔬 Common exam question

Client with a family history of colorectal cancer, unexplained weight loss, change in bowel habits, and Hgb of 9 (down from 14 just 12 months earlier) — which diagnostic test is expected?

Correct answer: Colonoscopy

🧠 A dropping H&H over months + bowel habit change + family history = colon cancer workup, and the test is always the scope.

🚨 Post-colonoscopy complication — perforation is an emergency

🌡️Fever 
💓Tachycardia 
💨Tachypnea 
🪵Rigid, board-like abdomen= distension

A perforated bowel spills contents into the peritoneum → peritonitis, a medical emergency.

🧠 Any procedure that goes through bowel wall (colonoscopy, EGD) carries the same perforation red flags — memorize this cluster once, apply it everywhere.
🩺

SCREENING & CARE

STEP 3 · CATCH IT EARLY, MODIFY THE RISK

Screening intervals, bowel prep, and the same three lifestyle changes that lower risk for both cancers.

🔭 Colonoscopy screening — who, when

Risk categoryScreening plan
Average riskStart age 45, repeat every 10 years if normal
Family history of colorectal cancerStart earlier & screen more often — provider individualizes timing
Ulcerative colitis / Crohn's (long-standing)More frequent surveillance colonoscopy per GI provider
🧠 "45 and alive, every decade you thrive" — average-risk screening starts at 45, repeats every 10 years if results are normal.

🥤 Before the colonoscopy

  • 🥤 Clear liquid diet + laxative bowel prep — e.g., polyethylene glycol (brand: GoLYTELY)
  • 🌙 NPO after midnight
🧠 A clean colon = a scope that actually sees the polyp. Bowel prep compliance is the #1 reason a screening exam fails.

🔭 What the scope is used for

Passed up through the rectum to inspect the colon for tumors, polyps, and to evaluate other GI conditions like ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease.

🧠 It's both a screening tool AND a treatment tool — polyps can be removed (polypectomy) during the same procedure.

✅ Modifiable risk factors — patient teaching

O
Obesity → lose weight — BMI goal < 30
A
Alcohol → stop
T
Tobacco → smoking cessation

High-fiber diet (e.g., cabbage, whole grains) also supports colon health and regular bowel elimination.

🧠 Same "OAT" letters from risk factors become the teaching plan — reduce the exact same three things that raised the risk.
