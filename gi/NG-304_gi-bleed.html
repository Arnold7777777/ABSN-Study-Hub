GI Bleed 🩸

Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage — Upper vs. Lower

NG-304 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

Bleeding anywhere inside the GI tract — the whole job is figuring out WHERE. Split the tube at the ligament of Treitz: bleed above it is an upper GI bleed, bleed below it is a lower GI bleed. Enough blood loss fast enough → hypovolemic (hemorrhagic) shock.

CAUSE

STEP 1 · FIND THE TUBE, PICK A SIDE

One landmark splits the whole GI tract into "upper" and "lower" — everything else follows from that split.

🗺️ The ligament of Treitz is the dividing line

DEFINITION A GI bleed is bleeding anywhere inside the GI tract, classified as upper or lower — one of the biggest jobs is figuring out the exact location because it changes every intervention that follows (NGT? scope type? drug?).

🧠 "Treitz splits the tube." Above the ligament of Treitz = upper (mouth-side). Below it = lower (anus-side). Everything about a GI bleed — the symptom, the scope, the NGT rule — depends on which side.

⚠️ Upper GI bleed — causes

  • 🔥 Gastritis
  • 🚪 GERD / esophagitis
  • 🕳️ Peptic ulcer disease — most common cause
  • 🩸 Esophageal varices — from portal hypertension / cirrhosis, can rupture & exsanguinate fast
  • 🤮 Mallory-Weiss tear — forceful vomiting/retching
🧠 "GGPV" — Gastritis · GERD · Peptic ulcer · Varices. The V is the scary one — varices bleed the fastest and hardest.

⚠️ Lower GI bleed — causes

  • 🩹 Hemorrhoids — most common lower cause
  • 🎗️ Colorectal cancer
  • 🕳️ Diverticulosis
  • 🔥 Ulcerative colitis
  • 🌸 Angiodysplasia (older adults)
🧠 "HeCDU" — Hemorrhoids · Cancer · Diverticulosis · Ulcerative colitis.

🚨 RED FLAG — call the provider now (hemorrhagic shock)

Any GI bleed can outrun the body's ability to compensate. These signs mean the client is decompensating — report immediately:

🧠 "Cool, pale, fast, low" — cool clammy skin + pale + fast HR + low BP is the classic hemorrhagic shock picture. Don't wait for BP to crash — tachycardia is the FIRST compensatory clue.

CARE

STEP 3 · STOP THE BLEED, REPLACE THE VOLUME

Stabilize first, scope second, feed last.

✅ Priority nursing interventions

1
Lower the head of the bed — improves perfusion to vital organs
2
IV normal saline — 2 large-bore IVs, stabilizes blood pressure
3
Oxygen
4
Blood transfusion — when Hgb < 7, type & crossmatch PRBCs
🧠 "LOB" — Lower HOB · Oxygen & IV fluids · Blood if Hgb < 7. Also: keep the client NPO in case of emergency endoscopy.

❌ NGT lavage — upper GI only, one huge exception

Nasogastric lavage (NGT) can be used to assess/clear an upper GI bleed.

NEVER insert an NGT if esophageal varices are known or suspected — the tube can rupture the fragile varix and cause catastrophic bleeding.

🧠 "Varices + tube = trouble." If the chart says cirrhosis or varices, the NGT answer on the exam is always "no."

💊 Drugs that stop a variceal bleed

  • 💧 Octreotide — reduces splanchnic blood flow & portal pressure
  • 💊 IV PPI (e.g., pantoprazole) — reduces acid, protects a healing ulcer
  • 🩸 Blood products — PRBCs, FFP, platelets as ordered
🧠 Octreotide = "shrink the plumbing." It squeezes down splanchnic vessels so varices bleed less.

🔪 Surgery / procedures — find it & fix it

  • 🔭 Endoscopy (EGD) — upper GI: cauterize, clip, or band a bleeding varix/ulcer
  • 🔭 Colonoscopy — lower GI: locate & treat the source
  • 🩹 Angiography / embolization if bleeding can't be localized or controlled by scope
🧠 "Scope up = upper, scope down = lower." EGD goes in the mouth; colonoscopy goes in the rectum.

🍽️ After the "-scopy" — diet progression

Client stays NPO before the procedure, then advances slowly once bleeding is controlled and gag reflex has returned:

🍎Apple juiceclear liquid
🍲Broth soupclear liquid
🍵Unsweetened teaclear liquid

Advance diet as tolerated — clear liquids → full liquids → soft → regular, watching for return of nausea, pain, or bleeding.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Client has coffee-ground emesis and black tarry stools. Upper or lower GI bleed?
Upper — hematemesis/coffee-ground emesis and melena are both upper GI bleed clues.
Q2: What lab value triggers a blood transfusion?
Hemoglobin less than 7 g/dL — "Hgb less than 7 = heaven."
Q3: Client with known esophageal varices needs gastric lavage. What do you do?
Do NOT insert an NGT — it can rupture the varix. Notify the provider instead.
Q4: HR 128, BP 84/52, skin cool and clammy. What's happening and what's priority?
Hypovolemic (hemorrhagic) shock. Priority: lower HOB, 2 large-bore IVs with normal saline, oxygen, notify provider, prepare for possible transfusion.
