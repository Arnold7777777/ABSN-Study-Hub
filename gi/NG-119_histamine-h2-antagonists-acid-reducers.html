H2 Antagonists 🚫

Histamine H2 Blockers — Acid Reducers

NG-119 GI · Pharmacology ADHD-friendly visual edition

H2 blockers reversibly block histamine at the H2 receptor on the parietal cell — less potent than a PPI but faster to kick in and generally a safer long-term profile. Pairs with PPIs (NG-140), the master cheat-sheet on Acid Prevention (NG-233), and Mucosal Protectants (NG-254).

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · BLOCK THE RECEPTOR

One receptor, one competitive blocker — histamine can't dock, so the pump never gets the "turn on" signal.

🔑 Mechanism — histamine can't find its lock

EXAM TIP H2 antagonists reduce gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the action of histamine at H2 receptor cells on the parietal cell. Unlike a PPI, this is a reversible, competitive block — one receptor, not the whole downstream pump.

🧠 “Parking spot, not a wrecking ball.” An H2 blocker just occupies the parking spot (the H2 receptor) so histamine can't pull in — it doesn't destroy the pump itself, which is why the effect wears off faster and is weaker than a PPI.

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · SAFETY

Cimetidine is the interaction-prone troublemaker of this family — know it by name.

😐 Adverse effects

  • 💫 Dizziness, somnolence
  • 🤕 Headache
  • 🌀 Confusion, hallucinations — especially older adults
  • 💩 Diarrhea
  • 🚻 Reversible impotence (cimetidine specifically — antiandrogenic effect)
🧠 “Confused Grandpa on Tagamet.” Cimetidine in an older adult = watch for confusion/CNS changes before anything else.

⚠️ Cautions & contraindications

  • 🚫 Hypersensitivity
  • 🩺 Renal or hepatic impairment — use cautiously
  • 👴 Severely ill, older, or debilitated patients
  • 🍬 Cimetidine — use cautiously in patients with diabetes
  • 🤰 Pregnancy category B — cimetidine, famotidine, ranitidine; category C — nizatidine

🔀 Drug interactions — cimetidine is the big one

Extra caution with cimetidine: it is a potent inhibitor of hepatic CYP450 enzymes, so it can raise blood levels of many other drugs cleared through the liver — one more reason famotidine is preferred when a choice is available.

🧠 “Cimetidine gums up the liver's traffic.” If a question pairs an H2 blocker with a weird interaction, it's almost always cimetidine behind it.

TEACH

STEP 3 · PATIENT & NURSING TEACHING

Same spacing rules as every acid drug — plus know that OTC availability changes the teaching conversation.

🛒 OTC vs. prescription strength

Lower-dose H2 blockers (e.g., famotidine 10–20 mg) are sold over the counter for occasional heartburn. Teach patients that OTC use is for short-term, occasional symptoms — persistent or worsening heartburn needs a provider evaluation, not just a bigger OTC dose.

🕐 Spacing from antacids

Because an antacid can interfere with absorption of other oral drugs, give any other oral medication — including an H2 blocker — 1–2 hours apart from an antacid dose.

🩺 Nursing management checklist

1
⏱️ IV push/infusion: monitor rate closely — too rapid an infusion may induce cardiac arrhythmias
2
📋 Track bowel movements — may cause constipation or diarrhea
3
💧 Watch for dehydration — poor skin turgor, dry mucous membranes, decreased urine output, restlessness, confusion
4
🥛 Chew tablets thoroughly, then drink a full glass of water or milk
5
👴 Older adults: reassess mental status after starting — cimetidine especially

🔗 See it in context — the acid-reducer family

H2 blockers sit in the middle of the potency ladder: stronger and longer-lasting than an antacid, weaker than a PPI, and — unlike sucralfate — they actually reduce acid rather than just coating the ulcer.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: How does an H2 blocker reduce acid, and how is that different from a PPI?
It reversibly blocks histamine at the H2 receptor on the parietal cell so the pump never gets the "turn on" signal — a PPI instead irreversibly disables the pump itself, which is why PPIs are stronger.
Q2: What suffix identifies this drug class?
-tidine — cimetidine, famotidine, ranitidine.
Q3: Which H2 blocker carries the most drug-interaction risk, and why?
Cimetidine — it's a potent CYP450 inhibitor and can cause confusion in older adults.
Q4: How long should an H2 blocker be spaced from an antacid dose?
1–2 hours apart, because the antacid can interfere with absorption of the other oral drug.
