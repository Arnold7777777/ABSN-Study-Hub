Celiac Disease 🌾

Autoimmune Reaction to Gluten

NG-300 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

An autoimmune reaction to gluten (the protein in wheat, rye & barley) causes the immune system to attack the lining of the small intestine, flattening the villi that absorb nutrients. Less absorptive surface = malabsorption of fats, nutrients, and fat-soluble vitamins. This is autoimmune, not functional (IBS) and not the same as inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) — three different diseases that all show up with GI complaints.

🌾 Gluten = the triggerWheat · rye · barley — an autoimmune attack on the small bowel.
🏔️ Villous atrophyFlattened villi → malabsorption → steatorrhea (fatty stools).
🅰️🅳🅴🅺 Fat-soluble vitaminsA, D, E, K are the ones lost when fat absorption fails.
🥗 Lifelong gluten-free dietTest BEFORE going gluten-free, or serology will read falsely negative.
🧨

CAUSE

STEP 1 · GLUTEN TRIGGERS AN IMMUNE ATTACK

One protein sets off an autoimmune reaction that flattens the gut's absorptive surface.

🏔️ Normal villi vs. celiac villous atrophy

MECHANISM Ingested gluten triggers an autoimmune response that damages the small intestine's mucosa. The finger-like villi that normally increase absorptive surface area flatten out (villous atrophy) — nutrients, especially fats, pass through unabsorbed.

🧠 "Flat villi, empty tank." No finger-like projections left = no efficient absorption = malnutrition even while eating a normal amount of food.

🌾 The gluten grains — what to avoid

  • 🌾 Wheat — bread, pasta, spaghetti, breaded meats
  • 🍞 Rye
  • 🍺 Barley — including most beer
🧠 "WRB" — Wheat, Rye, Barley. Hidden in dinner rolls, breaded meat, spaghetti, and beer.

🧬 Who's at risk

  • 🧬 Genetic link — HLA-DQ2 / DQ8, tends to run in families
  • 🦋 Often coexists with Type 1 diabetes and autoimmune thyroid disease
  • 🧒 Can present in childhood or adulthood
🧠 One autoimmune disease raises suspicion for another — celiac travels with T1DM and thyroid disease.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT MALABSORPTION

Everything downstream traces back to one thing: fat and nutrients aren't getting absorbed.

📉 Malabsorption cascade

🌾 Gluten ingested
▼
🛡️ Autoimmune attack on small intestine mucosa
▼
🏔️ Villous atrophy — absorptive surface lost
▼
💩 Steatorrhea, weight loss, vitamin/mineral deficiency
🧠 Every symptom below is just this cascade wearing a different hat.

🔎 Signs & symptoms

  • 💩 Steatorrhea — fatty, foul-smelling, floating stools (fat malabsorption)
  • 🌀 Diarrhea, abdominal pain, bloating
  • ⚖️ Weight loss, fatigue (iron-deficiency anemia)
  • 🧒 In children: stunted growth, delayed puberty
  • 🦴 Osteoporosis/osteopenia (Ca²⁺ & vitamin D malabsorption)
  • 🔴 Dermatitis herpetiformis — itchy, blistering rash
🧠 "Steatorrhea = the fat you didn't absorb, floating in the toilet."

🅰️🅳🅴🅺 Fat-soluble vitamin loss

Fat malabsorption takes vitamins A, D, E, K down with it.

🧠 "ADEK" — the fat-soluble vitamins go wherever fat goes. No fat absorption, no A/D/E/K absorption.

🧪 Diagnosis — order matters

  • 🧪 Serology first — tissue transglutaminase IgA (tTG-IgA), endomysial antibody (EMA)
  • 🔬 Confirm with small bowel biopsy — shows villous atrophy
  • Do NOT start a gluten-free diet before testing — it causes a false-negative result
🧠 Test first, treat second. Going gluten-free early erases the very evidence the test is looking for.
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · LIFELONG GLUTEN-FREE

The only treatment is diet — there is no medication that lets a celiac client eat gluten safely.

✅ Gluten-free foods

  • 🍗 Plain meats — fish, beef, chicken, turkey
  • 🌾 Grains: rice, corn, potatoes, soy, quinoa
  • 🥜 Nuts, beans, legumes
  • 🍎 Fruits & vegetables
🧠 "RCPSQ" — Rice, Corn, Potatoes, Soy, Quinoa — the go-to safe starches.

❌ Avoid — hidden gluten sources

🍞Dinner rolls 
🍗Breaded meat 
🍝Spaghetti 
🍺Beerrye & barley
🧠 Read every label — sauces, soups, and processed foods can hide wheat as a thickener.

🌀 Tell them apart — the 3-way mix-up (same as IBS/IBD)

Celiac = immune system attacking gluten-exposed mucosa. IBS = motility/sensitivity problem, nothing structurally wrong. IBD = chronic structural inflammation/ulceration independent of gluten.

🧠 Gluten-free helps celiac only — it will not fix IBS or IBD, even though all three can cause diarrhea and abdominal pain.

⚠️ NCLEX traps

  • Gluten-free is not just wheat-free — rye and barley are excluded too
  • Oats are naturally gluten-free but are frequently cross-contaminated during processing — advise caution unless labeled "certified gluten-free"
  • The diet is lifelong, not just during symptomatic flares
  • Monitor for fat-soluble vitamin deficiency (A, D, E, K) and iron-deficiency anemia even after diagnosis
🧠 "Lifelong, not just flare-long." Stopping the diet once symptoms improve lets the villi flatten right back out.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What causes celiac disease's intestinal damage?
An autoimmune reaction to gluten (wheat, rye, barley) that attacks the small intestine mucosa, causing villous atrophy — flattened villi and a smaller absorptive surface.
Q2: Why does celiac disease cause fatty, foul-smelling stools?
Steatorrhea — with fewer villi to absorb fat, undigested fat passes into the stool.
Q3: Which vitamins are at risk, and why?
Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K — they require fat absorption to be taken up, which is impaired in celiac disease.
Q4: A client wants to try a gluten-free diet before getting tested for celiac disease. Best response?
Get tested first (tTG-IgA serology, then biopsy if positive) — starting a gluten-free diet beforehand can cause a false-negative result.
