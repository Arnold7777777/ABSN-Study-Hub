Pancreatitis 🔥🥞

Acute & Chronic — the pancreas digests itself

NG-159 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

Autodigestion — digestive enzymes activate inside the pancreas instead of the duodenum, so the pancreas digests itself. Epigastric/LUQ pain that radiates to the back, sky-high lipase, and a gut that must be put to complete rest (NPO) while IV fluids and pain control lead the way.

CAUSE

STEP 1 · AUTODIGESTION

One organ, two jobs — when the digestive job turns on itself, the endocrine job (glucose control) gets dragged down with it.

🥞 The pancreas has two completely different jobs

Exocrine = digestive enzymes into the duodenum. Endocrine = hormones into the blood. Pancreatitis is an exocrine disease that drags the endocrine side down with it.

🧠 “The chef poisons the kitchen.” Enzymes are supposed to cook (digest) food in the duodenum — in pancreatitis they turn on early, inside the pancreas itself.

⚗️ Pathophysiology — autodigestion in 3 steps

1️⃣ Trigger injures pancreatic cells (alcohol toxin, gallstone blocking duct, ERCP trauma)
▼
2️⃣ Digestive enzymes activate prematurely, inside the pancreas instead of the duodenum
▼
3️⃣ Enzymes digest pancreatic tissue itself = autodigestion → inflammation, edema, possible hemorrhage/necrosis
🧠 PancreATEitis — the pancreas literally starts to eat itself.

🍺 Top causes — I GET SMASHED (short list she needs)

  • 🍷 Alcohol abuse — most common cause overall
  • 🪨 Gallbladder disease / gallstones — a stone blocks the shared duct
  • 🩺 ERCP — Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangio­Pancreatography, a known post-procedure trigger
  • 🫁 Cystic fibrosis — thick mucus plugs pancreatic ducts
  • 🧪 Also: hypertriglyceridemia, hypercalcemia, some drugs, trauma
🧠 “Booze & Stones.” If an NCLEX stem doesn't name a drug or CF, the answer is almost always alcohol or a gallstone.

⭐ Acute vs Chronic — same organ, different timeline

AcuteChronic
Sudden onset, reversible if caught earlyIrreversible fibrosis & scarring over years
Usually gallstones or alcohol bingeUsually long-term alcohol abuse
Enzymes markedly elevatedEnzymes may be normal — gland is “burned out”
Goal: rest the pancreas, treat the triggerGoal: enzyme replacement (see NG-217) + manage diabetes
🧠 Acute = attack. Chronic = aftermath. Chronic disease is why students learn pancrelipase — the scarred gland can't make its own enzymes anymore.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT IT

Pain location, two hemorrhagic skin signs, and the one lab that beats the others.

🔪 Pain — epigastric / LUQ radiating straight to the back

Sudden, severe, boring pain — worse lying flat or after a fatty meal/alcohol; classically eased by sitting up & leaning forward (fetal/knee-chest position takes pressure off the retroperitoneal pancreas).

🧠 “LUQ, then out the back door.” If a stem says epigastric pain boring through to the back, think pancreas first.

🚨 Cullen's sign vs Grey Turner's sign — hemorrhagic pancreatitis

Blue-gray bruising = retroperitoneal bleeding tracking through tissue planes → hemorrhagic/necrotizing pancreatitis, a severe complication.

🧠 “Cullen circles the belly button, Turner takes the flanks.” Both = internal bleeding, not a bruise from trauma.

🧪 Labs: lipase beats amylase

Lipase is more specific to the pancreas & stays elevated longer — trust lipase over amylase.

🧠 “LIPase LINGERS.” Both rhyme with staying power — lipase is the one you can still catch days later.

🧪 The rest of the lab panel

  • 📈 Elevated glucose — “hyperglycemia,” damaged islet cells
  • 📈 Elevated WBC — over 10,000/mm³, inflammation
  • 📈 Elevated bilirubin — jaundice if biliary cause
  • 📉 Hypocalcemia — calcium binds to fat that's been “saponified” by leaked lipase
  • 📈 Elevated PT/aPTT — coagulation disrupted in severe disease
  • 💧 Ascites may develop from third-spacing/peritoneal irritation
🧠 “Calcium goes where the fat goes.” Watch for Chvostek's/Trousseau's signs of hypocalcemia in severe pancreatitis.

🚨 Priority assessment after ERCP — SATA style

COMMON NCLEX QUESTION Post-ERCP epigastric pain = assess this client FIRST — think pancreatitis until ruled out.

Findings consistent with acute pancreatitis (select all that apply): gray-blue flank discoloration, abdominal guarding & tenderness, LUQ pain radiating to the back, elevated amylase/lipase, hyperglycemia, elevated WBC.

🧠 If the stem mentions a recent ERCP + new epigastric pain, your nursing brain should say “pancreatitis” before anything else.
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · REST THE PANCREAS

Stop feeding the fire, replace fluid, control pain safely, and watch for the complications that kill.

🥇 Priority interventions — in order

1
💧 IV fluids — aggressive resuscitation, third-spacing is huge
2
💊 IV pain control — hydromorphone (Dilaudid) preferred
3
🚫 NPO — complete bowel rest stops enzyme stimulation
4
🩹 NG tube to suction if vomiting/distention
5
🩸 Monitor glucose — treat hyperglycemia with insulin

NCLEX TIP Fluids + pain control together are the top priority intervention for acute pancreatitis — not just one alone.

🧠 “NPO, IV, and PAIN-O.” Nothing by mouth, fluids running, pain treated — in that order of urgency.

❌ Traditional teaching: avoid morphine

Older NCLEX-style teaching says avoid morphine — thought to spasm the sphincter of Oddi and worsen pain. Hydromorphone (Dilaudid) is the classic preferred choice.

🧠 Practice varies by facility — for exam purposes, remember hydromorphone yes, morphine caution.

💊 Pharmacology used in pancreatitis

ClassDrug
Proton pump inhibitorPantoprazole — cuts acid, reduces pancreatic stimulation
H2 blockerFamotidine
Opioid analgesicHydromorphone (Dilaudid)
InsulinSliding scale for stress hyperglycemia
🧠 Antacids/PPIs/H2 blockers aren't random — less stomach acid hitting the duodenum means less signal for the pancreas to secrete enzymes.

🍽️ Diet progression & chronic disease teaching

Acute phase: NPO → clear liquids → advance to low-fat, low-sugar diet as tolerated. Fat is the biggest enzyme trigger.

🍟NO Fried Chickenhigh fat
🥔NO Chipshigh fat
🥛NO Whole Milk+Cookiesfat + sugar
🍗Grilled Chickenlean protein — OK
🥔Baked PotatoOK
🧀Reduced-Fat Cheese+ whole wheat crackers — OK

In chronic pancreatitis, the gland can no longer make its own enzymes → lifelong pancreatic enzyme replacement (pancrelipase) with every meal and snack — see NG-217 for full drug teaching.

🧠 “Low fat keeps it flat.” A flat, calm pancreas doesn't need to secrete much — fat is what fires it up.

REPORT TO HCP Fever >100.3°F, rebound tenderness, rigid/board-like abdomen, increasing pain, restlessness, tachycardia/tachypnea.

🧠 Same red-flag cluster you'll see across every GI page this batch — memorize it once, apply it everywhere: fever + rigid belly + rising pain + restless + fast HR/RR = call the provider now.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: Which lab value is more specific for pancreatitis and stays elevated longer?
Lipase — amylase rises fast but falls within days; lipase lingers.
Q2: What do bluish bruises around the umbilicus vs at the flanks indicate, and what are they called?
Retroperitoneal hemorrhage from hemorrhagic/necrotizing pancreatitis — Cullen's sign (umbilicus) and Grey Turner's sign (flanks).
Q3: What is the priority nursing intervention combo for acute pancreatitis?
IV fluids and pain control, plus NPO to rest the pancreas.
Q4: Why do clients with chronic pancreatitis need pancrelipase with every meal?
The scarred, fibrotic gland can no longer make enough of its own digestive enzymes, so replacement enzymes are needed to digest fat, protein, and carbs and prevent malabsorption/steatorrhea.
