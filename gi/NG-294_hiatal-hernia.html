Hiatal Hernia ⬆️

The stomach pushes up through the diaphragm

NG-294 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

Part of the stomach herniates through the diaphragm and ends up sitting in — or beside — the esophagus. It's one of the most common causes of chronic reflux (see the GERD page, NG-036) because it drags the LES out of its normal high-pressure zone. See NG-293 Key Terms for dysphagia, reflux & epigastric pain.

⬆️ Stomach through the holeThe hiatus = the diaphragm's opening for the esophagus. Herniate through it = hiatal hernia.
🔥 GERD's landlordA hiatal hernia commonly causes GERD — same reflux, heartburn, worse-lying-down pattern.
🏋️ Straining = riskLifting, straining, and anything that raises abdominal pressure pushes the stomach up.
🚨 Paraesophageal = dangerThe rolling type can strangulate — sudden chest pain + can't vomit = emergency.
🧨

CAUSE

STEP 1 · THE WEAK HOLE

The diaphragm has a normal opening for the esophagus — when it stretches wide, the stomach follows.

🫁 Two types — same weak spot, different behavior

EXAM TIP Sliding = most common (95%). Paraesophageal is rarer but is the one that can strangulate.

🧠 "Sliding SLIDES together, PARA rolls up PAST it." In sliding, the GEJ and stomach move together like a drawer sliding up. In paraesophageal, the GEJ stays anchored and the fundus rolls up alongside — that's the dangerous one.

🔤 Say the word — it tells you the disease

  • Hiatus = the diaphragm's normal opening for the esophagus 🕳️
  • Hernia = protrusion of an organ through a weak spot ⬆️

Hiatal hernia = part of the stomach pushes through the diaphragm's esophageal opening, ending up in — or beside — the chest.

🧠 Same word family as an inguinal hernia — an organ escaping through a weak spot — just a different hole.

⚠️ Risk factors — what widens the hole

  • 🏋️ Straining / lifting heavy objects
  • 💪 Weightlifting exercises
  • 📈 Increased abdominal pressure (coughing, pregnancy, ascites)
  • ⚖️ Obesity
🧠 "Push down there, pop up here." Anything that raises pressure below the diaphragm forces the stomach up through it — the exact opposite direction of normal digestion.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT IT

Reflux, swallowing trouble, and chest symptoms that mimic cardiac disease — plus the one complication that can't wait.

🔥 Signs & symptoms — same reflux pattern as GERD

  • 😖 Dysphagia — difficulty swallowing (see NG-293)
  • 🔥 Gastric reflux — heartburn / epigastric pain
  • 💔 Chest pain & SOB — can mimic a cardiac event

Because a sliding hernia drags the LES out of the diaphragm's high-pressure zone, it reproduces the exact same reflux pattern taught on the GERD page — worse when lying flat, better sitting up.

🧠 "Chest pain + SOB — rule out the heart first." A hiatal hernia can look exactly like angina. Cardiac causes get ruled out before anyone blames the diaphragm.

🔬 Diagnostics

  • Barium swallow — outlines the herniated stomach on X-ray
  • EGD — direct visualization, same scope used for GERD/PUD
  • Chest X-ray — sometimes an incidental finding
🧠 EGD = "Every Gut Door" — Esophagus → Gastro → Duodenum, the same three-door scope from the GERD page.

🚨 Paraesophageal complication: strangulation

🌀 Fundus rolls & twists (like a volvulus, NG-293)
▼
🩸 Blood supply cut off
▼
☠️ Necrosis / perforation — EMERGENCY
🧠 Sudden severe chest pain + can't vomit + can't pass a nasogastric tube = strangulated hiatal hernia until proven otherwise. This is a surgical emergency, not a diet fix.

📋 Sliding vs. Paraesophageal — tell them apart

Type I · SlidingType II · Paraesophageal
~95% of hiatal herniasLess common, more dangerous
GE junction slides up with the stomachGE junction stays put; fundus rolls up beside it
Main problem: reflux (GERD-like)Main problem: strangulation risk
Usually managed medically (same as GERD)Often repaired surgically even without symptoms
🧠 NCLEX trap: not every hiatal hernia needs surgery — sliding hernias are usually managed like GERD. Paraesophageal hernias are the ones providers worry about even before symptoms show up.
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · TAKE THE PRESSURE OFF

Every intervention does one of two things: lowers abdominal pressure, or keeps acid from climbing — the same two levers as GERD.

✅ Nursing care & education

1
🏋️ Avoid lifting / straining
2
👖 NO tight clothing — "girdle," tight waistbands
3
🍽️ Small, frequent, low-fat meals
4
🛏️ Sit up / elevate HOB after eating & at night
🧠 "Sound familiar?" This is the exact same "SEE-3" logic from GERD: sit up, elevate, eat small, wait before lying down — because it's the exact same LES/pressure problem underneath.

❌ AVOID — same triggers as GERD

🚬NO Tobacco 
☕NO Caffeine 
🍫Chocolate 
🌿Peppermint 
🍺Alcohol 
🥓High-fat foodsgo low fat

Low fat diet = BEST — every item on this list can weaken the LES or raise abdominal pressure.

🧠 Same "midnight snack tray" mnemonic as GERD (NG-036) — every single item loosens the same door.

🔪 When surgery is needed

Nissen fundoplication — wraps the fundus of the stomach around the lower esophagus and sutures it, tightening the LES and reducing the hernia back below the diaphragm.

Reserved for: paraesophageal hernias (often even if asymptomatic, due to strangulation risk), or sliding hernias with severe reflux unresponsive to medical management.

🧠 Fundoplication = Fold the Fundus 🎀 — same surgery referenced on the GERD page, because a hiatal hernia and GERD are treated with the same fix once medication fails.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What's the difference between a sliding and a paraesophageal hiatal hernia?
Sliding (Type I, ~95%): the GE junction slides up with the stomach — causes GERD-like reflux. Paraesophageal (Type II): the GE junction stays put while the fundus rolls up beside it — risk of strangulation.
Q2: Why does a hiatal hernia cause the same symptoms as GERD?
It drags the LES out of the diaphragm's normal high-pressure zone, so acid climbs the same way it does in GERD — worse lying flat, better sitting up.
Q3: A patient reports sudden severe chest pain and cannot vomit. What should you suspect?
Strangulated paraesophageal hernia — a surgical emergency. Blood supply to the twisted stomach tissue is cut off.
Q4: Name three lifestyle teaching points that lower a hiatal hernia patient's abdominal pressure.
Avoid heavy lifting/straining, avoid tight clothing, and eat small frequent low-fat meals — plus sit up/elevate the HOB after eating.
