Irritable Bowel Syndrome 🌀

IBS — A Functional Gut Disorder

NG-299 GI ADHD-friendly visual edition

IBS is a functional disorder of the large intestine — cramping, bloating, pain, and altered bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or both) with no structural damage and no inflammation on scope or biopsy. Keep in mind: IBS is NOT IBD (Crohn's/ulcerative colitis) and it is NOT celiac disease — same symptoms, completely different pathology.

🌀 Functional, not structuralThe colon looks normal on scope — it's how it behaves, not how it looks.
🚩 Red flags ≠ IBSBlood in stool, fever, weight loss → think IBD or cancer, work it up.
📓 Journal + fiber + exerciseTrack triggers, add fiber/fluids gradually, move 3×/week.
🚫 IBS ≠ IBD ≠ CeliacFunctional vs. structural inflammation vs. autoimmune villous atrophy.
🧨

CAUSE

STEP 1 · WHY THE GUT MISBEHAVES

Nothing is structurally wrong — the gut-brain signaling and motility are just miswired.

🌀 A functional disorder — the colon "acts up" without being damaged

IBS involves visceral hypersensitivity (the gut feels normal stretch as pain) and altered motility (too fast → diarrhea, too slow → constipation), driven by dysregulated brain–gut axis signaling. There is no inflammation, no ulceration, no villous atrophy — a colonoscopy and biopsy in true IBS come back essentially normal.

🧠 "Miswired, not damaged." IBS is a signaling problem, not a structural disease — that's the whole reason biopsies look normal.

⚡ Common triggers

  • 😖 Stress / anxiety
  • 🍽️ Certain foods (gas-producing, high-FODMAP)
  • 🦠 Post-infectious (after a GI infection)
  • 🩸 Hormonal changes
🧠 Triggers flare IBS — they don't cause tissue damage the way IBD's triggers do.

🔤 Subtypes — know the letters

  • IBS-D — diarrhea-predominant
  • IBS-C — constipation-predominant
  • IBS-M — mixed (both)
🧠 Same disease, three different bowel habits — treatment (fiber vs. antidiarrheal) depends on which one.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · SPOT IT — AND SPOT WHAT IT ISN'T

Symptoms overlap with IBD and celiac disease on paper — the exam wants you to know the tell.

🔎 Classic IBS symptoms

  • 😣 Crampy abdominal pain, relieved by defecation
  • 🎈 Bloating, excess gas
  • 🔄 Alternating diarrhea & constipation (or one predominant pattern)
  • 💧 Mucus in the stool
  • ⏱️ Chronic/recurrent — ≥3 months, symptom-based diagnosis (Rome IV criteria)
🧠 Pain that gets better after a bowel movement is a hallmark of IBS.

🚩 Red flags that mean it is NOT just IBS

  • Blood in the stool
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Fever
  • Nighttime symptoms that wake the client from sleep
  • Symptom onset after age 50
🧠 IBS is a diagnosis of exclusion — red flags mean rule out IBD, celiac disease, or colorectal cancer first.

🧪 Common exam question — spot the correct diet picks

Which meal choices demonstrate correct understanding of an IBS diet? Beans · Cornbread · Fruit salad · Steak · Tomato basil soup · Yogurt with granola

  • ❌ Beans (legume — classic gas-producer)
  • ❌ Cornbread (refined, low fiber, doesn't help symptom control)
  • ✅ Fruit salad (low-gas fiber source)
  • ✅ Steak (protein, not a gas producer)
  • ✅ Tomato basil soup (well-tolerated, non-gas-forming)
  • ❌ Yogurt with granola (dairy + high-fiber granola can both trigger gas/bloating)
🧠 Correct answers = 3, 4, 5. The pattern: skip legumes, dairy, and eggs; protein, fruit, and simple cooked vegetables are safer picks.

CARE

STEP 3 · MANAGE THE TRIGGERS

There's no cure — management is diet, stress control, and symptom-targeted medication.

✅ Education & lifestyle

  • 📓 Keep a symptom/diet/stress journal to identify personal triggers
  • 🏃 Exercise 3× per week
  • 💧 Increase fiber & fluid intake — gradually, to avoid worsening gas/bloating
  • 🧘 Stress management techniques
🧠 Journal first. You can't limit a trigger you haven't identified yet.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What makes IBS "functional" rather than structural?
No inflammation, ulceration, or villous atrophy is found on colonoscopy/biopsy — the problem is altered motility and visceral hypersensitivity, not tissue damage.
Q2: Name 3 red-flag symptoms that mean it's probably NOT just IBS.
Blood in stool, unintentional weight loss, fever, nighttime symptoms, or onset after age 50 — any of these should prompt work-up for IBD, celiac, or colorectal cancer.
Q3: How is IBS different from celiac disease?
IBS is functional with no immune/structural change. Celiac is an autoimmune reaction to gluten causing villous atrophy in the small intestine — a biopsy finding IBS never has.
Q4: A client with IBS-C asks what to add to their diet. Best answer?
Gradually increase fiber and fluids; a bulk-forming fiber supplement like psyllium is appropriate for constipation-predominant IBS.
