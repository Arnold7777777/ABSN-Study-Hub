🧬 Addison disease vs Cushing syndrome

Recognise the pattern, then confirm with testing. The infographic, and the same facts as plain text for a screen reader or a tired pair of eyes.

Addison disease — primary adrenal insufficiency

Too little cortisol. Aldosterone is often low.

  • Visual clues: darkened skin creases or pressure points, weight loss, fatigue and weakness.
  • Blood pressure often decreases.
  • Glucose may decrease.
  • Sodium may decrease and potassium may increase.

Cushing syndrome — excess glucocorticoid effect

Too much steroid effect, from the body’s own cortisol or from glucocorticoid medicines.

  • Visual clues: rounded face, central weight gain, fat at the back of the neck, wide purple stretch marks, easy bruising and muscle weakness.
  • Blood pressure and glucose often increase.
  • Sodium varies; potassium may decrease.
  • May result from steroid medicines.

⚠️ Read the picture carefully

  • The illustrations show examples, not a diagnostic checklist. Not every person has all these features.
  • Hypoglycaemia in adrenal insufficiency matters most in children; it is less common in adults.
  • The sodium and potassium lines are common patterns, not guaranteed laboratory findings.
  • Addison disease means primary adrenal insufficiency. The hyperpigmentation and the mineralocorticoid loss do not generalise to secondary or tertiary adrenal insufficiency.
  • Cushing disease is the pituitary, ACTH-producing form of Cushing syndrome — not a synonym for every cause. Glucocorticoid medicines can produce Cushing features while the person’s own cortisol is suppressed.
  • Never stop prescribed steroids abruptly — they are tapered.
