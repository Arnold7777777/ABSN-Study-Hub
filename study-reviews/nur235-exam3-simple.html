Rules to remember

  • Watch effort and alertness as well as breath sounds.
  • A sudden cyanotic spell in tetralogy of Fallot needs prompt support.

See this → think this

A child with stridor is drooling and leaning forward, and appears frightened.

Think…
Possible epiglottitis. Agitation or a throat examination can worsen airway obstruction.

A child with tetralogy becomes intensely blue and distressed during crying.

Think…
Reduced pulmonary blood flow produces a hypercyanotic spell.

What do I do first?

Respiratory

First action
Keep the child calm in the position of comfort and call the airway team. Avoid examining the throat with a tongue depressor.
Look for a response
Watch air movement, color, alertness and readiness for a controlled airway.

Cardiac

First action
Calm the child, bring knees toward the chest and provide oxygen and emergency support per protocol.
Look for a response
Reassess color, oxygenation and level of alertness.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

A peak-flow plan compares today's value with the child's personal best. The usual green zone is at least 80%, yellow 50–79% and red below 50%.

A quiet chest in a tiring child with severe asthma can signal very poor airflow. Reassess work of breathing and alertness immediately.

Practice and memory cues

Stridor points toward the upper airway. Wheeze often reflects narrowed lower airways. A blue spell in tetralogy of Fallot suggests reduced lung blood flow.

A 3-year-old arrives with a temperature of 39.5 C, drooling, a muffled voice, and is sitting forward on his hands refusing to lie back. He has no cough. Which action should the nurse take first?

  1. Inspect the throat with a tongue blade for exudate
  2. Obtain a throat culture
  3. Keep the child upright with the parent and ensure emergency airway equipment is at the bedside
  4. Position the child supine for a chest x-ray
Check the answer and why

Keep the child upright with the parent and ensure emergency airway equipment is at the bedside

High fever, drooling, muffled voice, tripod posture and no cough is epiglottitis — never use a tongue blade or inspect the throat, because it can trigger complete airway obstruction. Croup is the look-alike, but croup brings a barking cough with a low-grade fever.

An infant with tetralogy of Fallot has a hypercyanotic (tet) spell. What is the nurse's first action?

  1. Place the infant in a knee-chest position
  2. Lay the infant flat and stimulate vigorously
  3. Give a rapid IV fluid bolus first
  4. Withhold oxygen to avoid closing the duct
Check the answer and why

Place the infant in a knee-chest position

Knee-chest raises systemic vascular resistance, which pushes blood back through the pulmonary circulation instead of shunting right to left. Older children squat instinctively for the same reason. Then calm the child, give oxygen and morphine as prescribed.
