Which statement about burn depth and pain is accurate?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. The deeper the burn, the more severe the pain, without exception
  2. Full-thickness burns are the most painful because they extend into muscle

    A full-thickness burn destroys the nerve endings, so the center can be dry, leathery and insensate — often surrounded by a painful partial-thickness rim.

  3. Superficial burns are painless because only the epidermis is involved

    Superficial burns are red, dry and painful .

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: “The worst burn should hurt the most” is false , and it is a favourite exam trap. A full-thickness burn destroys the nerve endings, so the center can be dry, leathery and insensate — often surrounded by a painful partial-thickness rim. Pain is therefore not a measure of depth. Blistered, wet and very painful = partial thickness. Dry, leathery eschar with little or no sensation = full thickness. Superficial burns are red, dry and painful .
