Which clients are at increased risk for hyperkalemia? Select all that apply.

Simple Nursing · Diabetes · DKA vs HHS (6:50)
Why the other answers do not fit
  1. A client taking furosemide for heart failure

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Hyperkalemia is nearly always a problem of decreased excretion or cellular shifting — renal disease, potassium-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, stored blood, and the acidosis of DKA all fit. A 67-year-old client with severe bilateral arthritis of the hands has been diagnosed with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Which inhalation delivery method is best for the bronchodilator ordered for this client?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. A conventional pressurized metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)

    Any pMDI requires dexterity between inspiration and the mechanics of the inhaler to be effective.

  2. A breath-actuated pressurized metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)

    Any pMDI requires dexterity between inspiration and the mechanics of the inhaler to be effective.

  3. A dry-powder inhaler (DPI)

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: A SVN is the best choice for the client with severe arthritis in both hands. The SVN is a machine that mixes the medication and turns it into a mist. The client then uses a handheld apparatus or mask and just breathes in the treatment. Any pMDI requires dexterity between inspiration and the mechanics of the inhaler to be effective. Although DPIs rely solely on inspiration for medication delivery, they still require the user to press a button or lever to dispense the medication. Poor inhaler technique has been linked to a lack of symptom control.
