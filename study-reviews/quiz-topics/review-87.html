Why does carbon monoxide cause tissue hypoxia?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. It destroys alveolar surfactant, collapsing the alveoli
  2. It causes bronchospasm that blocks airflow to the alveoli
  3. It reduces the amount of hemoglobin the bone marrow produces

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Carbon monoxide has roughly 200 times the affinity of oxygen for hemoglobin, so it displaces oxygen and forms carboxyhemoglobin . The hemoglobin is there and the oximeter is fooled, but it is not carrying oxygen — so the tissues are hypoxic while the numbers look fine. Loss of surfactant and bronchospasm are features of lower airway smoke injury, a different problem that can coexist.
