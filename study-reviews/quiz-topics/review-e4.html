A 2-month-old in the cardiology clinic is wearing only a diaper after a weight check. He is in no visible distress. BP 76/52 mm Hg, HR 146/min, respirations 34/min, temperature 36.3°C (97.4°F), SpO₂ 66% on room air. Which nursing actions are the priority? Select all that apply.

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. Send another nurse for the Broselow cart and oxygen

    The Broselow cart, oxygen and an urgent call to the provider are all responses to a child who is actually in trouble

  2. Call for another nurse to fetch the provider immediately

    The Broselow cart, oxygen and an urgent call to the provider are all responses to a child who is actually in trouble

  3. Start blow-by oxygen and recheck in 10 minutes

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Everything else is normal, and the baby looks well. BP, heart rate, respirations and temperature all sit in range for a 2-month-old — so a saturation of 66% in a child with no visible distress is a bad reading until proven otherwise. A truly hypoxic infant at 66% would be mottled, grunting, retracting and frantic. He has been undressed in a cool room . Cold hands and feet mean poor peripheral perfusion, and a pulse oximeter cannot read a pulse it cannot feel. So: warm him , look at his colour and warmth , and check whether the oximeter's heart rate matches his real one — if the numbers disagree, the machine is not tracking his pulse and the saturation it prints is meaningless. Move the probe and recheck on another extremity. Treat the equipment before you treat the number. The Broselow cart, oxygen and an urgent call to the provider are all responses to a child who is actually in trouble; using them here frightens the parent and treats an artefact. If the reading stays low once he is warm and the probe is tracking, then it is real — escalate then.
