A nurse is caring for a client hospitalized with acute diverticulitis. To reduce the risk of COMPLICATIONS, which nursing interventions should the nurse implement?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. Encourage frequent ambulation
  2. Apply a heating pad to the abdomen

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Read the stem carefully — it asks what prevents COMPLICATIONS, not what feels nice. Clear liquids rest an inflamed, possibly strictured bowel and reduce obstruction risk. Monitoring for peritonitis catches a ruptured diverticulum early — know the cues: ABDOMINAL RIGIDITY, GUARDING, REBOUND TENDERNESS. Antibiotics prevent progression to abscess and peritonitis. Ambulation increases peristalsis when you want bowel rest (and prevents DVT, not a diverticulitis complication). Heat causes VASODILATION, bringing more blood to already-inflamed tissue. A rule from lecture: whenever an option involves heat or cold, ask what it does physiologically — heat vasodilates, cold vasoconstricts — rather than memorizing a list.
