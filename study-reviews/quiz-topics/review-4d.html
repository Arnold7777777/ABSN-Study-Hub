Which laboratory findings should the nurse expect in a client with fluid volume deficit?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. Decreased hematocrit
  2. Urine output of 80 mL/hr

    A falling hematocrit and a generous urine output contradict the picture

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Losing plasma water concentrates everything left behind — specific gravity, BUN (normal 8–20), hematocrit, and RBC count all rise. A falling hematocrit and a generous urine output contradict the picture; oliguria is expected as the kidneys conserve fluid.

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. Capillary refill of 2 seconds

  2. Low urine specific gravity

    A 2-second refill is mild at most, and dehydration raises specific gravity because the urine is concentrated .
