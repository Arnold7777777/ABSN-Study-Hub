Which oxygen saturation target should the nurse aim for in a client who has COPD, and why?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. 88 to 92%, because a low oxygen level is what drives a COPD client to breathe

    The target of 88 to 92% is correct , and titrated oxygen genuinely lowers mortality compared with high-flow.

  2. Below 85%, to protect the respiratory drive
  3. Above 96%, because any hypoxia is dangerous

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: The target of 88 to 92% is correct , and titrated oxygen genuinely lowers mortality compared with high-flow. But the reason usually taught - the hypoxic drive - has been largely debunked . The real mechanisms are loss of hypoxic vasoconstriction, which worsens V/Q mismatch , and the Haldane effect , where oxygenated hemoglobin releases carbon dioxide. It matters because the myth leads people to withhold oxygen from a hypoxic client. Never withhold oxygen from someone who is hypoxic - titrate it to the target.
