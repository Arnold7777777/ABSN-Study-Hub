What is the key difference between defibrillation and synchronized cardioversion?

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. They are identical procedures
  2. Defibrillation is used only for stable clients
  3. Cardioversion is always done without sedation

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: Synchronized cardioversion times the shock to the R wave (avoids the vulnerable T wave) — used for unstable rhythms WITH a pulse (e.g., unstable A-fib, SVT, V-tach with pulse). Defibrillation is unsynchronized — for V-fib and pulseless V-tach.

Why the other answers do not fit

  2. The client will experience tissue hypoxia with no sensation of shortness of breath or labored breathing.
  3. The client will experience respiratory alkalosis with no ability to compensate.

Shared reasoning for choices without a separate note: With a normal hemoglobin level of 15 mg/dL and a PaO2 level of 40 mm Hg (SaO2 75%), there is adequate oxygen available for the tissues, but no reserve for physiologic stresses that increase tissue oxygen demand.
