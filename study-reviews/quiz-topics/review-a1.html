A 47-year-old man weighing 91 kg has burns totalling 28 percent TBSA. Using the Parkland formula, what rate in mL/hr should be infused during the first 8 hours?

Answer · 637 mL/hr

This question uses the classic adult Parkland formula. The calculation assumes the full first 8 hours are available and no resuscitation fluid has already been given.

The Parkland formula

4 mL × weight in kg × %TBSA

This estimates the volume of Lactated Ringer’s (LR) for the first 24 hours after the burn.

  • TBSA means total body surface area. Include partial-thickness and full-thickness burns in the calculation.
  • Use the percentage as a whole number — enter 28 for a 28% burn.
  • First 8 hours — give half the calculated volume.
  • Next 16 hours — give the remaining half.

Formula and timing · University of Cincinnati burn service protocol · Parkland Formula, full-text chapter

Work this question in three steps

  1. Calculate the 24-hour volume
    4 × 91 × 28 = 10,192 mL.
  2. Find the first half
    10,192 ÷ 2 = 5,096 mL.
  3. Divide by the first 8 hours
    5,096 ÷ 8 = 637 mL/hr.
The clock starts at the injury

If the patient arrives later, account for the hours already elapsed and the fluid already received. The first portion is due by hour 8 after the burn.

Why the other answers do not fit
  1. 425 mL/hr

    10,192 ÷ 24 = 424.7 mL/hr, rounded to 425. This spreads the entire volume evenly across 24 hours. The classic Parkland schedule puts half into the first 8 hours.

  2. 1,274 mL/hr

    5,096 ÷ 4 = 1,274 mL/hr. This would deliver the first half in only 4 hours. The question gives the full first 8-hour interval.

  3. 319 mL/hr

    5,096 ÷ 16 = 318.5 mL/hr, rounded to 319. This is the estimated rate for the remaining half over the next 16 hours.
