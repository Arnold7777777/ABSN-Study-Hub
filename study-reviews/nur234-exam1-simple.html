Rules to remember

  • Match the method to pregnancy goals and STI protection.
  • Trace where oxygen enters fetal circulation.
  • Separate evidence of pregnancy from maternal symptoms.
  • Use the prenatal history and the fetal assessment together.

See this → think this

A client wants to avoid pregnancy for several years and also reduce STI risk.

Think…
An implant or IUD gives lasting pregnancy protection. Condoms add STI protection.

The question asks which cord vessel carries oxygenated blood toward the fetus.

Think…
The umbilical vein brings oxygen from the placenta. Two arteries return blood to the placenta.

A missed period and nausea are followed by a positive urine pregnancy test.

Think…
Symptoms are presumptive. Detecting hCG is probable evidence. An embryo seen on ultrasound is positive evidence.

A pregnant patient reports a persistent severe headache and spots in her vision.

Think…
These are urgent maternal warning signs. Assess for a hypertensive disorder.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

GTPAL: count pregnancies separately from births. Twins count as one pregnancy and one delivery in their gestational-age category, but two living children if both survive.

Gravida includes the current pregnancy. For an uncomplicated twin birth at term with both children living and no other pregnancies, G1 T1 P0 A0 L2.

Practice and memory cues

A missed period is a symptom; fetal cardiac activity on ultrasound is direct evidence.

A client is pregnant. She has one child born at 39 weeks, one child born at 34 weeks, and a pregnancy loss at 10 weeks. Both children are living. What is her GTPAL?

  1. 4-1-1-1-2
  2. 3-1-1-1-2
  3. 4-2-0-1-2
  4. 4-1-1-0-3
Check the answer and why

4-1-1-1-2

Gravida counts all pregnancies including the current one , giving 4. Term (37 weeks or more) is 1, preterm (20 to under 37 weeks) is 1, abortions (under 20 weeks) is 1, and living children is 2.

A client at 6 weeks of gestation is upset that the nurse could not find the fetal heartbeat with a Doppler. Which response is accurate?

  1. Heart tones are usually audible by Doppler at 10 to 12 weeks
  2. Heart tones should have been audible by 4 weeks, so an ultrasound is needed today
  3. Heart tones are not audible by any method until quickening at 16 weeks
  4. Heart tones are audible only after the lungs mature at 34 weeks
Check the answer and why

Heart tones are usually audible by Doppler at 10 to 12 weeks

Doppler detects fetal heart tones at 10 to 12 weeks , so absence at 6 weeks is expected. Four weeks is a standing distractor, and the other options confuse the quickening and lung maturity numbers with the Doppler number.
