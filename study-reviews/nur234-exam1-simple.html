Module 1 · Reproductive life planning 3 short topics

Module 1

Reproductive life planning

  • Recognize: Pregnancy goals, medical history, STI exposure and ability to use a method guide reproductive planning.
  • Act: Ask privately about preferences, safety and access; support an informed, voluntary choice and teach correct use.
  • Remember: Pregnancy prevention and STI prevention are separate goals; condoms may accompany another contraceptive method.

Module 1

Fertility awareness

  • Recognize: Cervical mucus becomes clear/stretchy near ovulation; basal temperature rises after ovulation. Cycle tracking estimates fertile days.
  • Act: Teach consistent observations and abstinence or barrier use during the fertile window; assess whether cycles and routines support the method.
  • Remember: Basal temperature confirms ovulation afterward; it does not predict the fertile window by itself.

Module 1

Contraception methods (natural / barrier / hormonal / surgical )

  • Recognize: Natural, barrier, hormonal and permanent methods differ in effectiveness, timing, contraindications and reversibility.
  • Act: Review health history, adherence, backup requirements and emergency contraception; teach condoms for STI-risk reduction.
  • Remember: IUDs/implants provide lasting reversible prevention; sterilization is intended to be permanent. Estrogen-containing methods require assessment of clot risk and other contraindications.

Module 2 · Fetal growth and development 9 short topics

Module 2

Fetal growth and development / stages of development

  • Recognize: Embryonic organ formation occurs mainly during weeks 3–8 after fertilization; fetal growth and maturation follow.
  • Act: Clarify which pregnancy clock is being used when reviewing exposures, developmental milestones and screening dates.
  • Remember: Gestational age starts at the last menstrual period, about two weeks before conception; forming an organ does not mean it is mature.

Module 2

Embryonic and fetal structures

  • Recognize: The amnion surrounds the fluid cavity, the chorion contributes to the placental side, and the yolk sac supports early nutrition and blood-cell formation.
  • Act: Connect each structure with its job when reviewing fetal development and ultrasound findings.
  • Remember: Placenta, cord, membranes and fluid are supporting structures with different functions; they are not interchangeable names.

Module 2

Placental function

  • Recognize: The placenta exchanges oxygen, nutrients and wastes and produces hCG, progesterone, estrogen and hPL. Maternal and fetal blood normally remain separate.
  • Act: Review medication and substance exposures; teach avoiding alcohol and other harmful exposures.
  • Remember: The placenta transfers oxygen rather than manufacturing it, and it does not block every drug, toxin or infection.

Module 2

Amniotic membranes / fluid volumes

  • Recognize: Amniotic fluid cushions the fetus, supports movement and stabilizes temperature. Amnion and chorion form the membranes.
  • Act: Assess reported fluid leakage and follow ordered evaluation of membranes, fluid volume and fetal wellbeing.
  • Remember: Reduced fluid raises cord-compression concerns; excessive fluid can overdistend the uterus. A fluid abnormality requires evaluation of its cause.

Module 2

Umbilical cord

  • Recognize: A normal cord has two arteries and one vein surrounded by protective Wharton's jelly.
  • Act: Identify vessel number and trace flow toward or away from the fetus; report an abnormal vessel count for further assessment.
  • Remember: The vein brings oxygenated blood from placenta to fetus; the arteries return blood toward the placenta.

Module 2

Origin and development of organ systems

  • Recognize: Ectoderm forms skin/nervous tissue; mesoderm forms muscle, bone, blood and much cardiovascular/urinary tissue; endoderm forms much digestive/respiratory lining.
  • Act: Link exposure timing to developing organ systems and reinforce early prenatal care and prescribed folate.
  • Remember: Structural vulnerability is greatest during organ formation; growth and functional maturation continue later in pregnancy.

Module 2

AFP / chorionic villi / amniocentesis

  • Recognize: AFP is screening; CVS samples placental tissue and amniocentesis samples amniotic fluid for diagnostic testing.
  • Act: Explain purpose, risks and follow-up; support informed choice and ordered assessments before and after invasive testing.
  • Remember: Typical windows: CVS 10–13 weeks; AFP and amniocentesis commonly 15–20 weeks. An abnormal screen is not a confirmed diagnosis.

Module 2

Evaluating fetal wellbeing

  • Recognize: Fetal movement, NST, biophysical profile and contraction stress testing assess wellbeing. A reactive NST and negative CST are reassuring.
  • Act: Report decreased movement promptly; arrange prescribed follow-up for nonreactive or concerning results.
  • Remember: At 32 weeks or later, reactive NST means two accelerations of at least 15 beats/min for 15 seconds in 20 minutes.

Module 2

EDC/Nagel’s rule

  • Recognize: Naegele's rule estimates the due date from the first day of the last menstrual period.
  • Act: Subtract three months, add seven days and adjust the year; compare the estimate with menstrual history and ultrasound dating.
  • Remember: The rule assumes a regular 28-day cycle. It estimates a date rather than guaranteeing the day of birth.

Module 3 · Pregnancy: psychological and physiological changes 5 short topics

Module 3

Psychological changes of pregnancy

  • Recognize: Ambivalence, shifting attention and changing body image can occur as pregnancy becomes real. Readiness, culture, relationships and support shape adjustment.
  • Act: Invite concerns without judgment and assess coping, anxiety, depression, relationship safety and practical support.
  • Remember: Trimester tasks are a teaching framework: accept pregnancy, accept the fetus, then prepare for birth; people do not follow identical emotional stages.

Module 3

Physiological changes of pregnancy (trimester tasks)

  • Recognize: Blood volume and cardiac workload rise; renal filtration increases, and progesterone contributes to reflux and constipation. The growing uterus changes posture and venous return.
  • Act: Teach comfort measures and left-side positioning for supine hypotension; assess unexpected or severe symptoms.
  • Remember: Plasma expands more than red-cell mass, causing dilutional anemia; this is different from proven iron deficiency.

Module 3

Emotional responses

  • Recognize: Mood shifts and mixed feelings may reflect changing roles, body image and expectations. Support and prior mental-health history influence the response.
  • Act: Ask what the pregnancy means to the person and family; assess persistent distress, impaired functioning and safety concerns.
  • Remember: Early ambivalence alone does not prove rejection, but persistent depression or anxiety should not be dismissed as normal pregnancy.

Module 3

Couvade syndrome

  • Recognize: Some partners develop pregnancy-like physical or emotional symptoms, described as couvade syndrome. Their experience may accompany adjustment to the pregnancy and parenting role.
  • Act: Include the partner in discussion and teaching while assessing individual concerns and support needs.
  • Remember: Couvade describes a partner's response; it does not establish pregnancy or justify ignoring persistent symptoms needing evaluation.

Module 3

Signs of pregnancy (presumptive, probable, positive)

  • Recognize: Presumptive signs are subjective symptoms; probable signs include positive hCG testing and cervical changes; positive signs come directly from the fetus.
  • Act: Confirm pregnancy through appropriate clinical evaluation rather than symptoms alone.
  • Remember: Amenorrhea/quickening are presumptive; a positive pregnancy test is probable; fetal heart activity, ultrasound visualization or examiner-felt movement are positive.

Module 4 · Prenatal care and screening 7 short topics

Module 4

Prenatal care/screening

  • Recognize: Prenatal visits combine history, maternal/fetal assessment, screening and education to identify problems early. Needs vary with gestation and risk.
  • Act: Review medicines, psychosocial needs and warning signs; encourage scheduled visits and arrange barriers-to-care support.
  • Remember: Routine textbook visits become more frequent as birth approaches; high-risk findings require an individualized plan rather than waiting for the next routine visit.

Module 4

Terms related to pregnancy status

  • Recognize: Gravida counts pregnancies; para counts pregnancies reaching the course's gestational threshold, regardless of the number of infants or survival.
  • Act: Obtain the complete pregnancy history, including current pregnancy, losses, gestational ages and living children.
  • Remember: Twins count as one pregnancy and one qualifying birth event; they may count as two living children.

Module 4

Gestational classification/history / GTPAL

  • Recognize: GTPAL separates total pregnancies, term births, preterm births, losses and living children.
  • Act: Count each pregnancy outcome by gestational age, then count living children separately; include the current pregnancy in gravida.
  • Remember: Course thresholds: term at least 37 weeks; preterm 20–36 weeks 6 days; losses before 20 weeks. Multiples do not multiply the pregnancy count.

Module 4

Fundal height and fetal heart tones

  • Recognize: Fundal height follows gestational landmarks; Doppler fetal heart tones are generally heard around 10–12 weeks.
  • Act: Compare serial fundal measurements and fetal heart findings with dating and the clinical picture; investigate discrepancies rather than diagnosing from one measurement.
  • Remember: Fundus is near the pubic symphysis at 12 weeks and umbilicus at 20 weeks; ultrasound establishes dating more accurately.

Module 4

Pelvic anatomy / pelvic exam

  • Recognize: The pelvic inlet, midpelvis and outlet form the passage; ischial spines mark fetal station. Pelvic examination may include external, speculum and bimanual assessment.
  • Act: Explain the examination, obtain consent, preserve privacy and stop if consent is withdrawn.
  • Remember: Pelvic shape labels alone do not determine birth outcome; station is measured relative to the ischial spines.

Module 4

Laboratory screenings

  • Recognize: Initial screening commonly includes CBC, blood type/Rh, antibody screen, urine culture and selected infection/immunity tests. Later tests address diabetes and GBS.
  • Act: Verify timing, review results, arrange indicated follow-up and explain the purpose of each screen.
  • Remember: A screening result identifies risk or need for evaluation; it does not replace diagnostic confirmation or clinical assessment.

Module 4

Danger signs during pregnancy

  • Recognize: Bleeding, fluid leakage, reduced fetal movement, severe headache, visual change, chest pain or breathing difficulty need assessment.
  • Act: Obtain relevant maternal/fetal findings and contact the obstetric team urgently; do not dismiss new severe symptoms as ordinary discomfort.
  • Remember: Mild expected adaptations differ from abrupt, persistent or severe changes. Visual symptoms with headache raise concern for hypertensive complications.

Every exam: priority + dose checks
  • Clinical judgment: Recognize cues → analyze → prioritize → plan → act → evaluate. Compare with baseline; reassess the response.
  • Dose safety: Verify patient, allergies, drug, dose, route and time. Use kilograms for weight-based doses; check the safe range and maximum.
  • Calculate: Ordered dose ÷ available dose × volume. Example: 250 mg ÷ 125 mg × 5 mL = 10 mL. Clarify an unsafe or unclear order before giving it.

Rules to remember

  • Match the method to pregnancy goals and STI protection.
  • Trace where oxygen enters fetal circulation.
  • Separate evidence of pregnancy from maternal symptoms.
  • Use the prenatal history and the fetal assessment together.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

  • GTPAL: count pregnancies separately from births. Twins count as one pregnancy and one delivery in their gestational-age category, but two living children if both survive.
  • Gravida includes the current pregnancy. For an uncomplicated twin birth at term with both children living and no other pregnancies, G1 T1 P0 A0 L2.

Practice and memory cues

A missed period is a symptom; fetal cardiac activity on ultrasound is direct evidence.

A client is pregnant. She has one child born at 39 weeks, one child born at 34 weeks, and a pregnancy loss at 10 weeks. Both children are living. What is her GTPAL?

  1. 4-1-1-1-2
  2. 3-1-1-1-2
  3. 4-2-0-1-2
  4. 4-1-1-0-3
Check the answer and why

4-1-1-1-2

Gravida counts all pregnancies including the current one , giving 4. Term (37 weeks or more) is 1, preterm (20 to under 37 weeks) is 1, abortions (under 20 weeks) is 1, and living children is 2.

A client at 6 weeks of gestation is upset that the nurse could not find the fetal heartbeat with a Doppler. Which response is accurate?

  1. Heart tones are usually audible by Doppler at 10 to 12 weeks
  2. Heart tones should have been audible by 4 weeks, so an ultrasound is needed today
  3. Heart tones are not audible by any method until quickening at 16 weeks
  4. Heart tones are audible only after the lungs mature at 34 weeks
Check the answer and why

Heart tones are usually audible by Doppler at 10 to 12 weeks

Doppler detects fetal heart tones at 10 to 12 weeks , so absence at 6 weeks is expected. Four weeks is a standing distractor, and the other options confuse the quickening and lung maturity numbers with the Doppler number.
