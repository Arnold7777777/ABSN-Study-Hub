Rules to remember

  • Bleeding with pain or faintness changes the priority.
  • Check breathing, reflexes and urine output during magnesium therapy.
  • Recognize a delivery emergency from the sequence of events.

See this → think this

An early-pregnancy patient has one-sided pelvic pain, vaginal spotting and new dizziness.

Think…
Possible ectopic pregnancy. Faintness or hypotension can signal internal bleeding.

A patient receiving magnesium sulfate becomes drowsy with slow breathing and absent reflexes.

Think…
Magnesium toxicity can suppress breathing. Poor renal clearance increases risk.

The head delivers and draws back against the perineum. The shoulders do not follow.

Think…
The anterior shoulder may be trapped behind the pubic bone: shoulder dystocia.

What do I do first?

Early Pregnancy Bleeding & Ectopic Pregnancy

First action
Assess circulation and summon urgent obstetric assistance. Prepare IV access and evaluation.
Look for a response
Trend mental status, pulse and blood pressure while definitive care is arranged.

Hypertensive Disorders & Magnesium Sulfate

First action
Stop the infusion, call for help and support ventilation. Prepare prescribed calcium gluconate.
Look for a response
Reassess respiratory effort, alertness, reflexes and urine output.

Dysfunctional Labor, Uterine Rupture & Obstetric Drug Math

First action
Call for help and assist with McRoberts positioning and suprapubic pressure as directed. Avoid fundal pressure.
Look for a response
After delivery, assess newborn breathing and arm movement and assess maternal bleeding.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

Blood pressure at or above 160 systolic or 110 diastolic in pregnancy is severe range. Notify the team promptly and confirm according to the urgent pathway.

Painless bleeding raises concern for previa; painful bleeding with a tender uterus suggests abruption. Either needs urgent assessment. Avoid digital vaginal examination until previa is excluded.

Practice and memory cues

McRoberts flexes the maternal hips. Suprapubic pressure is applied above the pubic bone by a trained team member. Fundal pressure can worsen shoulder impaction.

A client arrives in the emergency department with a suspected unruptured ectopic pregnancy. She is alert with stable vital signs. Which action does the nurse take first?

  1. Establish large-bore intravenous access
  2. Place her in Trendelenburg position
  3. Prepare her immediately for laparoscopic surgery
  4. Administer a folic acid supplement
Check the answer and why

Establish large-bore intravenous access

Large-bore IV access comes first so fluids and blood can be given instantly if she ruptures, along with continuous vital signs and methotrexate as prescribed. Trendelenburg is not indicated, surgery is not automatic for an unruptured ectopic, and folic acid is contraindicated with methotrexate.

A provider prescribes oxytocin at 3 milliunits/min for labor augmentation. The pharmacy supplies 20 units of oxytocin in 1,000 mL of lactated Ringer's. How many mL/hr does the nurse set on the pump?

  1. 3 mL/hr
  2. 9 mL/hr
  3. 15 mL/hr
  4. 30 mL/hr
Check the answer and why

9 mL/hr

20 units = 20,000 milliunits in 1,000 mL, so the bag is 20 milliunits/mL . Then 3 milliunits/min divided by 20 milliunits/mL = 0.15 mL/min, and 0.15 x 60 = 9 mL/hr . Skipping that final x60 leaves 0.15 and is the single most commonly missed step.
