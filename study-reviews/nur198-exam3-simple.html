Rules to remember

Match the electrical rhythm to perfusion. Distinguish pulmonary congestion, systemic congestion and symptoms suggesting acute coronary syndrome.

What do I do first?

Coronary disease, rhythms and hypertension

  1. First action
    • Assess ABCs, vital signs and symptoms, activate the chest-pain pathway and obtain a prompt 12-lead ECG per protocol.
  2. Look for a response
    • Trend pain, rhythm, perfusion and ordered serial troponin results. Escalate clinical deterioration.

Heart failure, valves and vascular disorders

  1. First action
    • Position a breathless patient upright as tolerated, assess oxygenation and obtain prompt evaluation. Trend daily weights and response to prescribed therapy.
  2. Look for a response
    • Reassess breathlessness, lung sounds, perfusion, urine output and weight trends.

Topics in plain words and pictures

Module 7 · Coronary disease, rhythms and hypertension

  • Compare stable exertional symptoms with new, worsening or persistent symptoms. Chest pain can be absent, especially in older adults.
  • New pressure at rest, diaphoresis, dyspnea or nausea can indicate acute coronary syndrome. A rhythm change should be interpreted alongside the patient’s perfusion.

Module 8 · Heart failure, valves and vascular disorders

  • Mitral stenosis restricts flow from the left atrium to the left ventricle. Compare arterial insufficiency with venous swelling when assessing a limb.
  • Orthopnea and crackles suggest pulmonary congestion. Jugular venous distention and peripheral edema suggest systemic congestion. Both can coexist.

Numbers and distinctions

  • Pulmonary congestion commonly causes orthopnea and crackles. Systemic congestion can produce dependent edema and jugular venous distention.
  • Look at symptoms, pulse and blood pressure when deciding how urgently a rhythm change needs treatment.

Practice and memory cues

A patient has new chest pressure, nausea and diaphoresis at rest. What should the nurse do first?

  1. Schedule routine teaching.
  2. Activate the chest-pain response and obtain a prompt ECG.
  3. Encourage a walk to reproduce the pain.
Check the answer and why

B. Activate the chest-pain response and obtain a prompt ECG.

Symptoms can reflect acute myocardial ischemia. Prompt assessment and ECG guide further care.

Which finding most directly suggests pulmonary congestion?

  1. Crackles with dyspnea when lying flat.
  2. Discoloration over an ankle alone.
  3. An isolated callus.
Check the answer and why

A. Crackles with dyspnea when lying flat.

Pulmonary fluid accumulation can cause crackles and orthopnea.
