Rules to remember

Connect a change in mentation or breathing to the patient’s baseline, recent medicines and fluid status.

  • Falls, medication effects, sensory loss and functional changes often interact. Verify hearing aids, glasses, mobility aids and usual assistance.
  • Compare pH first, then PaCO₂ and bicarbonate. Typical arterial ranges are pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg and HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L.
  • Before surgery, verify identity, consent and required checks. After surgery, assess airway, pain, circulation, wound and mobility.

What do I do first?

Older adults, chronic illness and disability

  1. First action
    • Assess airway, breathing and vital signs, then investigate new confusion and reversible causes with the team.
  2. Look for a response
    • Look for return toward baseline attention and safer transfers. Recheck after interventions.

Fluid, electrolytes and acid–base balance

  1. First action
    • Assess the patient’s breathing, mental status, fluid balance and recent losses. Address the immediate problem and review the trend.
  2. Look for a response
    • Recheck breathing, intake and output, weight and relevant labs after treatment.

Perioperative care, pain and skin integrity

  1. First action
    • For shallow breathing, assess responsiveness and ventilation, summon help and support the airway per protocol. Reposition and protect vulnerable skin.
  2. Look for a response
    • Reassess sedation and respiratory effort after analgesia. Check skin during position changes.

Topics in plain words and pictures

Module 1 · Older adults, chronic illness and disability

  • Falls, medication effects, sensory loss and functional changes often interact. Verify hearing aids, glasses, mobility aids and usual assistance.
  • New inattention that varies over hours suggests delirium. Ask someone who knows the patient when the change began.

Module 2 · Fluid, electrolytes and acid–base balance

  • Compare pH first, then PaCO₂ and bicarbonate. Typical arterial ranges are pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg and HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L.
  • Slow, shallow breathing can retain CO₂ and lower pH. Loss of gastric acid through prolonged vomiting favors metabolic alkalosis.

Module 3 · Perioperative care, pain and skin integrity

  • Before surgery, verify identity, consent and required checks. After surgery, assess airway, pain, circulation, wound and mobility.
  • Increasing sedation with shallow breathing after an opioid is an urgent cue. Nonblanchable redness over a pressure point needs offloading.

Numbers and distinctions

  • Typical adult serum potassium is 3.5–5.0 mEq/L. Both low and high levels can alter cardiac conduction.
  • Typical arterial pH is 7.35–7.45, PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg and bicarbonate 22–26 mEq/L. Read these together.

Practice and memory cues

A postoperative patient is difficult to awaken and breathing shallowly after an opioid. Which action takes priority?

  1. Offer more analgesia.
  2. Assess and support breathing and call for assistance.
  3. Begin discharge teaching.
Check the answer and why

B. Assess and support breathing and call for assistance.

Increasing sedation with shallow breathing can signal impaired ventilation.

An older adult becomes confused over a few hours. Which question best guides your assessment?

  1. How many birthdays have they celebrated?
  2. Have they ever needed glasses?
  3. When were they last at their usual level of attention?
Check the answer and why

C. When were they last at their usual level of attention?

Time of onset helps identify an acute change that needs evaluation.
