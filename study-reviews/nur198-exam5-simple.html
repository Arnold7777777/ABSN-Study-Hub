Rules to remember

Use bleeding, perfusion and signs of an acute abdomen to decide which patient you would see first.

  • Reflux causes heartburn or regurgitation. Sudden severe pain with a rigid abdomen in ulcer disease raises concern for perforation.
  • Crohn disease can affect separated segments throughout the GI tract. Ulcerative colitis usually starts in the rectum and extends continuously through the colon.

Why is faintness with black tarry stools more urgent than isolated heartburn?

Think…
  • The combination suggests bleeding that may be compromising circulation.

Why does worsening continuous pain in a suspected obstruction need urgent reassessment?

Think…
  • It can signal ischemia or perforation.

What do I do first?

Upper GI disorders and procedures

  1. First action
    • Assess airway, circulation, vital signs and signs of shock. Obtain prompt evaluation and prepare ordered IV access, labs and treatment.
  2. Look for a response
    • Reassess mentation, perfusion and ongoing blood loss. Watch for escalating abdominal pain or rigidity.

Lower GI, bowel obstruction and inflammatory bowel disease

  1. First action
    • Assess abdominal findings, fluid losses and circulation. With suspected obstruction, keep NPO, notify the team and prepare for prescribed decompression or fluids.
  2. Look for a response
    • Trend output, pain, hydration and abdominal findings. Escalate new peritoneal signs.

Topics in plain words and pictures

Module 11 · Upper GI disorders and procedures

  • Reflux causes heartburn or regurgitation. Sudden severe pain with a rigid abdomen in ulcer disease raises concern for perforation.
  • Coffee-ground emesis, hematemesis or black tarry stool can indicate GI bleeding. Dizziness or falling pressure suggests impaired perfusion.

Module 12 · Lower GI, bowel obstruction and inflammatory bowel disease

  • Crohn disease can affect separated segments throughout the GI tract. Ulcerative colitis usually starts in the rectum and extends continuously through the colon.
  • Distention, cramping, vomiting and reduced stool or gas can suggest obstruction. Continuous severe pain, fever or rigidity raises concern for a complication.

Numbers and distinctions

  • Coffee-ground emesis suggests upper GI bleeding. Assess circulation and ongoing losses.
  • A new rigid abdomen or continuous severe pain raises concern for perforation or ischemia.

Practice and memory cues

Which finding during assessment of a patient with peptic ulcer disease needs immediate escalation?

  1. Mild heartburn after a meal.
  2. Sudden severe pain with a rigid abdomen.
  3. A history of belching.
Check the answer and why

B. Sudden severe pain with a rigid abdomen.

Acute pain with rigidity raises concern for perforation.

Which finding suggests that a bowel obstruction may be worsening?

  1. Pain becomes continuous with increasing tenderness.
  2. Distention resolves.
  3. The patient resumes passing gas comfortably.
Check the answer and why

A. Pain becomes continuous with increasing tenderness.

Continuous pain and tenderness raise concern for ischemia or a developing complication.
