First actions

Magnesium toxicity

  • Stop the infusion;
  • summon help and support breathing.

Oxytocin + tachysystole / concerning fetal tracing

  • Turn off oxytocin and initiate the ordered response.

Cord prolapse

  • Call for help and lift the presenting part off the cord.

Shoulder dystocia

  • Call for help;
  • assist with McRoberts and suprapubic pressure.

Orthopnea with cardiac disease

  • Sit her up.

Suspected abruption

  • Left side, oxygen, large-bore IV access and urgent evaluation.

Placenta previa bleeding

  • Assess bleeding and fetal heart tones;
  • no digital vaginal examination.

Severe-range blood pressure

  • Prompt assessment and antihypertensive treatment per protocol.

Newborn of a diabetic mother

  • Early feeding and glucose checks per neonatal protocol.

Suspected uterine rupture

  • Emergency surgical response.

Hypotonic labor with full bladder

  • Empty the bladder.

Amniotomy

  • Assess fetal heart tones before and after.

Module 8 · Pregnancy complications

Cardiac disease in pregnancy · guide 8.1

Priority assessment

  • VS often;
  • lung sounds (crackles);
  • dyspnea/orthopnea;
  • edema;
  • daily weight.
  • Highest strain 28–32 wks and right after placenta delivery.

Nursing interventions

  • Rest (2 periods/day + full night);
  • low sodium;
  • meds exactly as prescribed;
  • HOB up + left tilt;
  • epidural;
  • assisted vaginal delivery;
  • continuous FHR;
  • SCDs;
  • stool softeners.

Safety precautions

  • Never supine in labor.
  • Watch for cardiac collapse/HF right after the placenta delivers (fluid shift).
  • Left-sided failure is worse for the fetus.

Common exam trap

  • Left = lungs (crackles, orthopnea);
  • JVD/ascites are RIGHT-sided.
  • Constipation and nausea at 8 wks are normal;
  • 4 lb in a week is not.
  • HF drugs aren't tested.

Venous thromboembolism (DVT) · guide 8.3

Priority assessment

  • Unilateral calf swelling, warmth, redness, tenderness;
  • compare calf diameters.
  • Ultrasound, PTT/INR.

Nursing interventions

  • Ambulate (best), hydrate, SCDs, prophylactic anticoagulant for high-risk post-cesarean.

Safety precautions

  • Bleeding on anticoagulants;
  • delivery may be timed with the drug stopped ~24 h before.

Common exam trap

  • Anticoagulants do NOT dissolve the clot.
  • D-dimer is unreliable in pregnancy.

UTI / pyelonephritis · guide 8.4

Priority assessment

  • Urine dip every visit (nitrites, WBC, glucose, protein).
  • Pyelo: flank pain on tapping over kidney, fever, uterine irritability.

Nursing interventions

  • Pyelo = inpatient IV antibiotics, fetal monitoring, stay until afebrile and not contracting.
  • Teach: wipe front to back, void after sex, fluids, cotton, no bubble baths.

Safety precautions

  • Asymptomatic UTI can become pyelo, sepsis, and preterm labor.

Common exam trap

  • Back labor is rhythmic, back- to-front;
  • pyelo pain is over the kidney and reproduced by tapping. "Holding urine" and bubble baths are wrong answers.

Diabetes / GDM · guide 8.5

Priority assessment

  • Screen everyone 24–28 wks (earlier if high risk). 1-h > 140 → 3-h test;
  • 2 abnormal values = GDM.
  • Newborn: heel-stick glucose;
  • jittery, lethargic, poor feeding.

Nursing interventions

  • Carb control (complex carbs), ~30 min exercise daily, glucose diary, insulin adjustments in 2nd/3rd trimester, growth US, NSTs, kick counts.

Safety precautions

  • Newborn hypoglycemia: feed within 30 min of birth.
  • Macrosomia → shoulder dystocia risk.
  • GDM → higher lifetime type 2 risk.

Common exam trap

  • Insulin does NOT cross the placenta (glucose does).
  • One abnormal value ≠ GDM.
  • Type 1/2 clients don't get a GTT.
  • Values are given;
  • don't memorize the chart.

Module 9 · Sudden pregnancy complications

Vaginal bleeding / spontaneous abortion · guide 9.0–9.1

Priority assessment

  • Gestational age;
  • amount (count pads), color, clots, tissue;
  • pain;
  • Rh type;
  • shock signs (↓BP, ↑HR, pale, clammy).

Nursing interventions

  • "Come in to be examined and save any tissue." Sort SAB by tissue lost.
  • RhoGAM if Rh- negative.

Safety precautions

  • Missed abortion/retained stillbirth → infection, septic shock, DIC.

Common exam trap

  • Bed rest, a tampon, or "spotting is harmless" are wrong.
  • Threatened = closed cervix, FHT present (most hopeful).

Ectopic pregnancy · guide 9.2

Priority assessment

  • Sudden one-sided lower abdominal pain + shock signs (case: BP 95/58, HR 132).

Nursing interventions

  • Ruptured → surgery.
  • Unruptured → methotrexate: stop prenatal vitamins/folic acid, no alcohol, serial hCG, wait months to conceive.

Safety precautions

  • Ruptured ectopic = hemorrhage emergency.
  • RhoGAM if Rh-negative.

Common exam trap

  • IUD, PID, tubal ligation, endometriosis are risk factors.
  • Methotrexate is only for UNruptured.

Molar pregnancy (GTD) · guide 9.3

Priority assessment

  • Very high hCG, uterus larger than dates, dark brown "sludgy" bleeding, grape-like tissue, early preeclampsia signs.

Nursing interventions

  • D&C with suction;
  • serial hCG follow-up.

Safety precautions

  • Delay pregnancy 6 months–1 year;
  • retained tissue can regrow (choriocarcinoma).

Common exam trap

  • A rising hCG during follow-up can't be told apart from a new pregnancy, which is why she must wait.

Cerclage (cervical insufficiency) · guide 9.4

Priority assessment

  • Painless dilation history;
  • signs of labor or ROM.

Nursing interventions

  • Nothing in the vagina, no tub soaks, take it easy.

Safety precautions

  • Remove at 37 wks, or early if labor or ROM (or the cervix tears).
  • Abdominal cerclage → cesarean.

Common exam trap

  • Cerclage is for painless dilation, not labor.

Previa vs abruption · guide 9.5

Priority assessment

  • Previa: painless bright red, soft uterus.
  • Abruption: painful dark red, board-like uterus, FHR drifting down → bradycardia.

Nursing interventions

  • Previa: assess bleeding + FHT, CBC, T&S.
  • Abruption: left side, O2, 2nd large-bore IV, CBC/T&S/coags, emergent delivery.

Safety precautions

  • NO vaginal exams with previa.
  • Abruption can be concealed;
  • DIC risk.

Common exam trap

  • A slowly falling FHR baseline with bloody show can be an early abruption.
  • Complete previa = cesarean.

DIC · guide 9.6

Priority assessment

  • Petechiae, purpura, oozing IV sites, gum/nose bleeding.
  • Labs: platelets ↓, fibrinogen ↓, PT/PTT ↑.

Nursing interventions

  • Volume expanders, blood products, keep warm, ICU care.

Safety precautions

  • Confirm type & screen, CBC, coags are actually drawn before procedures.

Common exam trap

  • Fibrinogen is LOW, not high.
  • D-dimer isn't reliable in pregnancy.

Preterm labor · guide 9.7

Priority assessment

  • Regular contractions, pelvic pressure, cervical change, nitrites (infection). fFN, UA, cultures.

Nursing interventions

  • Hydrate, treat infection.
  • Terbutaline subcut (buys time).
  • Betamethasone IM before 34 wks (2 doses, 24 h apart).

Safety precautions

  • Hold terbutaline if maternal HR > 120 (cardiac boxed warning).

Common exam trap

  • Terbutaline doesn't mature lungs;
  • betamethasone does.
  • A positive fFN does NOT mean she will deliver.

Clinical side note · Positive-pressure rooms are not routine for every low WBC count

CDC protective-environment recommendations specifically address allogeneic stem-cell transplant recipients. Other patients need individualized infection precautions; an airborne infection can change room requirements. Source

Preeclampsia / HELLP / magnesium · guide 9.8

Priority assessment

  • BP, headache not relieved by Tylenol, vision changes, epigastric pain, brisk DTRs/clonus, urine output, RR, LOC.
  • HELLP: AST/ALT ↑, platelets ↓.

Nursing interventions

  • BP ≥ 160/110 → antihypertensive within ~30 min.
  • Mag: low stimulation, IV fluids ≤ 125 mL/h, VS q30 min, strict I&O, continuous FHR.

Safety precautions

  • Mag toxicity: STOP infusion → mag level → calcium gluconate → provider.
  • PPH risk after birth.
  • Platelets < 100,000 = no epidural.

Common exam trap

  • Warm and flushed is EXPECTED on mag, not toxicity.
  • The first action is stopping the drip, not giving calcium.

Clinical side note · Magnesium toxicity: do not wait for the lab result

Stop magnesium and summon urgent help. Support breathing and give calcium gluconate as ordered or per emergency protocol. Draw ordered labs without delaying emergency treatment. Source

Module 9 continued · Module 10 labor

Rh / amniotic fluid / twins · guide 9.9–9.11

Priority assessment

  • Maternal Rh type and Coombs.
  • Oligo < 300 mL (renal);
  • poly > 2000 mL (GI).

Nursing interventions

  • RhoGAM at 28 wks, within 72 h of birth if baby Rh+, and any time blood may mix.

Safety precautions

  • Fluid problems raise cord prolapse, PPH, and prolonged labor risk.

Common exam trap

  • RhoGAM protects FUTURE pregnancies.
  • Identical twins share a placenta (higher risk).

GBS · guide 10.1

Priority assessment

  • GBS status from ~36-wk swab (sooner in preterm labor).

Nursing interventions

  • Penicillin in labor every 4 h;
  • ≥ 2 doses before birth = covered.

Safety precautions

  • Not covered → increased newborn surveillance for infection.

Common exam trap

  • GBS is NOT an STI.
  • Swab even if a cesarean is planned.

Power problems / dysfunctional labor · guide 10.2

Priority assessment

  • Contraction frequency, duration, resting tone;
  • bladder;
  • dilation (stage 1) vs descent (stage 2).

Nursing interventions

  • Hypotonic: empty bladder, oxytocin, amniotomy.
  • Hypertonic: stop oxytocin, fluids, reposition, terbutaline if it persists.

Safety precautions

  • Full bladder blocks descent, weakens contractions, raises hemorrhage risk.
  • Goal: q2–3 min with ≥ 60 s rest.

Common exam trap

  • Stage 1 problem = dilation;
  • stage 2 problem = descent.
  • Arrest = halted despite everything.

Oxytocin / cervical ripening · guide 10.4–10.5

Priority assessment

  • Before oxytocin: head engaged, vertex, ripe cervix (Bishop ≥ 8), category I/II strip, no CPD.
  • Contractions and FHR q30 min.

Nursing interventions

  • Own pump, LR main line, port closest to the client.
  • Titrate up if q5 min;
  • leave if q2–3 min.
  • Prostaglandin first if Bishop is low.

Safety precautions

  • Stop for > 5 contractions/10 min, a contraction > 2 min, or a bad strip.
  • Remove Cervidil (tape) for tachysystole/distress.
  • Watch water intoxication.

Common exam trap

  • Never piggyback oxytocin. "It is not a race." Apply the Bishop chart, don't memorize it.

Uterine rupture / inversion / AFE · guide 10.6

Priority assessment

  • Rupture: sudden excruciating pain (prior cesarean scar).
  • Inversion: excessive bleeding.
  • AFE: sudden dyspnea, cyanosis, arrest, DIC.

Nursing interventions

  • Rupture → OR, O2, fluids, blood.
  • Inversion → provider replaces uterus.
  • AFE → emergency response, DIC care.

Safety precautions

  • Avoid excess fundal pressure and pulling on the cord (inversion).

Common exam trap

  • Inversion is never normal.
  • Sudden acute pain in labor is never normal.

Umbilical cord prolapse · guide 10.7

Priority assessment

  • Sudden FHR drop after ROM;
  • cord felt or seen.
  • Risk: high station at ROM, preterm, polyhydramnios, transverse.

Nursing interventions

  • Lift presenting part off the cord and hold;
  • call for help;
  • O2;
  • prepare emergency cesarean;
  • saline-soaked towel on exposed cord.

Safety precautions

  • Don't get her up if ruptured with a high station.

Common exam trap

  • Knee-chest is NOT the first action;
  • it's a temporary band- aid.
  • Don't let go until the OR.

Shoulder dystocia / macrosomia · guide 10.8

Priority assessment

  • Risk: GDM, macrosomia > 4000 g, post-dates.
  • After birth: arm movement (Erb's), clavicle crepitus.

Nursing interventions

  • Call for help + NICU;
  • McRoberts;
  • suprapubic pressure when directed;
  • record head and body times.

Safety precautions

  • ~5 min before serious hypoxia;
  • be ready to resuscitate.

Common exam trap

  • NEVER fundal pressure.

Clinical side note · Shoulder dystocia: correct the picture labels

McRoberts means sharply flexing the mother’s hips, with knees toward the chest. Suprapubic pressure is a separate maneuver. Never use fundal pressure. Evaluate whether episiotomy is needed for access; it does not release the bony obstruction itself. Call for help immediately. Source

Malpresentation / ECV / CPD · guide 10.9–10.10

Priority assessment

  • Position (OP, breech, transverse, face/brow);
  • pelvimetry for inlet/outlet contraction.

Nursing interventions

  • ECV: empty bladder, US in room, FHT, terbutaline, towel under right hip.
  • Trial of labor if CPD borderline.

Safety precautions

  • ECV needs ≥ 37 wks, adequate fluid, single fetus, reactive NST.
  • Transverse lie → cord prolapse risk.

Common exam trap

  • ECV is scheduled, not done in labor.
  • Breech, transverse, face/brow usually → cesarean;
  • OP can deliver vaginally.

Module 10 · Birth procedures

Forceps / vacuum · guide 10.11

Priority assessment

  • Full dilation and effacement, ROM, head engaged, bladder empty.

Nursing interventions

  • Empty bladder before the device;
  • assess newborn for bruising, cephalohematoma.

Safety precautions

  • Max 3 vacuum pop-offs.
  • Only a skilled provider uses them.

Common exam trap

  • 9 cm is NOT enough (a 9 cm forceps case hemorrhaged).

Cord & placenta anomalies · guide 10.12

Priority assessment

  • Vasa previa: red blood with ROM + sudden bradycardia.
  • Two-vessel cord at delivery.

Nursing interventions

  • Vasa previa: surveillance, scheduled cesarean.
  • Two-vessel cord: work up for cardiac/renal anomalies.

Safety precautions

  • Accreta spectrum → major hemorrhage;
  • percreta may need hysterectomy.

Common exam trap

  • Vasa previa blood is FETAL.
  • Accreta = endometrium, increta = myometrium, percreta = through.

Amniotomy / internal monitoring / scalp stim · guide 10.13–10.15

Priority assessment

  • FHT before and after AROM;
  • fluid color, odor, amount, time.
  • FSE/IUPC need ROM + ≥ 3 cm.

Nursing interventions

  • Be ready for meconium.
  • IUPC allows amnioinfusion.

Safety precautions

  • AROM → cord prolapse, AFE.
  • Internal devices → infection, limited mobility.

Common exam trap

  • Most important AROM step = FHT.
  • Scalp stim: an acceleration is reassuring.

Cesarean birth · guide 10.16

Priority assessment

  • Indications: scheduled (CPD, malpresentation, HSV, prior classical), emergency (distress, abruption, prolapse, arrest).

Nursing interventions

  • NPO after midnight, teaching, IV, prep;
  • SCDs;
  • early ambulation.

Safety precautions

  • Hemorrhage, infection, organ injury;
  • newborn respiratory issues and cold stress.

Common exam trap

  • Classical incision = no vaginal birth later (rupture risk).

Common exam traps

  1. Choosing a normal pregnancy change (nausea, constipation, frequency, foot edema) as the "abnormal" finding.
  2. Treating the monitor instead of the patient.
  3. Mixing up left (lungs) and right (body) heart failure.
  4. Thinking insulin crosses the placenta.
  5. Diagnosing GDM from one abnormal GTT value.
  6. Telling a possible miscarriage to stay home on bed rest instead of coming in.
  7. Doing a vaginal exam on a client with placenta previa.
  8. Calling warmth/flushing on magnesium a sign of toxicity.
  9. Giving calcium gluconate before stopping the magnesium.
  10. Giving terbutaline with maternal HR over 120.
  11. Expecting terbutaline (not betamethasone) to mature fetal lungs.
  12. Reading a positive fFN as "she will deliver."
  13. Expecting high fibrinogen or trusting D-dimer in DIC.
  14. Thinking anticoagulants dissolve the clot.
  15. Increasing oxytocin when contractions are already every 2–3 minutes.
  16. Piggybacking oxytocin or connecting it far from the client.
  17. Positioning first (knee-chest) instead of lifting the head off a prolapsed cord.
  18. Fundal pressure during a shoulder dystocia.
  19. Forceps or vacuum before full dilation.
  20. Treating vasa previa bleeding as maternal blood.
  21. Calling GBS a sexually transmitted infection.
  22. Offering a trial of labor after a classical incision.
