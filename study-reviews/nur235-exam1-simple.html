Rules to remember

  • Choose a pain tool the child can use.
  • Adjust infant care to development and sleep safety.
  • Offer a toddler two acceptable choices.
  • Match explanations to the child's stage of thinking.

What do I do first?

Intro to Pediatric Nursing

First action
Score face, legs, activity, cry and consolability. Provide appropriate pain relief.
Look for a response
Repeat the same scale after the intervention and compare with caregiver observations.

The Infant (0–1 year)

First action
Teach safe sleep using an empty crib and ask the caregiver to demonstrate.
Look for a response
The caregiver shows the sleep setup without loose blankets, pillows or toys.

Toddler & Preschooler

First action
Offer a small choice such as which arm to check and keep the caregiver nearby.
Look for a response
The child participates with less distress and the needed assessment is completed.

School-Age & Adolescent

First action
Show the supplies, explain the steps and give the child a manageable job.
Look for a response
The child can describe the sequence and identify a coping strategy.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

FLACC has five behaviors scored 0–2 each, for a total of 0–10. Follow the change after an intervention.

Use serial growth measurements and a developmentally appropriate chart. A trend is more informative than a single percentile.

Practice and memory cues

Parallel play fits toddlers; cooperative rule-based play becomes more prominent in school-age children. Match teaching to what the child can understand.

A nurse observes a parent settle a 2-month-old for a nap prone on a soft quilt with crib bumpers in place. Which action should the nurse take first?

  1. Document the observation in the record
  2. Reposition the infant supine on a bare, firm mattress
  3. Ask the parent why she prefers the prone position
  4. Plan to teach safe sleep at the discharge visit
Check the answer and why

Reposition the infant supine on a bare, firm mattress

Safe sleep is supine in a bare, firm crib with no co-sleeping, and an unsafe sleep surface is an active airway risk — remove it now, then teach. Teaching later does not protect the infant currently in the crib.

A nurse is counseling a 16-year-old who has just been licensed to drive. Which instruction is the priority?

  1. Keep an emergency kit with flares and a blanket in the trunk
  2. Verify that his insurance card is in the glove compartment
  3. Never drive after using alcohol or drugs, and never ride with a driver who has
  4. Install a GPS app so his parents can locate the car
Check the answer and why

Never drive after using alcohol or drugs, and never ride with a driver who has

Unintentional injury — motor vehicle crashes above all — is the leading cause of adolescent death , with alcohol and drugs a major contributor. Emergency kits, insurance and GPS are reasonable, but none of them prevents the crash.
