Rules to remember

  • Recognize fever as urgent during cancer treatment.
  • An airway or circulation change makes an allergic reaction an emergency.
  • Protect the child and time the seizure.

See this → think this

A child on chemotherapy has a new fever even though other symptoms are mild.

Think…
Neutropenia can mask signs of serious infection.

Hives appear with wheezing and faintness soon after a known allergen.

Think…
Anaphylaxis can narrow the airway and cause circulatory collapse.

A child suddenly stiffens and has rhythmic jerking movements.

Think…
During a seizure the immediate risks include injury and impaired breathing.

What do I do first?

Hematology & Childhood Neoplasms

First action
Notify the oncology team immediately and follow the febrile-neutropenia pathway.
Look for a response
Reassess perfusion, mental status, temperature and response to timely treatment.

Immune, Infectious Disease, Skin & Burns

First action
Call for help and give intramuscular epinephrine per the emergency protocol. Support breathing and circulation.
Look for a response
Reassess airway, respiratory effort, perfusion and recurrence of symptoms.

Neurologic & Neuromuscular

First action
Protect the head, clear hazards, time the seizure and turn to the side when safe. Keep objects out of the mouth.
Look for a response
Afterward assess breathing, responsiveness and return toward baseline.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

Time a seizure from onset. A convulsive seizure reaching 5 minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery, needs an emergency response.

Compare behavior and responsiveness to the child's baseline. An infant may show irritability or a bulging fontanelle rather than an adult pattern.

Practice and memory cues

Red cells carry oxygen, platelets help stop bleeding and neutrophils fight infection. Decide which function is failing before choosing a precaution.

A nurse receives report on four children. Which child should the nurse assess first?

  1. An 8-year-old with sickle cell disease who has sudden chest pain, fever and dyspnea
  2. A 3-year-old with iron deficiency anemia who is pale and tired
  3. A 6-year-old with hemophilia who has mild knee swelling after bumping a table
  4. A 15-year-old with mononucleosis who reports a sore throat
Check the answer and why

An 8-year-old with sickle cell disease who has sudden chest pain, fever and dyspnea

Chest pain with fever and dyspnea in sickle cell disease is acute chest syndrome , a life-threatening emergency. The other three are expected findings for their diagnoses — pallor in iron deficiency, a minor bleed in hemophilia, and pharyngitis in mono.

A 7-year-old at a school event develops hives, lip swelling and audible stridor minutes after eating a cookie containing peanuts. Which action should the school nurse take first?

  1. Administer oral diphenhydramine
  2. Administer intramuscular epinephrine
  3. Apply high-flow oxygen by mask
  4. Call the parents for permission to treat
Check the answer and why

Administer intramuscular epinephrine

IM epinephrine is the first action in anaphylaxis — it is the only agent that reverses airway edema and vasodilation. Antihistamines are adjuncts, oxygen supports but does not treat the cause, and calling for permission delays a life-saving drug.

A 5-year-old is being observed after a fall from a playground structure. Which finding should the nurse report as the priority?

  1. The child reports a headache
  2. The child, previously alert and chatty, is now confused and difficult to arouse
  3. The child vomited once shortly after the fall
  4. The heart rate has drifted from 96 to 88
Check the answer and why

The child, previously alert and chatty, is now confused and difficult to arouse

A change in level of consciousness is the earliest sign of neurologic deterioration and outranks the others. Cushing's triad — rising BP, falling HR, falling RR — is a late finding, so waiting for the heart rate to drop meaningfully means waiting too long.
