Rules to remember

  • Check both uterine tone and position when bleeding increases.
  • Judge feeding by transfer and output.
  • A newborn loses heat quickly through wet skin.
  • Link prematurity to the work of breathing.

See this → think this

A postpartum uterus feels boggy and sits to the right of midline.

Think…
A full bladder can displace the uterus and interfere with contraction.

A newborn latches often, but swallowing is seldom heard and output is low for age.

Think…
Frequent feeds alone do not show that enough milk is transferred.

A wet newborn is left on a cool surface after birth.

Think…
Evaporation removes heat as moisture dries. Direct contact with a cold surface adds conductive loss.

A preterm newborn is grunting, flaring the nostrils and retracting between the ribs.

Think…
Respiratory distress can reflect immature lungs and inadequate surfactant.

What do I do first?

Postpartum Assessment & Hemorrhage

First action
Activate the hemorrhage response for heavy bleeding. For this course cue, empty the bladder first, then massage the fundus. Give ordered uterotonics and oxygen by mask per the course pathway.
Look for a response
Recheck fundal firmness and position, quantified blood loss and perfusion.

Newborn Feeding

First action
Observe a complete feed, check latch, and review diaper output and weight trend with the team.
Look for a response
Look for audible swallowing and improving age-appropriate output and weight trajectory.

The Normal Newborn & Thermoregulation

First action
Dry promptly, replace damp linens, and use skin-to-skin or a warmer as appropriate.
Look for a response
Reassess temperature, breathing and color.

The High-Risk Newborn & Course Review

First action
Get neonatal assistance, assess oxygenation and support temperature and breathing.
Look for a response
Recheck work of breathing, oxygenation and response to ordered support.

Postpartum changes that need urgent review

Fever, uterine tenderness and foul-smelling lochia
Think endometritis. Assess vital signs and notify the obstetric team for evaluation and prescribed treatment.
Unilateral leg swelling and pain
Suspect a clot. Avoid massaging the leg and obtain urgent assessment. New chest pain or breathlessness requires an emergency response.
Hallucinations, delusions or thoughts of harming the baby
Keep the parent and baby safe with supervision and obtain emergency psychiatric and medical help.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

Check the Apgar score at 1 and 5 minutes. A newborn who needs ventilation needs immediate support; care begins before the score is completed.

A boggy uterus suggests atony. Persistent bright bleeding with a firm uterus points toward trauma. Escalate heavy bleeding in either case.

Practice and memory cues

A wet infant loses heat by evaporation. A cold scale causes conduction. A draft causes convection. A cold nearby window causes radiation.

A client 3 hours postpartum has a firm midline fundus at the umbilicus, yet a steady trickle of bright red blood without clots continues. Which action does the nurse take?

  1. Massage the fundus vigorously
  2. Inspect the perineum, vagina and cervix for a laceration
  3. Encourage her to void and reassess
  4. Document the finding as normal lochia rubra
Check the answer and why

Inspect the perineum, vagina and cervix for a laceration

A firm fundus with continued bleeding means the uterus is not the source , so look for a laceration or hematoma, classically a bright red continuous trickle without clots. Massage will not help a uterus that is already contracted, and this is not normal lochia.

A newborn has a scalp swelling that crosses the suture lines and was present at birth. How does the nurse document this finding?

  1. Cephalohematoma, which raises the risk of jaundice
  2. Caput succedaneum, which resolves in a few days
  3. Molding, which requires no documentation
  4. A subgaleal hemorrhage requiring immediate surgery
Check the answer and why

Caput succedaneum, which resolves in a few days

Caput succedaneum crosses suture lines and resolves within days; a cephalohematoma is bounded by sutures, does not cross them, and carries a higher jaundice risk as the blood breaks down. Whether it crosses the suture line is the entire discrimination.

A newborn born at 28 weeks is receiving gavage feedings. On day 6 the abdomen is distended, a feeding is returned undigested, and the diaper contains bloody stool. Which action does the nurse take first?

  1. Stop all feedings and report the findings
  2. Give the next feeding more slowly over a longer time
  3. Place the newborn prone and reassess the abdomen in 2 hours
  4. Change the newborn from gavage feedings to nipple feedings
Check the answer and why

Stop all feedings and report the findings

A distended abdomen, bloody stool and feeding intolerance point to NEC , so all feeds are stopped immediately and reported — feeding a hypoxic gut makes the ischemia worse. Slowing the feeding or changing the route is still feeding, so it does not protect the gut.
