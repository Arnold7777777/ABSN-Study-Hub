Rules to remember

  • Match the method to pregnancy goals and STI protection.
  • Trace where oxygen enters fetal circulation.
  • Separate evidence of pregnancy from maternal symptoms.
  • Use the prenatal history and the fetal assessment together.
  • Match a nutrition problem to a practical food choice.
  • Cervical change distinguishes true labor.
  • Read the timing of decelerations against contractions.
  • Bleeding with pain or faintness changes the priority.
  • Check breathing, reflexes and urine output during magnesium therapy.
  • Recognize a delivery emergency from the sequence of events.
  • Check both uterine tone and position when bleeding increases.
  • Judge feeding by transfer and output.
  • A newborn loses heat quickly through wet skin.
  • Link prematurity to the work of breathing.

See this → think this

A client wants to avoid pregnancy for several years and also reduce STI risk.

Think…
An implant or IUD gives lasting pregnancy protection. Condoms add STI protection.

The question asks which cord vessel carries oxygenated blood toward the fetus.

Think…
The umbilical vein brings oxygen from the placenta. Two arteries return blood to the placenta.

A missed period and nausea are followed by a positive urine pregnancy test.

Think…
Symptoms are presumptive. Detecting hCG is probable evidence. An embryo seen on ultrasound is positive evidence.

A pregnant patient reports a persistent severe headache and spots in her vision.

Think…
These are urgent maternal warning signs. Assess for a hypertensive disorder.

A pregnant patient with low iron intake often drinks tea with meals.

Think…
Iron absorption depends partly on what is eaten with it. Vitamin C helps absorb plant-based iron.

Contractions become regular and the cervix dilates and thins over successive assessments.

Think…
Dilation is opening. Effacement is thinning. Together they document labor progress.

Repeated fetal heart-rate dips begin after contractions start and recover after the contractions end.

Think…
Recurrent late decelerations raise concern for impaired placental oxygen exchange.

An early-pregnancy patient has one-sided pelvic pain, vaginal spotting and new dizziness.

Think…
Possible ectopic pregnancy. Faintness or hypotension can signal internal bleeding.

A patient receiving magnesium sulfate becomes drowsy with slow breathing and absent reflexes.

Think…
Magnesium toxicity can suppress breathing. Poor renal clearance increases risk.

The head delivers and draws back against the perineum. The shoulders do not follow.

Think…
The anterior shoulder may be trapped behind the pubic bone: shoulder dystocia.

A postpartum uterus feels boggy and sits to the right of midline.

Think…
A full bladder can displace the uterus and interfere with contraction.

A newborn latches often, but swallowing is seldom heard and output is low for age.

Think…
Frequent feeds alone do not show that enough milk is transferred.

A wet newborn is left on a cool surface after birth.

Think…
Evaporation removes heat as moisture dries. Direct contact with a cold surface adds conductive loss.

A preterm newborn is grunting, flaring the nostrils and retracting between the ribs.

Think…
Respiratory distress can reflect immature lungs and inadequate surfactant.

Early Pregnancy Bleeding & Ectopic Pregnancy

Hypertensive Disorders & Magnesium Sulfate

First action
Stop the infusion, call for help and support ventilation. Prepare prescribed calcium gluconate.
Look for a response
Reassess respiratory effort, alertness, reflexes and urine output.

Dysfunctional Labor, Uterine Rupture & Obstetric Drug Math

First action
Call for help and assist with McRoberts positioning and suprapubic pressure as directed. Avoid fundal pressure.
Look for a response
After delivery, assess newborn breathing and arm movement and assess maternal bleeding.

Postpartum Assessment & Hemorrhage

First action
Activate the hemorrhage response for heavy bleeding. For this course cue, empty the bladder first, then massage the fundus. Give ordered uterotonics and oxygen by mask per the course pathway.
Look for a response
Recheck fundal firmness and position, quantified blood loss and perfusion.

Newborn Feeding

First action
Observe a complete feed, check latch, and review diaper output and weight trend with the team.
Look for a response
Look for audible swallowing and improving age-appropriate output and weight trajectory.

The Normal Newborn & Thermoregulation

First action
Dry promptly, replace damp linens, and use skin-to-skin or a warmer as appropriate.
Look for a response
Reassess temperature, breathing and color.

The High-Risk Newborn & Course Review

First action
Get neonatal assistance, assess oxygenation and support temperature and breathing.
Look for a response
Recheck work of breathing, oxygenation and response to ordered support.

Fever, uterine tenderness and foul-smelling lochia
Think endometritis. Assess vital signs and notify the obstetric team for evaluation and prescribed treatment.
Unilateral leg swelling and pain
Suspect a clot. Avoid massaging the leg and obtain urgent assessment. New chest pain or breathlessness requires an emergency response.
Hallucinations, delusions or thoughts of harming the baby
Keep the parent and baby safe with supervision and obtain emergency psychiatric and medical help.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

Tie each assessment to its time point: prenatal screening, stages of labor, minutes after birth, then postpartum recovery.

At birth, distinguish maternal bleeding from newborn breathing needs. Both patients may need simultaneous assessment and help.

Practice and memory cues

Trace the route from pregnancy to labor to postpartum. At each point ask whose status changed, what threatens perfusion or breathing, and how to check the response.

A client is pregnant. She has one child born at 39 weeks, one child born at 34 weeks, and a pregnancy loss at 10 weeks. Both children are living. What is her GTPAL?

  1. 4-1-1-1-2
  2. 3-1-1-1-2
  3. 4-2-0-1-2
  4. 4-1-1-0-3
Check the answer and why

4-1-1-1-2

Gravida counts all pregnancies including the current one , giving 4. Term (37 weeks or more) is 1, preterm (20 to under 37 weeks) is 1, abortions (under 20 weeks) is 1, and living children is 2.

A laboring client on oxytocin has repetitive decelerations that begin after each contraction peaks and return to baseline after the contraction ends. Which action does the nurse take first?

  1. Position the client on her left side
  2. Increase the oxytocin to strengthen contractions
  3. Document the finding and continue monitoring
  4. Perform a cervical examination to check dilation
Check the answer and why

Position the client on her left side

Decelerations that begin after the peak and recover after the contraction are late decelerations from uteroplacental insufficiency , and of these options repositioning is the intrauterine resuscitation measure - alongside stopping the oxytocin, an IV bolus and early notification, matched to the cause rather than run in a fixed order. Oxygen is not routine; it is for maternal hypoxia. Increasing oxytocin worsens it and documentation alone is never sufficient.

A client 3 hours postpartum has a firm midline fundus at the umbilicus, yet a steady trickle of bright red blood without clots continues. Which action does the nurse take?

  1. Massage the fundus vigorously
  2. Inspect the perineum, vagina and cervix for a laceration
  3. Encourage her to void and reassess
  4. Document the finding as normal lochia rubra
Check the answer and why

Inspect the perineum, vagina and cervix for a laceration

A firm fundus with continued bleeding means the uterus is not the source , so look for a laceration or hematoma, classically a bright red continuous trickle without clots. Massage will not help a uterus that is already contracted, and this is not normal lochia.

A newborn is being treated for neonatal abstinence syndrome. Which interventions does the nurse include in the plan of care? Select all that apply.

  1. Cluster care and keep the lights dim
  2. Swaddle the newborn snugly
  3. Place the crib in a brightly lit area for close observation
  4. Offer small, frequent, high-calorie feedings
  5. Provide vigorous stimulation to maintain alertness
Check the answer and why

Cluster care and keep the lights dim Swaddle the newborn snugly Offer small, frequent, high-calorie feedings

NAS care is the opposite of stimulation : cluster care, dim lights, low noise, swaddling, non-nutritive sucking, and small frequent high-calorie feedings with the head elevated. Bright light and vigorous stimulation worsen the hyperirritability and tremors.
