What do I do first?

Nutrition & Health Promotion in Pregnancy

First action
Help plan an iron-rich meal with a vitamin C source and review prescribed supplements.
Look for a response
The patient identifies a realistic meal and knows when follow-up labs are due.

Labor & Birth

First action
Assess contraction pattern, maternal status and fetal response.
Look for a response
Progress is documented with cervical change and a reassuring maternal-fetal assessment.

Fetal Monitoring, Comfort & Analgesia in Labor

First action
Reposition laterally, assess maternal blood pressure, stop oxytocin if running and notify the team.
Look for a response
Reassess the tracing, including baseline variability and whether the late dips resolve.

Numbers and distinctions

What to remember

Contraction frequency runs from the start of one contraction to the start of the next. Duration runs from the beginning to the end of one contraction.

A baseline fetal heart rate of 110–160 beats/min is generally expected. Always interpret the baseline with variability and decelerations.

Practice and memory cues

Early decelerations mirror contractions. Variable dips suggest cord compression. Late dips follow the contraction peak and can signal impaired placental exchange.

A client at 39 weeks reports a gush of clear fluid. The fetal head is at a minus 2 station. Which action does the nurse take first?

  1. Assess the fetal heart rate
  2. Note the color and odor of the fluid
  3. Test the fluid with nitrazine paper
  4. Take the client's temperature
Check the answer and why

Assess the fetal heart rate

The first action after any rupture of membranes is to check the fetal heart rate , because you are ruling out cord prolapse, and an unengaged head raises that risk. Color, odor, nitrazine testing and temperature all matter, but they come second.

A laboring client on oxytocin has repetitive decelerations that begin after each contraction peaks and return to baseline after the contraction ends. Which action does the nurse take first?

  1. Position the client on her left side
  2. Increase the oxytocin to strengthen contractions
  3. Document the finding and continue monitoring
  4. Perform a cervical examination to check dilation
Check the answer and why

Position the client on her left side

Decelerations that begin after the peak and recover after the contraction are late decelerations from uteroplacental insufficiency , and of these options repositioning is the intrauterine resuscitation measure - alongside stopping the oxytocin, an IV bolus and early notification, matched to the cause rather than run in a fixed order. Oxygen is not routine; it is for maternal hypoxia. Increasing oxytocin worsens it and documentation alone is never sufficient.
