Alpha Blockers 🩸

Alpha-1 antagonists — tamsulosin, doxazosin, prazosin & phentolamine

NG-141 PHARM ADHD-friendly visual edition

Alpha-1 blockers relax smooth muscle in blood vessels, the prostate, and the bladder neck by blocking norepinephrine's vasoconstricting signal. That relaxation is the whole benefit for BPH — and the whole danger for BP. First-dose orthostatic hypotension is the single most-tested safety point in this class.

🩸 Alpha-1 blocked = vessels relaxSmooth muscle in vessels/prostate/bladder neck loosens → BP drops, urine flows easier.
🚨 First-dose phenomenonBiggest BP drop is after the VERY FIRST dose — give it at bedtime, teach fall precautions.
🎯 Uroselective = tamsulosinTargets alpha-1A in the prostate/bladder — less BP drop than doxazosin/prazosin.
🧊 Phentolamine saves tissueLocal injection reverses IV dopamine/norepinephrine extravasation necrosis.
💊

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · THE MECHANISM

Block alpha-1 receptors → smooth muscle relaxes wherever that receptor sits. Where it sits determines what you use the drug for.

🎯 Uroselective (alpha-1A) vs non-selective alpha-1 blockade

EXAM TIP All alpha-1 blockers relax smooth muscle by blocking norepinephrine — but tamsulosin is "uroselective", meaning it prefers the alpha-1A receptor subtype concentrated in the prostate and bladder neck, with much less action on vascular alpha-1 receptors. Doxazosin and prazosin hit vascular alpha-1 receptors too, so they drop BP more.

🧠 "Uro-SELECT-ive SELECTS the urinary tract." Tamsulosin's job is the bladder neck, not the vessels — that's why it's FDA-approved only for BPH, not hypertension, while doxazosin and prazosin are approved for both.

💊 Phentolamine — the nonselective, short-acting outlier

Phentolamine (Regitine, Oraverse) blocks alpha receptors broadly and is used very differently from the daily oral drugs above:

  • HTN from pheochromocytoma (a catecholamine-secreting adrenal tumor)
  • Pre-op BP control before pheochromocytoma surgery
  • Antidote for tissue damage from IV dopamine or norepinephrine extravasation

Dose: 5 mg given 1–2 hr pre-op, repeated PRN; can infuse at 0.5–1 mg/min during surgery. Route: IM, IV, or local injection.

🧠 "Phen-TOLL-amine tolls the alarm on a leaking IV." If a dopamine or norepinephrine IV infiltrates, local phentolamine reverses the vasoconstriction before the tissue dies.
⚠️

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · THE DANGER LIST

Every red flag in this class traces back to one thing: relaxed vessels drop blood pressure.

🚨 First-dose phenomenon — the #1 safety teaching point

The first dose of any alpha-1 blocker causes the sharpest fall in standing BP — syncope and falls are the real risk, especially in older adults already on other antihypertensives. Give the first dose at bedtime, have the client already lying down, and warn them not to get up quickly overnight.

🧠 "First hit hardest." Every subsequent dose is tolerated better as the body adapts — but the very first one can drop someone to the floor.

🚨 Adverse effects

  • Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, syncope
  • Weakness, fatigue
  • Nasal stuffiness (vasodilation of nasal mucosa)
  • Reflex tachycardia, palpitations
  • Weight gain (fluid retention)
🧠 "Loosen the pipes, and gravity wins." Every symptom on this list is downstream of vessels relaxing more than the body can immediately compensate for.

⚠️ Phentolamine-specific adverse effects

  • Cerebrovascular spasm, dizziness, weakness
  • Hypotension, MI, angina, arrhythmias, tachycardia
  • Abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea/vomiting — can aggravate peptic ulcer disease
  • Flushing; local injection-site pain

Contraindicated in coronary artery disease.

🧠 "A dilated vessel can also steal blood from where it's needed" — that's the angina/MI risk behind a drug that vasodilates hard and fast.

TEACH

STEP 3 · WHAT THE CLIENT NEEDS TO HEAR

Position changes, timing of the first dose, and knowing which symptoms mean "call the office."

✅ Orthostatic hypotension precautions

1
🛏️ First dose at bedtime, already lying down
2
🪑 Sit up, pause ~1 minute before standing
3
🚶 Stand still ~1 minute before walking
4
📞 Call light within reach — ask for help getting up
🧠 "Sit-a-minute, stand-a-minute." The two 1-minute pauses give the vasculature time to catch up before gravity takes over.

✅ Everyday teaching

  • Change positions slowly — sit → stand, lying → sit
  • Avoid hot showers/baths — heat adds more vasodilation on top of the drug
  • Report chest pain during any IV infusion
  • Don't drive or operate machinery until you know how the first dose affects you
🧠 "Hot water is a second vasodilator." Heat + alpha blockade stack the same way alcohol does.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What is the #1 safety teaching point for a client starting an alpha-1 blocker?
First-dose orthostatic hypotension — give the first dose at bedtime, teach slow position changes, and use fall precautions.
Q2: Why is tamsulosin used for BPH but not typically for hypertension?
It's uroselective for the alpha-1A receptor concentrated in the prostate/bladder neck, with minimal effect on vascular alpha-1 receptors, so it doesn't lower BP much.
Q3: What is phentolamine used to treat when an IV infiltrates?
Tissue damage from dopamine or norepinephrine extravasation — local phentolamine reverses the vasoconstriction and can save the tissue.
Q4: What interaction should you flag between an alpha blocker and a PDE5 inhibitor like sildenafil?
Additive vasodilation — increased risk of hypotension. This combination is heavily tested for tamsulosin + sildenafil/tadalafil.
