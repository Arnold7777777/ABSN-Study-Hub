SSRI 🧠

Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor — the first-line antidepressant

NG-205 PSYCH PHARM ADHD-friendly visual edition

Blocks the pump that vacuums serotonin back out of the synapse, so more serotonin stays available to bind receptors. Selectively — unlike older classes, it leaves norepinephrine & dopamine reuptake mostly alone, which is exactly why SSRIs are gentler and sit at the bottom of the antidepressant ladder — tried first, before SNRIs, TCAs, or MAOIs.

WHAT IT DOES

STEP 1 · THE MECHANISM

One pump, blocked selectively — serotonin floods the synapse instead of getting vacuumed back up.

🔬 Block the reuptake pump → serotonin stays in the gap

MOA Normally, a reuptake pump (transporter) on the presynaptic neuron sucks leftover serotonin back inside after it fires, ending the signal. SSRIs selectively block that pump — serotonin can't be reabsorbed, so it lingers in the synaptic cleft and keeps stimulating postsynaptic receptors.

🧠 “SSRI = the pump gets a boot.” No reuptake in → serotonin stays out in the synapse, longer & stronger. Selective because norepinephrine and dopamine pumps are left alone — that selectivity is what makes SSRIs gentler than TCAs or MAOIs.

🔤 Say the name — it's the mechanism

  • Selective = targets serotonin, mostly leaves NE/dopamine alone
  • Serotonin = the neurotransmitter affected
  • Reuptake = the neuron sucking the chemical back in
  • Inhibitor = blocks that process
🧠 Inhibits the RE-uptake → keeps MORE serotonin around → serotonin levels are increased in the synapse.

💊 Know the names — the "-ine/-am" family

  • Sertraline
  • Escitalopram & Citalopram
  • Paroxetine
  • Fluoxetine
🧠 Most end in -ine or -(cit)alopram — build a personal flash list, since suffix alone isn't 100% reliable across classes.

🎯 Indications

  • 😔 Depression — first-line
  • 😰 Anxiety disorders
  • 🎗️ PTSD
  • 🌀 OCD & panic disorder (common off-label/expanded use)
🧠 “SSRI = Start Simple, Relatively safe, Indication-flexible.” One class, four psych diagnoses.
🔎

WATCH FOR

STEP 2 · SAFETY

Three things this class is tested on hardest: serotonin syndrome, the black box warning, and never stopping cold-turkey.

🚨 Serotonin syndrome — the shared danger across every antidepressant class

Happens when serotonin activity gets too high — usually from combining two serotonergic drugs (SSRI + MAOI, SSRI + triptan, SSRI + tramadol, SSRI + St. John's wort). Same 5 symptoms every time — memorize this list once, it repeats on SNRI/TCA and MAOI pages too:

🧠 “A HOT DAT.” Agitation · HOT = hyperthermia · Diaphoresis · Agitation-driven Tremor + clonus. A patient who looks hot, shaky, and sweaty with jumpy reflexes after adding a second serotonergic drug = serotonin syndrome until proven otherwise.

⚠️ Black box warning — suicidality

Increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults up to age 24–25 — highest risk is the first few weeks of therapy or right after a dose change.

🚨 ASSESS FIRST if the patient says "this medication isn't working after 2 weeks" — check for worsening hopelessness, despair, suicidal thoughts, or self-harm before anything else.

🧠 Young + new + increasing dose = watch closest. The warning isn't "SSRIs cause suicide" — it's a monitoring mandate in the vulnerable window before the antidepressant effect kicks in.

💊 Common side effects — NCLEX traps live here

EffectTrap to avoid
🛌 InsomniaNOT sedation — don't get tricked into expecting drowsiness
💔 Sexual dysfunctionCommon reason patients self-stop — ask about it, don't assume they'll volunteer it
⚖️ Weight gainCan appear over months, not days
🤢 GI upset / nauseaUsually improves after the first 1–2 weeks
🧠 “SSRIs keep you UP, not down.” Insomnia is the classic wrong-answer trap — students assume any psych med = sedating.

TEACH

STEP 3 · PATIENT EDUCATION

Slow to start, slow to stop — the two teaching points that prevent the most calls back to the clinic.

✅ The teaching ladder

1
⏱️ Full effect takes 4–6 weeks — don't judge it a failure early
2
🚫 Never stop abruptly — taper under provider guidance
3
💬 Report worsening mood, hopelessness, or self-harm thoughts right away
4
💔 Sexual side effects — tell your provider, don't just quit the med
🧠 “Slow onset, slow taper.” Everything about this class moves on a weeks-long timeline in both directions — starting and stopping.

⚠️ Discontinuation syndrome

Stopping abruptly can cause dizziness, flu-like symptoms, irritability, "brain zaps," and rebound anxiety within days.

🧠 Never stop cold turkey. Always taper — the prescriber writes the schedule down.

🧪 NCLEX traps to flag

  • Insomnia is expected — not sedation
  • "Not working" at 2 weeks is expected, not a failed trial — but still assess for suicidality
  • SSRI ≠ SNRI ≠ TCA ≠ MAOI — know which page's side effects belong to which class
🧠 See NG-187 (SNRI vs. TCA) and NG-154 (MAOI) to keep the four antidepressant classes from blurring together.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What does SSRI block, and what does that do to serotonin levels?
Blocks the presynaptic serotonin reuptake pump — serotonin stays in the synapse longer, so levels of available serotonin increase.
Q2: A patient on sertraline says "this isn't working, it's been 2 weeks." What do you assess first?
Assess for worsening hopelessness, despair, suicidal thoughts, or self-harm — the black box warning window — before reassuring them about onset time.
Q3: Name the 5 signs of serotonin syndrome.
Agitation, hyperthermia, clonus/hyperreflexia, diaphoresis, tremor.
Q4: True or false — SSRIs commonly cause sedation.
False — they commonly cause insomnia, not sedation. This is a classic NCLEX trap.
