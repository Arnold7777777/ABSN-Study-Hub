TABLE 2

These suffixes cluster around receptors in the brain, spinal cord, and neuromuscular junction.

🧠 CNS suffix map — where in the nervous system each class acts

🧠 "-PAM calms, -APINE/-AZINE quiets psychosis, -CURIUM paralyzes, -CAINE numbs." Four different nervous-system jobs, four different suffix families.

📋 CNS, neuromuscular & anesthesia suffix reference table

SuffixDrug classMechanismExamples
-pam / -lamBenzodiazepinePotentiates GABA → sedation, anxiolysis, anticonvulsantDiazepam, Lorazepam, Alprazolam
-tal / -barbitalBarbiturateGABA agonist → CNS depression, narrow safety marginPhenobarbital
-trigineAnticonvulsant & mood stabilizer (phenyltriazine)Blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and damps glutamate release → steadies both seizures and moodLamotrigine
-apine / -idoneAtypical antipsychoticBlocks dopamine & serotonin receptorsClozapine, Olanzapine, Risperidone
-azineTypical antipsychotic / antihistamine (phenothiazines)Blocks dopamine (or H1) receptorsChlorpromazine, Thioridazine
-caineLocal anestheticBlocks sodium channels → blocks nerve conductionLidocaine
-aneInhaled general anestheticCNS depression via inhalationHalothane
-stigmineAcetylcholinesterase inhibitorBlocks breakdown of acetylcholine → more ACh availableNeostigmine
-curium / -curoniumNondepolarizing neuromuscular blockerBlocks acetylcholine receptors at the NMJ → paralysisAtracurium, Pancuronium
-caponeCOMT inhibitor (Parkinson's)Blocks COMT enzyme → more levodopa available to the brainEntacapone, Tolcapone
⚠️ EXAM TIP Any rash on lamoTRIGINE is an emergency until proven otherwise — it carries a boxed warning for Stevens–Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis. That is why the dose is titrated up slowly, and why going too fast, or restarting at the old dose after a break, raises the risk. Teach her: new rash → stop the drug and call, do not wait for the next appointment.
🧠 EXAM TIP -curium/-curonium paralyzes muscle but never touches consciousness — a paralyzed client on these drugs can still hear and feel everything unless also sedated.

MATERNAL & OBSTETRIC

TABLE 4

Two suffixes, one job: work on the uterus — ripen it and start labor, or clamp it down afterwards so it stops bleeding.

🩸 Uterotonic suffix reference table

SuffixDrug classMechanismExamples
-ergonovineErgot alkaloid uterotonicClamps uterine smooth muscle into a sustained contraction, squeezing the bleeding vessels shutMethylergonovine (Methergine), Ergonovine
-prostProstaglandinSoftens and ripens the cervix and contracts the uterus — used both to start labor and to stop bleeding after itCarboprost (Hemabate), Dinoprostone (Cervidil), Misoprostol (Cytotec)
⚠️ EXAM TIP Take the blood pressure before every dose — methylERGONOVINE raises it, so it is contraindicated in hypertension and in preeclampsia. That is the pairing they test: postpartum hemorrhage plus a high blood pressure means this is the wrong drug, and the answer is to hold it and tell the provider.
⚠️ EXAM TIP Carboprost is the one you hold in asthma — prostaglandins bronchoconstrict. So the two uterotonics have opposite disqualifiers: methylergonovine is out in hypertension, carboPROST is out in asthma, and oxytocin is the one with neither. Carboprost also causes diarrhea, nausea and fever often enough that it is expected rather than a surprise.
🧠 Why the name helps: it is an ergot alkaloid, and ergots constrict vessels — the same family as the -triptan-adjacent migraine ergots. Vasoconstriction is the through-line: useful in the uterus, risky in a client whose pressure is already up.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A drug ends in "-pril" — what class is it and what does it do?
ACE inhibitor — blocks angiotensin-converting enzyme, lowering blood pressure and reducing aldosterone.
Q2: What's the difference between -mab and -tinib drugs?
-mab = monoclonal antibody; -tinib = tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Both are targeted cancer therapies, but they work through completely different mechanisms.
Q3: -cillin and -floxacin both treat bacterial infections — what's the mechanism difference?
-cillin (penicillins) attack the bacterial cell wall; -floxacin (fluoroquinolones) block DNA gyrase, stopping DNA replication.
Q4: What suffix marks a nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker, and what's the key safety point?
-curium/-curonium. These paralyze muscle but do not sedate or block pain — a client needs separate sedation/analgesia.
