CBT 🧠

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy — first-line talk therapy

NG-221 MENTAL HEALTH ADHD-friendly visual edition

CBT is talk therapy that reframes thinking to change how a client feels and behaves. It's first-line treatment for anxiety, phobias, PTSD, OCD, depression, and eating disorders — this page's cognitive triangle and distortion list is the toolkit those disease-specific pages point back to.

🔺 Thoughts → Feelings → BehaviorsChange the thought, and the feeling & behavior downstream change too.
🌀 Distortions = broken thinking patternsCatastrophizing, all-or-nothing, overgeneralization — name the distortion to fix it.
🪜 Systematic desensitizationGradual, ranked exposure to the feared thing — the phobia answer.
⭐ First-line for many disordersPhobias, PTSD, OCD, depression, eating disorders all start here.
🧠

THE COGNITIVE TRIANGLE

STEP 1 · THE MECHANISM

One triangle explains why CBT works — thoughts drive feelings, feelings drive behavior, and behavior reinforces the original thought.

🔺 Thoughts ↔ Feelings ↔ Behaviors — a closed loop

EXAM TIP CBT works by interrupting the loop at the thought — because thoughts are the easiest of the three to catch and challenge in the moment.

🧠 "Catch it, check it, change it." CBT teaches clients to catch the automatic thought, check it against evidence, and change it — which breaks the loop before the feeling and behavior follow.

💬 What CBT treats

A common type of psychotherapy (talk therapy) that helps clients reframe thought processes to slowly cope with stress & anxiety.

  • PTSD & OCD
  • Eating disorders — anorexia & bulimia
  • Depressive disorders
  • Phobias & panic disorder
🧠 CBT = the default first answer. When an NCLEX question asks "what non-drug therapy," CBT is very often the correct first-line choice.

COGNITIVE DISTORTIONS

STEP 2 · NAME THE BROKEN THOUGHT

These are the specific "thought errors" CBT targets — spotting the pattern is half the treatment.

🌀 The most-tested distortions

DistortionExample
Catastrophizing ⚡"If I fail this quiz, I'll fail nursing school and never get a job."
All-or-nothing / black-and-white thinking ⚪⚫"If I'm not perfect at this, I'm a total failure."
Overgeneralization 🔁"I got one bad grade — I'm always going to be bad at this."
Mind reading 🔮"My coworker didn't say hi — she must hate me."
Emotional reasoning 💭"I feel anxious about the flight, so it must actually be dangerous."
Personalization 🎯"My patient's blood sugar dropped — that's totaly my fault."
Labeling 🏷️"I made one mistake — I'm an idiot."
Mental filter / discounting the positive 🔍Focusing on the one negative comment in an otherwise good evaluation.
🧠 "CAMPLL" — Catastrophizing · All-or-nothing · Mind reading · Personalization · Labeling · mental fiLter. If a client's statement fits one of these patterns, cognitive restructuring is the tool.

🎯 Cognitive reframing / restructuring

Examining negative automatic thoughts and consciously restating them in a more balanced, positive way.

  • Nurse teaches reframing to counteract depression.
  • Effective teaching indicator: client says "I have many friends who love me and care for me."
🧠 Reframe = same facts, new lens. The event doesn't change — the interpretation does.

🪜 Systematic desensitization

Gradual, ranked exposure to a feared object or situation, paired with relaxation, until anxiety fades.

  • Client: "I only travel by train — I'm terrified of flying." → Best intervention: systematic desensitization.
🧠 Desensitization = a fear ladder, climbed one safe rung at a time, never jumping to the top.

THE 5 CBT STRATEGIES

STEP 3 · WHAT SESSIONS LOOK LIKE

Five building blocks, used together across a course of treatment.

🛠️ 5 CBT strategies — the exam list

1
Learn about the disorder — psychoeducation on symptoms & triggers
2
Exposure — systematic desensitization to feared situations/events (behavioral strategy)
3
Self-observation & monitoring — track thoughts, moods, triggers
4
Relaxation techniques — deep breathing, guided imagery, biofeedback
5
New coping skills & cognitive restructuring — reframe distorted thoughts
🧠 "LEARN, EXPOSE, OBSERVE, RELAX, REFRAME." Say it as a checklist for any CBT-based question — most answer choices map onto one of these five.

🌄 Guided imagery

A mind-body intervention: the client concentrates on mental images to reduce stress and anxiety and improve concentration.

  • Advantages: relaxation, inner peace, improved concentration.
  • Focuses on descriptive mental pictures.
🧠 Guided imagery = a mental vacation the client controls.

📟 Biofeedback

Like guided imagery, but with machines/monitors that give real-time feedback (heart rate, muscle tension) to help the client learn to control the stress response — teaching the body to respond differently to stress.

🧠 Biofeedback = guided imagery + a screen. The device turns an invisible stress response into something the client can actually watch and change.

👥 Milieu & group therapy — supporting structures around CBT

Therapeutic milieu: a safe, structured social & physical environment — the goal of every psych/behavioral health unit.

Group therapy goal: reduce isolation in a structured, controlled environment; primary focus is personal feelings that affect behavior.

  • Silent member → encourage interaction, or divide into pairs.
  • Aggressive/interrupting member → "You seem very upset" — let the group handle it first.
  • For personality disorders specifically, milieu's primary goal is managing the effect of the behavior on the entire group.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What are the three points of the cognitive triangle?
Thoughts, feelings, and behaviors — each influences the other two, forming a loop.
Q2: A client says "I made one mistake at work, so I'm a complete failure." Which cognitive distortion is this?
All-or-nothing (black-and-white) thinking, with elements of labeling.
Q3: A client is terrified of flying and only travels by train. Best CBT-based intervention?
Systematic desensitization — gradual, ranked exposure paired with relaxation.
Q4: What is the primary goal of group therapy?
Reduce isolation in a structured, controlled environment and communicate acceptance to the client.
