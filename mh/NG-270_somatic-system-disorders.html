Somatic Disorders 🩹

Real symptoms, no medical cause — and the imposter that isn't a mental illness at all

NG-270 MENTAL HEALTH ADHD-friendly visual edition

These disorders are constantly confused with each other and with a condition that isn't even a psychiatric diagnosis. The physical symptoms are real — the client is not faking, and there is NO medical cause on testing. The one exception on this whole page is malingering, which IS intentional and IS for a clear external reward.

🩻 SSD = unexplained symptomsReal pain, real distress, no medical cause found on testing.
😨 Illness anxiety = fear, not symptomsConvinced they're sick despite few/no actual symptoms.
🎭 Factitious = fakes it, no rewardIntentional, but for the sick role itself — not money or time off.
🎯 Malingering = NOT a mental illnessIntentional faking for a clear external gain (money, avoiding work/jail).

THE 4 DISORDERS

STEP 1 · DEFINITIONS

Same theme across all four: the mind is producing (or fixating on) physical illness — the difference is what drives it and whether it's conscious.

🩻 Somatic Symptom Disorder (SSD)

EXAM TIP Unexplained physical symptoms — abdominal pain, weakness, chest pain, shortness of breath — with NO medical cause. All diagnostic tests come back negative. Symptoms are real; the client is not making them up or faking it.

Clients obsessively focus time & energy on the symptoms, often seeing many different doctors to get a diagnosis that doesn't exist. The pain is typically driven by stress.

🧠 SSD = the body speaking stress. Not fabricated, not for a reward — just a real symptom with a psychological driver.

😨 Illness Anxiety Disorder (hypochondriasis)

Preoccupation with having or getting a serious illness, despite few or no actual physical symptoms and reassuring test results. The fear itself is the disorder — not the symptom.

🧠 SSD = symptom-focused. Illness anxiety = fear-focused — one has the body complaint front and center, the other is mostly worry about a disease that isn't there.

🦵 Conversion Disorder

Neurological-type symptoms (paralysis, blindness, seizures, numbness) that have no medical explanation — the classic teaching example is a symptom appearing after a major psychological stressor.

🧠 Conversion = stress "converted" into a neuro symptom — not fabricated, and often triggered by an identifiable stressful event.

🎭 Factitious Disorder (Munchausen's)

Intentionally produces or fakes symptoms/injuries — but the motivation is internal: to assume the "sick role" and receive care/attention, not for money, time off, or any other outside reward.

🧠 Factitious = fakes it on purpose, for the attention of being sick itself — the "gain" is psychological, not material.
🔎

TELL THEM APART

STEP 2 · THE MASTER COMPARISON

Two axes decide everything here: is the symptom production conscious, and is there an external reward?

🧭 Conscious vs. unconscious, internal vs. external gain

🧠 "Fake it for feelings vs. fake it for a favor." Factitious disorder fakes illness for the emotional payoff of being cared for. Malingering fakes illness for a concrete outside reward — and that distinction is exactly why malingering isn't classified as a mental illness.

CARE

STEP 3 · WHAT NOT TO DO MATTERS MOST

The nursing approach across SSD, illness anxiety, and conversion disorder is almost entirely about what to avoid.

🚫 Don't let the exam trick you — the 3 big NEVERs

💊NEVER reinforcenegative test results → asking for more pain meds
🗣️NEVER debunkdon't say "your symptoms aren't real"
🔬NEVER over-testdon't advocate for more diagnostics or a new diet plan — it's psychological
🧠 "Don't feed it, don't fight it, don't chase it." Don't feed the sick role, don't fight the client's reality, don't chase more tests that won't find anything.

✅ Limit focus on being sick

Gently limit the time spent discussing physical symptoms in conversation — redirect toward coping and function instead of the symptom itself.

🧠 Redirect, don't dismiss. Acknowledge the symptom once, then pivot to coping strategies.

✅ Coping mechanisms — stress-reducing techniques

  • Deep-breathing
  • Meditation
  • Exercise
🧠 Since the physical symptoms are stress-driven, stress-reduction is the actual treatment lever — not more imaging.

✅ Promote insight & identify stressors

Help the client connect symptom flares to specific life stressors — building the mind-body insight is the long-term goal, alongside CBT-based reframing (see NG-221).

🧠 Insight is the win condition. The goal isn't "no more symptoms" overnight — it's the client starting to see the stress connection.

🔗 Cross-reference

📎 Use Therapeutic Communication (NG-271) techniques here: state facts without debating the client's reality, use open-ended questions to explore stressors, and never give false reassurance ("it's all in your head" is never acceptable). CBT (NG-221) cognitive restructuring is a common adjunct treatment for the anxiety driving these disorders.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: A client has chronic unexplained abdominal pain with all negative workups and is not faking it. What disorder fits?
Somatic Symptom Disorder — real symptoms, no medical cause, not intentional.
Q2: How is factitious disorder different from malingering?
Both are intentional faking, but factitious disorder is for internal/psychological gain (the sick role); malingering is for a clear external gain (money, avoiding work or legal trouble) and is NOT classified as a mental illness.
Q3: A client with SSD asks the nurse to advocate for more diagnostic testing. What should the nurse do?
Do NOT advocate for more testing or a new diet plan — this is a psychological disorder, not an undiscovered medical one; redirect toward coping strategies instead.
Q4: What is the key difference between somatic symptom disorder and illness anxiety disorder?
SSD centers on real, distressing physical symptoms themselves. Illness anxiety disorder centers on fear/preoccupation with having or getting a serious illness, often with few or no actual symptoms.
