Sleep & Circadian Rhythm 🌙

The Body Clock, Sleep Architecture & Circadian Disorders

NG-089 Mental Health ADHD-friendly visual edition

One master clock, the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), uses light hitting the retina to time everything — melatonin release, body temperature, cortisol, and when you feel sleepy. Chronic sleep loss raises risk for HTN, obesity, depression, diabetes & stroke. This is the biology page — see Sleep Hygiene (NG-135) for the practical nursing-teaching companion.

CAUSE

STEP 1 · THE BODY CLOCK

Light in, melatonin out — one feedback loop drives the entire 24-hour rhythm.

👁️ Light → SCN → pineal gland → melatonin

PATHWAY Light hits photoreceptors in the retina → signal travels the retinohypothalamic tract → reaches the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus, the brain's master circadian clock → SCN tells the pineal gland to hold back or release melatonin.

🧠 "No light, all right (to sleep)." Any bright light late at night — including phone/screen light — tricks the SCN into thinking it's still day and delays melatonin release.

📈 24-hour rhythm — melatonin & core body temperature

Core body temperature dips to its lowest point in the early morning hours, then climbs through the day and peaks in the evening — the two rhythms run in sync to time sleep onset and wake.

⭐ Why chronic sleep loss matters

Even a few days of poor sleep raises risk for:

  • ❤️ Hypertension
  • ⚖️ Obesity
  • 😔 Depression & decreased cognitive function
  • 🩸 Diabetes
  • 🧠 Stroke
🧠 "HODDS." HTN · Obesity · Depression · Diabetes · Stroke — the five-condition list chronic sleep debt drags up.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · ARCHITECTURE & DISORDERS

A normal night cycles through stages every 90 minutes — a misaligned clock or a broken cycle both look different from the outside.

🔁 Stages of sleep — repeats about every 90 minutes

StageWhat's happening
Stage 1Wakefulness → sleep, non-REM. Lasts a few minutes; loss of awareness but easily aroused.
Stage 2Light sleep, non-REM. 10-20 minutes; vitals & metabolism start to decrease.
Stage 3Deep / slow-wave / delta sleep, non-REM. Vitals decrease further; difficult to awaken; physical/cognitive restoration.
REMRapid eye movement; vivid dreaming; begins ~90 min after falling asleep and recurs every ~90 min; psychological rest.
🧠 "1-2-3-REM, then repeat." Deeper each stage, then REM breaks the pattern for dreaming — the whole loop takes about 90 minutes and repeats all night, with REM periods growing longer closer to morning.

✈️ Circadian rhythm disorders — the clock and the schedule disagree

🏭 Shift work disorder

Rotating or night-shift work forces sleep against the SCN's light-set schedule → insomnia during intended sleep + excessive sleepiness on shift, with real accident/error risk.

🛫 Jet lag

Crossing time zones faster than the SCN can re-entrain (roughly 1 day per time zone) → fatigue, GI upset, trouble sleeping/waking on the new local clock. Eastward travel is typically harder to adjust to than westward.

🌒 Delayed sleep phase

The internal clock runs later than the desired schedule — can't fall asleep until very late (e.g., 2-6am), then struggles to wake for school/work. Common in adolescents/young adults.
🧠 Management lever = light & melatonin timing. Bright light exposure at the right time of day (and avoiding it at the wrong time), plus timed melatonin, helps re-entrain the SCN in all three — the specific timing differs by disorder and is individualized by a sleep specialist.

😴 Insomnia

Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, awakening too early, or not feeling rested on waking.

Chronic insomnia: occurs 3+ nights/week for more than 3 months.

🫁 Sleep apnea — two types

  • Obstructive (OSA): soft tissue in the mouth/throat relaxes and blocks the airway
  • Central: the CNS fails to signal the body to breathe — no airway blockage, the drive to breathe itself pauses

Both show cycles of breathing stops/starts, with pauses lasting more than 10 seconds.

🚨 Narcolepsy — the safety-critical sleep disorder

  • 😴 Sudden "sleep attacks" — falls asleep without warning
  • 💪 Cataplexy — sudden muscle weakness triggered by strong emotion
  • 👁️ Hypnagogic hallucinations — vivid hallucinations at sleep onset
  • 🧠 Linked to a lack of REM regulation — REM-like features intrude into wakefulness
🧠 Huge safety risk — driving, walking, standing. The highest-priority assessment question is about safety during high-risk activities: "Do you have difficulty staying awake while driving?"

✅ Nursing diagnosis example

Disturbed sleep pattern related to anxiety or alteration in client routine — common framing when the SCN's schedule is disrupted by stress, travel, or shift changes.

🎯 Cover & check — 4 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What structure is the body's master circadian clock, and what sets it?
The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus — set primarily by light entering the eyes via the retinohypothalamic tract.
Q2: What gland releases melatonin, and when?
The pineal gland — release increases as light fades in the evening and is suppressed by light exposure.
Q3: How often does the sleep-stage cycle repeat, and which stage brings vivid dreaming?
About every 90 minutes; REM sleep brings vivid dreaming and lengthens as the night goes on.
Q4: Name the three circadian rhythm disorders covered here.
Shift work disorder, jet lag, and delayed sleep phase — all cases where the SCN's internal timing doesn't match the required or desired schedule.
