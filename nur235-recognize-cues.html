🔍 Which finding needs follow-up?

Six sets of findings. In each one, three are what you would expect — and one is the reason you pick up the phone. Tap every finding to see which is which.

🧠 The three questions that sort them

  1. Is it expected for this condition, at this point? Vomiting after a pyloromyotomy is expected. Bilious vomiting never is.
  2. Is it a trend going the wrong way? One dry nappy is nothing. None in eight hours is a direction.
  3. Does it fit a complication I already know to fear? Fever in neutropenia, rupture in appendicitis, obstruction after bowel surgery.

Answer first, then justify. Pick the finding, then say which complication it points at. If you cannot name the complication, it is probably not the answer.

1 · Six hours after pyloromyotomy

A 5-week-old is back on the unit after surgical repair of pyloric stenosis and has just taken a first small feed. Which finding needs follow-up?

★ The point
Non-bilious projectile vomiting at 3–6 weeks is what brought the baby in. Bilious vomiting afterwards is a different problem entirely. The colour is the cue.

2 · Infant with acute gastroenteritis

An 8-month-old has had diarrhoea for two days and is on oral rehydration solution. Which finding needs follow-up?

★ The point
Daily weight is the most accurate measure of fluid loss in a child — but on the shift in front of you, urine output and level of consciousness are what move first. Plain water, juice and sports drinks all make it worse.

3 · Appendicitis, waiting for theatre

A 10-year-old has right lower quadrant pain and is nil by mouth awaiting an appendicectomy. Which finding needs follow-up?

★ The point
This is the one the exam loves, because relief sounds like good news. Never apply heat and never give an enema — both raise the risk of rupture.

4 · Child on chemotherapy with mucositis

A 6-year-old having chemotherapy has mouth ulcers and is reluctant to eat. Which finding needs follow-up?

★ The point
Mucositis is expected. Fever on top of it is not. The mouth is the entry point, and in neutropenia there may be no pus, no redness and no other localising sign — the fever is the whole warning.

5 · Preschooler with sleep terrors

Parents describe their 4-year-old screaming and thrashing at night. Which finding needs follow-up?

★ The point
Nightmare = awake + recall + reassure. Sleep terror = partial arousal + little recall + protect. Escalate if episodes injure, happen very often, wreck daytime function, or come with snoring and breathing pauses.

6 · Four children, one nurse

You take handover on four children. Which one do you see first?

★ The point
When several options all sound reasonable, ask which one has a complication that gets worse while you deal with the others. Infection in an immunosuppressed child is almost always that one.
