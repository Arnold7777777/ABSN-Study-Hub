Tinea 🍄

Fungal skin infection — ringworm, athlete's foot, jock itch & fungal nails

NG-067 INTEGUMENTARY · FUNGAL ADHD-friendly visual edition

Tinea is a superficial fungal infection of keratin — skin, hair or nails — caused by dermatophytes. It is highly contagious and is named for where it lands: capitis, corporis, cruris, pedis, unguium. The classic lesion is a round, itchy, scaly patch with a raised active edge and a clearing centre. Treatment is an antifungal — topical for skin, but oral for hair and nails because a cream cannot reach inside a hair shaft or under a nail. The most-tested nursing action is teaching how to stop the spread.

🍄 A fungus, not a wormDermatophytes eat keratin, so they live in the dead top layer, plus hair and nails. "Ringworm" is a shape, not an animal.
📍 Named by the addresscapitis scalp · corporis body · cruris groin · pedis feet · unguium nails.
💊 Fungus → antifungalSkin = topical "-azole" or terbinafine. Hair or nail = ORAL, for weeks to months.
🚨 Teach: do not shareHIGHLY contagious. No sharing brushes, hats, towels, clothes. Keep skin dry.

THE SKIN MODEL

STEP 0 · LEARN THIS ONCE

Same skin drawing as every page in this set — this time with fungal filaments living in the dead surface layer.

🔬 Skin cutaway — tinea is the SHALLOWEST of all

DEPTH = THE ANSWER Dermatophytes need keratin to eat, and the only fully keratinised, dead tissue is the stratum corneum — plus hair shafts and nail plates. That is the whole disease in one sentence: fungus lives in dead keratin; the redness and itch are your immune system reacting from below.

fungal hyphae (branching filaments) spores scale (infectious flakes) immune reaction below
🧠 "Fungus is a surface squatter." It never invades living tissue in a healthy person — which is why creams work on skin, and why hair and nails (where creams cannot go) need tablets.

📖 Lesion vocabulary — tinea is a SCALE disease

Tinea produces an erythematous scaly plaque with an annular (ring) shape. "Annular + scaly + clearing centre" is exam code for tinea.

🧠 Scale = keratin. Fungus eats keratin, so fungus makes scale. Follow the flakes.
🍄

PATHO & CAUSES

STEP 1 · WHY IT HAPPENS

Warm + damp + contact with fungus = tinea. Get any of those three away and it struggles.

🧠 The one-sentence definition

Tinea is a superficial fungal infection of keratinised tissue — skin, hair or nails — caused by dermatophytes (genera Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton). It is highly contagious.

"Ringworm" is a misnomer — there is no worm. The name describes the expanding ring-shaped lesion.

🧠 "Ringworm has no worm, and tinea has no location — until you add the second word."

⚙️ Mechanism, step by step

Fungal spores land on skin — person, animal, soil or object
▼
Warm + moist + a small break = they germinate
▼
Hyphae digest keratin and spread OUTWARD through the stratum corneum
▼
The immune system reacts from the dermis below
▼
Raised, red, scaly ACTIVE edge + itch
▼
The centre clears as the fungus moves on = the RING
🧠 The ring is a footprint. The edge is where the fungus is now; the middle is where it used to be.

⭐ How people catch it

  • Person to person — direct skin contact, contact sports
  • Animal to person — puppies, kittens, calves, guinea pigs (often the more inflamed lesions)
  • Object to person — brushes, combs, hats, towels, clothing, bedding, gym mats, shared shoes
  • Soil to person — gardening without gloves
  • Self-spread — scratching the groin after touching the feet
🧠 "Pedis first, cruris second." Teach patients to put socks on before underwear so they do not carry the fungus from foot to groin.

🌡️ Risk factors — what makes skin friendly to fungus

  • Heat and humidity; sweating; occlusive shoes and synthetic clothing
  • Moist skin folds — groin, under the breasts, between the toes
  • Communal wet floors — showers, changing rooms, pool decks
  • Diabetes, obesity, immunosuppression, peripheral vascular disease
  • Contact sports — wrestling ("tinea gladiatorum"), rugby
  • Existing tinea pedis — the reservoir for nail and groin infection
🧠 "Fungus loves a warm wet cave." Dry it out and you have already started treatment.

🧪 Bug → drug: the rule that runs this whole set

🧠 Fungus → antifungal. Look for "-azole" or terbinafine. An antibiotic will do absolutely nothing for tinea — and a steroid will make it worse.
🔎

CLUES

STEP 2 · WHAT YOU SEE

Itchy, scaly, and shaped like a ring — then find out which body part it landed on.

👀 The three signs, every time

  • Circular / annular rash — an expanding ring
  • Scaly, with a raised, more active outer border and a clearer centre
  • Pruritus — itching, sometimes intense
  • Plus: mild redness; occasionally small pustules or vesicles along the edge
  • Not usually painful; the patient is systemically well
🧠 "Round · Rough · Restless." Round shape, rough scale, restless itching.

📍 Name it by where it is

  • Tinea capitis — scalp (children); patchy hair loss, black dots, scale, possible kerion
  • Tinea corporis — trunk, arms, legs = the classic ringworm
  • Tinea cruris — groin = jock itch; usually spares the scrotum
  • Tinea pedis — feet = athlete's foot
  • Tinea manuum — hand (often just one, with tinea pedis on both feet)
  • Tinea unguium — nails = onychomycosis
  • Tinea barbae / faciei — beard area / face
  • Tinea versicolor is a different yeast (Malassezia) — patchy pigment change, not a ring
🧠 "CAP-COR-CRU-PED-UNG" — head, body, groin, feet, nails. Say it as one word.

🗺️ Same fungus, different address

🧠 If a question names a Latin body part you do not recognise, translate it — the answer is usually just "which body part is this?"

💍 The ring, explained — and the trap that ruins it

🧠 Scrape the edge, not the middle. And if the rash "got better on steroid cream then spread," think tinea incognito.

🔬 Confirming it — KOH prep & Wood's lamp

🧠 KOH dissolves you, not the fungus. Skin cells melt away; branching septate hyphae stay behind.

💅 Nails — normal vs onychomycosis

🧠 "Thick, yellow, crumbly, lifting." Four adjectives = fungal nail = oral therapy = months, not weeks.

⭐ Scalp & feet — the two that behave differently

🧠 Scalp needs a tablet. Feet need a towel.
🩺

CARE

STEP 3 · WHAT YOU DO

Kill the fungus, dry the environment, and above all — stop it spreading.

💊 Cream or tablet? One question decides it

🧠 "Hair and nails need a tablet." A cream cannot get inside a hair shaft or under a nail plate.

⭐ PRIORITY: teach how to stop the spread

On this topic the highest-priority nursing action is almost always education to prevent transmission.

  • Do NOT share brushes, combs, hats, towels, bed linen, clothing, shoes or sports gear
  • Wash towels, linen and clothing in hot water; hot-wash or replace hair brushes
  • Cover lesions during contact sport; no wrestling/swimming until treated per policy
  • Wear thongs/flip-flops in communal showers and changing rooms
  • Wash hands after applying cream and after touching the lesion
  • Check the household — and the pets. Take an itchy, patchy-coated cat or puppy to the vet
🧠 "Share nothing that touches skin." If the option says "teach not to share personal items," it is almost certainly the answer.

💨 Keep it DRY — the environmental half of treatment

  • Dry thoroughly after washing — especially between the toes and in skin folds
  • Pat, do not rub; dry the groin before the feet so you do not carry fungus upward
  • Change socks daily (more often if sweaty); cotton or moisture-wicking
  • Alternate shoes so each pair dries out for 24 h; avoid occlusive plastic shoes
  • Loose cotton underwear; avoid sitting around in damp gym or swim gear
  • Absorbent powder may help in folds — but it does not replace the antifungal
🧠 "Dry between the toes" — small sentence, big exam points, and it prevents cellulitis too.

💊 Griseofulvin — the classic exam drug

  • Absorbed BEST with a high-FAT meal — take it with whole milk, peanut butter or ice cream
  • Takes weeks to months — a scalp course typically runs many weeks
  • Do NOT stop when the itching stops. Itch settles long before the fungus is gone — stopping early is the number-one cause of relapse
  • May cause photosensitivity — teach sun protection
  • May cause headache, GI upset, rash; report a sore throat, fever or jaundice
  • Interacts with warfarin and can reduce the effectiveness of oral contraceptives; avoid alcohol
  • Derived from a Penicillium mould — ask about penicillin allergy before giving
🧠 "GRISEOfulvin = GREASY food." Fat drives absorption. And: itch gone ≠ fungus gone.

💊 The other antifungals

  • Topical "-azoles" — clotrimazole, miconazole, ketoconazole. Apply to the lesion plus ~2 cm of normal skin around it
  • Terbinafine — topical cream, or oral for nails and scalp
  • Itraconazole / fluconazole — other oral options
  • Ketoconazole or selenium sulfide shampoo may be used as an adjunct in tinea capitis to reduce shedding — it does not replace the oral drug
  • Oral agents may need liver function monitoring; check interactions before giving
  • An antihistamine may be prescribed for the itch — it treats the symptom, not the fungus
🧠 "-azole = a fungus killer." If the drug name ends in -azole or is terbinafine, the stem is about fungus.

✅ Nursing care & comfort

  • Gloves for direct contact with lesions; hand hygiene before and after
  • Assess and document the lesion: site, size, shape, border, scale, satellite lesions
  • Keep nails short and discourage scratching — scratching spreads and can cause secondary bacterial infection
  • Cool compresses and loose clothing for comfort
  • Watch for secondary bacterial infection: increasing pain, warmth, pus, fever
  • Reassure — it is treatable and, in most sites, does not scar
🧠 Scratching turns a fungal problem into a bacterial one.

❌ Never do these

  • Never treat a suspected fungal rash with a topical steroid alone.
  • Never stop the antifungal because the itching or the redness has gone.
  • Never let the patient share brushes, hats, towels or bed linen.
  • Never expect a cream to cure a scalp or nail infection.
  • Never give griseofulvin on an empty stomach — it needs fat to be absorbed.
🧠 Don't steroid it, don't stop it, don't share it.

TELL THEM APART

STEP 4 · THE REAL EXAM SKILL

A ring with a clear centre is a fungus until proven otherwise.

🗺️ Where each one shows up

🧠 Rings anywhere warm and damp = tinea. Same body, five patterns.

📊 The look-alikes

CONDITIONHOW IT DIFFERS FROM TINEA
PsoriasisSilvery, thick scale on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees), well-demarcated but no central clearing. Autoimmune, not infectious. Responds to steroids — tinea does not.
Nummular eczemaCoin-shaped, but solid — itchy, weeping or crusted, with no clear centre and no raised scaly ring.
ImpetigoHoney-coloured crust, no ring, bacterial → needs an antibiotic.
CellulitisHot, painful, spreading, often febrile, no scale. Bacterial and deep.
Pityriasis roseaA single "herald patch" then a Christmas-tree pattern of oval scaly patches on the trunk. Self-limiting, viral-associated.
Tinea versicolorA yeast (Malassezia), not a dermatophyte: fine scaly patches of lighter or darker skin on the chest and back — patchy pigment, no ring.
Nail psoriasis / traumaNail changes can be pitting (psoriasis) or a single damaged nail (trauma). Confirm fungus before committing to months of oral therapy.
🧠 Ring with a clear centre = fungus. Silver scale on elbows = psoriasis. Golden crust = bacteria.

⭐ NCLEX traps

  • The priority for tinea is nearly always teaching to prevent spread.
  • Griseofulvin with a high-fat meal — do not pick "on an empty stomach."
  • Do not stop when the itching stops — courses run weeks to months.
  • Scalp and nail infections need ORAL therapy; a cream is the wrong answer.
  • Scrape the active border, not the cleared centre, for the KOH prep.
  • Recurrent groin tinea? Look at the feet — that is usually the reservoir.
  • Tinea is not a reason for isolation, but it is a reason to stop sharing personal items.
🧠 If an answer choice mentions sharing personal items or finishing the full course, read it twice.

🧠 One-line contrasts

  • Tinea vs psoriasis — clearing centre vs solid silvery plaque
  • Tinea vs eczema — ring vs ill-defined itchy patch
  • Tinea vs impetigo — scale vs honey crust
  • Tinea capitis vs alopecia areata — scaly with broken hairs vs smooth bald patch
  • Tinea vs cellulitis — itchy and scaly vs hot and painful
  • Tinea versicolor vs corporis — pigment patches vs an expanding ring
🧠 Scaly ring, smooth patch, honey crust, hot leg. Four pictures, four diagnoses.

🎯 Cover & check — 9 rapid-fire questions
Q1: What actually causes ringworm?
A dermatophyte fungus that digests keratin — not a worm. It lives in the dead stratum corneum, hair shafts and nail plates.
Q2: Describe the classic lesion.
A round, itchy, scaly patch with a raised, more active outer border and a clearing centre — the ring.
Q3: Name the tinea of the scalp, body, groin, feet and nails.
Capitis (scalp), corporis (body/ringworm), cruris (groin/jock itch), pedis (feet/athlete's foot), unguium (nails/onychomycosis).
Q4: What is the single highest-priority teaching point?
Prevent the spread — do not share brushes, combs, hats, towels, clothing, bedding or shoes, wash items in hot water, and wash hands after touching lesions.
Q5: Which infections cannot be cured with a cream, and why?
Tinea capitis (scalp) and tinea unguium (nails). The fungus is inside the hair shaft or under the nail plate where topical drug cannot reach, so an oral antifungal is required — for weeks to months.
Q6: How should griseofulvin be taken, and what do you teach about stopping it?
With a high-fat meal, because fat greatly improves absorption. Do not stop when the itching stops — the course runs weeks to months and stopping early causes relapse. Also teach sun protection.
Q7: Where do you take the sample for a KOH prep?
Scrape scale from the active, raised, scaly border. The cleared centre may have no organisms. For scalp disease, pluck affected hairs with the root.
Q8: A rash improved on a topical steroid and then spread with a faint edge. What happened?
Tinea incognito — the steroid suppressed the inflammation that was containing the fungus, so it looked better briefly and then spread. It needs an antifungal.
Q9: A patient keeps getting tinea cruris. What do you check?
The feet. Untreated tinea pedis is usually the reservoir. Teach them to treat the feet, dry thoroughly between the toes, and put socks on before underwear.
