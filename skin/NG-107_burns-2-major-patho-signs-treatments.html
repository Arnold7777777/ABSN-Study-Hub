✦2. From injury to shock

Inflammatory injuryVascular permeability rises and fluid distribution changes.

Less effective circulating volumeReduced preload and altered cardiac function can reduce tissue perfusion.

Assess organ perfusionFollow mentation, blood pressure, capillary refill, hourly urine output and laboratory trends together.

Electrical injury can be deeper than it looks

  • Assess the mechanism and associated falls or trauma.
  • Deep muscle injury can release potassium and myoglobin; monitor rhythm, renal function and urine findings.
  • Fluid and urine-output targets may differ from a routine thermal-burn plan.

The gut and metabolism

  • Shock can reduce gut perfusion; assess distention, vomiting and feeding tolerance.
  • Major burns increase energy demands and protein breakdown. Start nutrition support when clinically appropriate; bowel sounds alone do not determine readiness.
  • Use stress-ulcer prevention when indicated by the patient’s risk and protocol.

Carbon monoxide: SpO₂ can mislead

A conventional pulse oximeter cannot reliably exclude carbon monoxide poisoning. In a compatible exposure, give oxygen and obtain urgent evaluation with co-oximetry according to protocol. Neurologic symptoms or loss of consciousness are concerning even with a “normal” saturation.

Read more: CDC: Clinical guidance for carbon monoxide poisoning
