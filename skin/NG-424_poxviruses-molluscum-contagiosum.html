🦠 Poxviruses & Molluscum Contagiosum

Poxviruses are big DNA viruses that cause rashes and skin lesions — smallpox (gone), mpox, and the common, harmless-but-catching childhood skin bumps called molluscum contagiosum.

⭐ If you read nothing else

🔵 Molluscum = small, shiny, dome-shaped bumps with a dimple in the middle (umbilicated), 2–5 mm.
🧒 Most common in children; in adults it can be sexually transmitted (genital lesions).
✋ Spreads by skin contact, scratching (autoinoculation) and shared towels.
⏳ Usually goes away by itself in 6–18 months — treatment is optional.
⚠️ Widespread or stubborn lesions in an adult → think immunosuppression (e.g. HIV).

🦠 1 · The poxvirus family

Poxviridae: large, brick-shaped, enveloped, double-stranded DNA viruses that, unusually, replicate in the cell’s cytoplasm.

🔵 2 · Molluscum contagiosum

🩺 3 · Treatment and teaching

Often no treatment at all — in a healthy child the bumps clear on their own in 6–18 months. Diagnosis is by look (dermoscopy shows the dimple).

💊 If it is treated
🧊
Freeze or scrapecryotherapy, curettage, laser
🧹
Topical agentscantharidin (blistering), podophyllotoxin, imiquimod, potassium hydroxide, salicylic acid
💉
Cidofoviran antiviral for severe cases in immunosuppressed people
🗣️ Teach the family
✋

🩺
Cover the bumpswith clothing or a bandage, especially for sports
🧻
No shared towels or clothing
🛡️
Condomsfor genital lesions; an adult with genital molluscum should be screened for other STIs

🧠 4 · Quick check

What feature tells molluscum apart from a wart?

A shiny dome with a central dimple (umbilicated papule).

A parent asks if the bumps need treatment. What do you say?

Often no — they usually clear on their own in 6–18 months. Keep them covered and don’t pick.

An adult has dozens of large molluscum lesions that won’t clear. What should you suspect?

Immunosuppression, such as HIV.

Which poxvirus disease has been eradicated?

Smallpox — still kept on the bioterrorism list.
